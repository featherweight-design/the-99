The 99 · Art wash · scrim recovery · all surfaces

Getting the picture back

Two things were making the bands dark, and only one of them was the scrim. Every surface below is drawn twice at the same size, same art, same ink — as it ships today, and as proposed. Numbers are worst-case: a pure white pixel directly behind the glyph, in Eldraine, which measures as the least forgiving of the six themes for body text. Three themes are wired to the prop here, including that worst case.

CAUSE 1 · THE LAYERS MULTIPLY
The art was dimmed before the scrim arrived
At opacity .58 the image is already 42% faded toward the background. The scrim then takes more on top. In the middle of the band — where the subject is — 45% of the picture survived on bottom-labelled bands, and 33% on top-labelled ones.
CAUSE 2 · THE RAMP OVERSHOT
It kept darkening past the type
Bottom-labelled bands darken past the type they protect — the hero reaches α .751 where .598 clears. Darkness bought and not spent. Top-labelled bands have the opposite bug, and it is worse — see below.
FOUND WHILE MEASURING · LIVE DEFECT
Deck-card subtitles fail in production today
A top-labelled ramp reaches its dark value at the title, then lightens through the rest of the block. The subtitle lands at 3.15:1 on shipped deck cards and Home ready-to-build — below the 4.5:1 floor, right now. The fix is to hold the dark value across the whole block rather than reach it at the first line. This correction stands on its own and can ship ahead of the art lift.
THE FIX · BOTH LEVERS
Art to .80, dark stop at the type
Mid-band goes 45% → 69% on bottom-labelled bands and 33% → 61% on top-labelled ones, while every line of type lands on α .73 — 4.80:1 body, and no line below the floor. Each ramp is derived from its own measured block.

Two constants, not a per-surface pass. Art opacity and the ramp both live in <ArtWash>. The label position is already passed as band, so deriving the dark stop from it is a change to the derivation, not to six call sites.
The rows are three cases, not one. An earlier draft of this card said rows carry no vertical ramp; art-wash.tsx ships one on 168 (.30 .10 .46 .42) and 148 (.62 .16 .42 .74), and none on 72 — so the claim was true of exactly one of three. At desktop width all three put type past the flat stop, so those ramps do nothing for legibility and need no change: 168 and 148 take the art lift only (sharp .80→.92, ambient .20→.28), and 72 ships .85/.14 → .95/.22, the largest lift in the system, because nothing reads on it at all. That only holds because each row's scrim is keyed to its own portrait (0 / .58w / w / w+10, per decision 8). Share one literal across rows and the 99px resume band goes flat at 230px instead of 109px, leaving its type on the ambient layer — where the lift would cost it 6.18:1 → 4.99:1.
The 148 row at phone width is the one surface the lift cannot pay for. Its type starts 13px from the edge, on the sharp copy, and spans the band — so there is no dark end to derive a stop from, only the whole block. Holding the ramp flat at .78 clears all three lines at 5.05:1, and leaves the art at 22% mid-band — darker than what this document was written to fix. Two lines are already under the floor today (3.12 and 3.92:1), so that hold ships regardless, as an accessibility fix. But the recommendation is to stop paying with the scrim and move the type off the portrait at this width — the fix desktop already uses. That is a layout change, so it is a separate proposal, not a value in this one.
The hero pips keep their bare edge. Measured rather than assumed, since the lift genuinely brightens the ground under them: the lightest pip (--w, cream) against art .80 under a .50 bottom stop reads 4.01–4.09:1 across themes. A pip is a non-text component, so its floor is 3:1 — it clears with room. No hairline. The deliberate omission stands.
Where this stops. .73 sits .022 above the 4.5:1 floor at art .80. That margin is the whole remaining budget — going lighter, or pushing art to 1.0 without also darkening the label stop, drops body text under the floor on a white-skied crop.
