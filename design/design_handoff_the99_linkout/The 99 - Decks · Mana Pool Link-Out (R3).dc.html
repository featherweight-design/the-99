Decks · #330 · the Mana Pool link-out

Both surfaces already had a pinned bottom row. The link belongs in it.

Two blockers, and both were the same mistake: R2 drew desktop by extrapolating from a 390px sheet, and drew that sheet from the addendum canvas rather than from shipped. The desktop card modal has a pinned ‹ Prev · N of M · Next › row under its own var(--border) rule; the export footer is one flex row of two buttons at every breakpoint. Both corrections point the same way, and both make R2’s rule more general rather than less: the link-out lives in its container’s pinned bottom row, below the hairline that row already has — never in a scroll column, and never under a hairline of its own invention.

The card modal did have an actions row after all. R2’s claim that it lacked one was wrong, and the consequence is better than the claim: no new hairline is added to either surface. Two token corrections follow from the same audit — the active ink and the focus ring, item 1. Items 2–5 of R2 otherwise stand, and the label ruling from §2.2 is unchanged.

grep -r 'target="_blank"' src/ returns zero hits, so whatever this pass draws becomes the convention every future link-out inherits. It is therefore drawn once — ink, glyph, states, label grammar, a11y contract — and then placed twice, in the pinned bottom row each surface already has.

Read LINKOUT-R3-README.md and LINKOUT-R3-READINESS.md. The price addendum’s rulings are inherited, not reopened: the buy tag is never a target, the price strip is the Shopping filter’s, exports copy names only. Colours resolve against amonkhet except in item 5; no hex here is normative.

What this pass was told, rather than read

docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-05-manapool-checkout-design.md and src/components/cards/card-modal.tsx are still not in this project. Every shipped fact this round corrects against — the pinned nav row and its rule, the two-button footer, the 590px sheet, the md: split, the two URL shapes — reached me in review, and is recorded here as relayed rather than as read.

That distinction is the reason this round exists: R2 drew a desktop modal it could not see, from a phone sheet that was itself drawn from an addendum canvas rather than from shipped markup. The drawings below are corrected against those relayed facts and should be checked against the files once — particularly the nav row’s own padding, which I have matched to the shipped footer’s and not measured.

1a · The underline is not decoration — it is the whole signal
In this app brass tint already means five other things: the price tail, buy, 6 printings you own ▾, Rare, ×1. Tinted text is the app’s ambient state, so tint cannot also mean “this navigates.” The underline is the only mark not already spent — nothing else in the shipped surfaces carries one.
So the convention reads: tint alone is a fact; tint plus an underline leaves the app. That is one sentence, it is enforceable in review, and it generalises to every link-out after this one.
1b · Glyph: yes, and named destination: yes
• The glyph stays. A new tab that opens without warning is the one navigation players cannot undo with Back. ↗ costs six pixels and is the standing convention for it — and it is aria-hidden with real hidden text behind it, because a glyph is not an announcement.
• “Mana Pool” is named, always. This is the only control that hands the player to another company; a label that hid that (Buy these cards) would be the app spending trust it has not asked for. Naming it also makes the price strip’s prices from ManaPool provenance line and this link visibly the same relationship.
• Label grammar: Buy … at Mana Pool ↗ — at, not on, and the middle names what the player will find there. Deck: these, a basket. Card: any printing, a shelf.
1d · two inks corrected, and neither needed a new token
Active was wrong and the catch is a good one. R2 drew #AB6E37 — darker than the --accent item 5 rejects for failing AA, and unmeasured in all six. Any darker step has the same problem in the two themes that already fail, so active does not darken at all: the ink stays --fg and the underline goes 1px→2px. Press is legible as a change in weight, contrast can only improve, and no seventh value enters the table.
The ring is the specified one, not the drawn one. R2’s asymmetric box-shadow plus padding was a drawing artifact and it is now a real outline at 2px offset. It matters beyond tidiness: outline follows the text box across a line wrap and needs no layout space, which is what lets the link keep no box of its own in a row it shares with buttons.
1c · :visited is not styled — ruled, not skipped
A visited state on a store link makes the browser’s purchase history visible on the player’s own screen — on a shared screen, on a stream, in a screenshot of a decklist. The app does not own that fact and should not render it.
It would also be wrong on its face: the state is per-URL, so one visited card in a list of eight would read as done when the player only looked. The link states a fact about the card, not about the player.

The hairline is where the app ends. Above it, two actions that keep the player here and one note about them. Below it, one link that does not. The hierarchy is then read off the structure rather than off a colour, which is the only version of “quieter” that survives six themes and a reviewer who disagrees about brass.
Why a second row and not a third slot
The footer already carries two buttons and export.pricingNote at 560px. A fourth item in that row either wraps or forces the note off the line — and the note is a live string the price addendum just ratified across two call sites. Adding a row costs 45px; editing a ratified layout costs an argument.
It is also the placement that lets the phone sheet be identical rather than analogous: there the local action is one full-width button, so a trailing slot does not exist at all, and a second row does.
What the link does not change
The dialog’s tabs, the list block, both local buttons, the note, the est. total row and the info callout are byte-identical to shipped. export.pricingNote keeps its ratified wording: the exports still copy names only, and the link does not make that false — it does not export anything.
Decklist tab — rejected, not deferred
No link on the Decklist tab, and no slot held for one. A 62-card basket including the 54 the player already owns is not a thing anyone wants bought, and a link that silently means the 8 while the tab says 62 is a mislabel. The Shopping tab is the only tab where a basket is the unit — the same reason the tab exists.

One state, and now for two reasons. The link reads no price, so a card we have a price for and one we don’t render identically — and the destination does not vary by card either. The hairline now does real work: a link sitting directly under 6 printings you own ▾ would read as buy this printing, which is the one thing it does not mean.
§2.2 · the label says the shelf out loud
A bare Buy at Mana Pool is not wrong, and it is not enough: it is silent about the one thing that makes this destination unlike a checkout. A player who expects one listing and lands on a grid of twelve has been surprised by us — precisely the cost the glyph and the named destination exist to avoid.
So any printing — two words that promise a plural, name no price, pre-select nothing, and keep Buy, which is still the player’s intent. See all printings was the alternative: more literal, and it demotes the link to a lookup on a surface that already has a printing picker two rows up.
Why the last position, and not the meta grid
The grid’s four cells are facts about the card, each a label over a value. An action in a fact grid has no label that fits and no cell that means do this. Putting it last, under a hairline, gives the modal the actions row it did not have — with exactly one action in it, which is why no row had to be invented.
And it is the same position as the export dialog’s. Two surfaces, one rule, and the next link-out inherits a place to go rather than a precedent to argue about.
Three routes, one modal — nothing route-dependent
deck builder  → same link, same place
/collection    → same link, same place
commander pg → same link, same place
The link takes the oracle card and nothing else — not the printing on screen, not the caller, not the collection. No deck context, no owned/unowned branch, no from= in the label. There is one link per card in the whole app, which is the strongest form the parity table’s claim could take.
Unconditional, and now over-determined
A player who owns one copy is the player most likely to want a second, a foil, or a different printing. Branching on ownership would hide the link from exactly them, and would make the modal’s content depend on the collection — which the availability block already reports and does not gate.
§2.2 adds a second reason, and it is the simpler one: nothing about the destination varies by card. There is no condition left to branch on — not ownership, not price, not printing — so a conditional would be a branch with one arm.
Desktop: the link joins the pinned nav row
Drawn below, because it cannot be extrapolated from the sheets: desktop pins ‹ Prev · N of M · Next › outside the scroll column, already under its own rule. So the modal did have an actions row, and R2 was wrong to say it lacked one.
Above it puts two hairlines 44px apart and a one-item row between them — and puts the link in the scroll column, where it leaves the screen while a navigation row holds the bottom. Replaces it is not on the table: prev/next is a shipped affordance doing an unrelated job, and no design pass trades a player’s way through their deck for a store link. Joins it costs no hairline, no height, and no scroll.

The pinned row and its var(--border) rule are shipped; the link is the only addition, and the row’s padding is matched to the shipped footer’s rather than measured. Mobile has no equivalent row, which is why the 390px sheets place the link differently and correctly.
Link left, navigation right — and why that order
The counter has to stay between its arrows, so the nav cluster cannot be split or moved; it keeps the trailing edge it already has. That leaves the leading edge for the link, which is the reading position for the one item in the row that is not navigation.
They do not read as one control: the nav is right-aligned, unlined, mono-numbered and --fg-dim; the link is left, underlined, glyphed and brass. 16px of gap and a flex spacer between them, never a divider — a divider inside a row under a rule is two rules doing one job.
R2’s rule, in its corrected form
R2 said the outbound link is the last thing in its container, below a hairline. That was the mobile case mistaken for the rule. Corrected: the link-out lives in its container’s pinned bottom row, below the hairline that row already has — and where no pinned row exists, it becomes one.
Both surfaces then satisfy it without a hairline of the link’s invention: the export footer’s rule is shipped, the modal’s is shipped. The convention adds no dividers to this app, which is a better inheritance for the next link-out than R2’s wording would have left.
What the pinned row fixes about the scroll column
A link at the end of the content column is a link the player must scroll to find, on a surface whose height is fixed and whose content is not. Pinning it means the link is at the same place for every card — a five-line oracle text and a planeswalker with four abilities put it in the same pixel.
It also keeps the hairline’s meaning intact where it matters most: the printing picker is now clearly inside the scroll column and the link clearly outside it, so nothing reads as buy this printing.

Ratios computed from the theme table’s own hexes against each theme’s --surface. Recompute in CI rather than trusting these; the ranking is the load-bearing part, not the second decimal.
One rule, not two exceptions
--accent is the obvious ink and it is the wrong one: phyrexia lands at 4.3 and kaldheim at 4.5 for 14px text. The tempting fix is a per-theme override for those two, which is the shape this handoff has rejected twice — a primitive that looks different in some themes is six primitives.
So the link’s rest ink is the lighter step that already exists in every theme. One token, six themes, AA clear in all six, and no new value added to the table.
Then hover goes to --fg, which is better anyway
Spending --accent-hover on rest leaves hover nowhere to go inside the accent ramp — so hover leaves it: --fg, the palette’s brightest ink, with the underline at full opacity. It brightens in all six, it is the strongest contrast available, and it makes hover the one moment the link stops being brass — which is exactly the moment the player is about to leave.
Item 5 stands
The ink question is about 14px text on six backgrounds; neither blocker touches it. The active-ink correction leans on this measurement rather than reopening it — a darker press state fails in exactly the two themes named above.
The ring keeps --accent
A 2px outline is a graphic, not text: 3:1 is the bar and all six clear it comfortably. Keeping it on --accent also matches every other focus ring in the app, so the link’s focus state is the app’s, not its own. Only its mechanism changed in R3, not its colour.

Three things worth one look in place
• The desktop row at its narrowest. Link plus nav is 29 characters and three nav elements; below the modal’s min-width the link should wrap before at Mana Pool rather than compress the counter.
• The lost list row at 590px. Drawn as four and a half rows; if that reads as cramped, the sheet should grow for the list, not lose the link.
• Tab order in the pinned row. The link precedes Prev/Next in DOM order, matching its leading position. If the shipped nav is focus-trapped first for keyboard deck-walking, keep the nav first and leave the visual order alone.
