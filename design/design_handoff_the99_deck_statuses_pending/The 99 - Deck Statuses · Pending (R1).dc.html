Drafts, committing, and versions /decks · opens a deck’s Versions tab
A deck has a committed list and a draft you are working on. Every add and cut sits in the draft until you commit it, and committing cuts a version you can read back. Cards staged in a draft already count against your collection, which is why a draft can make another deck read short 1.

3a deck you have never committed shows an unfilled status badge wherever it is listed — it is a plan, not a deck yet. Once you commit v1 the badge fills in, and a bright edge appears whenever the list drifts from the version again.
Why the versions entry and not the statuses one
Because neither mark is a status, and that entry teaches the six. Putting them there would be the seventh-value mistake in prose — and the entry is already at the template’s four-key cap, so it has no room that would not cost a status its line.
The versions entry owns the question both marks answer — is this list the version? — and has two keys. One line covers both, in the order a deck lives them: unfilled while it is a plan, filled once v1 lands, edged whenever it drifts again.
No new entry, so the count does not move. Thirty-three stays thirty-three, chapter 03 and chapter 04 keep their counts, no NEW plate is spent, and since stays 1 on both. An amendment to a shipped entry is not news the reader has to be told about.

It gains “An unfilled badge belongs to a deck you have never committed — a plan rather than a deck. A filled badge with a bright left edge is a real deck you have edited since its last version. Neither is a seventh status.” at the end of its caption, and not a key line: the four keys are the six values, and the whole point is that neither mark is one of them.

“When would a Draft ever be pending?”
By cutting a committed deck to nothing — and that is the only way. Rare, and it is also the state where the pill lies loudest: Draft on a deck holding a committed 99-card v2.
R1’s reason for excluding it was false. It said a deck at 0 has never committed a version. It can have: built is the draft’s count, not the version’s. The exclusion is withdrawn — the mark applies to all six values.
“When would Building ever come into play?”
Constantly — it is the commonest draft there is. Cut three, add three: between the cuts and the adds the deck is at 96, which is Building. Add first and it is at 102, which is Over.
Which is the finding. One deck flips Complete → Building → Over → Complete in the course of one edit. The count pill goes transient while a draft is open — it reports the editing, not the deck. Nothing is blocked either way: state 6 already rules that a committed 101 commits fine.

Ruling 1 · the pill keeps its value
The six values partition one fact — how the matched count stands against the body size. Pending is a different axis: whether the list on screen is the list that was committed. A Complete deck with one swap is still ninety-nine resolved, owned cards.

Ruling 5 · where it sits in the facet list
Not a seventh Status row. A row in that list is a value of STATUS_ORDER: it serialises to ?status=, it is what filterDecks compares statusPill against, and the heading's 6 of 6 is an assertion about the six. Ticking a seventh would OR it against the others and return a deck whose pill says something else.
Its own heading, immediately below Status — one row, Has pending changes. Adjacency says the two are read together; the boundary says they are different questions. Within a heading values OR, across headings they AND, exactly as every other facet, so Complete + pending is one tick each and an AND between them.
One boolean, not a pair. No Clean row: it is the unticked state of this one, and a two-row heading where the second is the negation of the first is a control that can say nothing. ?pending=1, absent when off, and activeFilterCount gains one — a value, which is what the phone badge counts.
Brass on the heading, and only while it is on. The other three headings are --fg-faint; this one is the single-row facet that would otherwise read as a stray checkbox.
