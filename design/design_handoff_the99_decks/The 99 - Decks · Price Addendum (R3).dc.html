Why a third string was the wrong fix
Hanging the total off the spine made it look like a property of the deck — which is what pulled R1 into inventing a deck-level noun for it in the first place. It is the sum of what one filter is showing. Put it where its scope is and the surface question stops recurring.
The mobile omission is preserved, not overturned
That comment is a considered decision about restatement: 0 owned · 0 to buy under 0 / 99 says one thing twice. A pass that needed the line at phone width would be reversing it sideways. This one doesn’t need it at all.
The derivation is now total
priced  = live && cents !== null
foilOnly= live && cents null &&
         (foil ?? etched) !== null
soldOut = row exists && !live
absent  = default
A live row with all three columns null tells us nothing about buying the card — which is what the bare tag means. Counts as unpriced in the strip’s denominator.

Renamed, not shared
• It keeps what the label earned. Players who learned Cheapest first recognise it; two words disambiguate rather than replace.
• A dollar figure is never “to cast.” The collision becomes impossible in the only phrase where it could occur, without importing jargon — Lowest mana value is precise and the wrong register for this voice.
• Fail-quiet survives verbatim. After the rename nothing ranks, filters or sorts on price, so R1’s argument stands as written. That is the real reason to rename rather than annotate: a caveat on a locked argument is a slow leak.
Sharing the word, declined — with precedent
The availability pass turned down available as a one-word swap because buy and the marker share a tile four millimetres apart, and available next to buy reads as purchasable. This is the same collision with the currencies reversed: a price-shaped word above a price-shaped figure, on the same shelf. One vocabulary per concept, and money already has this one.

A word is recognised; a figure is read
buy survives a poor ratio because a player matches its shape, its colour and its position. $45.00 is read digit by digit, and a misread digit is a wrong number in somebody’s budget. The tail is the first thing on that scrim that has to be read rather than recognised, so the scrim it inherited is no longer sufficient — pre-existing or not.
Unconditional, and 10 points only
Not “raised when a figure renders” — that is a primitive deciding how to look from its own content, which is the objection R5 made to a conditional pip ring. One value, no state; the bare tag gets a darker scrim it doesn’t need at a cost of nothing.
A larger step, or an opaque plate, starts reading as a chip glued to the card rather than a label on it — and the tag’s whole position in the tile anatomy depends on looking like part of the art’s furniture. Nothing else moves: the inset stays at 50%, the inks stay as the R2 token table has them.
If 92% still falls short
The next move is the theme’s --warning, not another 10 points of scrim. Six themes are the variable here; the tag is not.
