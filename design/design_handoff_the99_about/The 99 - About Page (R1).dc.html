ABOUT PAGE R1 nothing in the app explains what the app does
§open for design · all five, answered

It is a reference document, not a screen — one column of twenty-six linked entries under four chapter headings, an index in the margin at desktop and in the flow at phone, and a “new” mark that spends the one thing this app still has: an inverted plate with a word in it.

The six rulings are taken as given and none of them is reopened. What follows answers the five calls the spec carried into the handoff, in the order it asked them, each with the alternative that lost drawn rather than described.

1 · the entry
A row, and the whole row is the link. Title, then the route in mono accent on the same baseline, then one paragraph. The route is the link affordance — twenty-six accent titles would be an accent field, and the destination is the thing worth reading anyway. Cards rejected in 1b.
2 · the mark
A mono NEW knocked out of a filled accent plate. Not a shape, because every small geometry is booked; not a dashed box, because that box now means card mark. Accent is spendable here for one reason: no cardboard appears on this page, so nothing on it can be mistaken for in use or to buy. Three alternatives drawn in §2.
3 · one page, index in the margin
One scroll at both widths. Desktop gains a 216px sticky index on the right, never the left — the left edge is already the app’s nav rail and a second column of links there is a sub-nav. Phone gets the same four items inline, once, under the lede: contents, not a sticky control, and never a second horizontal scroller.
4 · dismiss
Bottom of the page, in the flow, never sticky. It writes “I have read version N”, so it must sit where reading ends. The gate is already cleared by the cookie, so nothing traps a reader who scrolls away — which is exactly what makes a sticky button unnecessary rather than merely unwelcome. Got it, and a mono clause naming where it lands you.
5 · the menu row
About The 99, first — above Manage account. The shipped two rows are account acts and the second is destructive; a destination dropped between them splits a pair. And it carries no badge, ever — a dot on the avatar is ruling 3’s banner with extra steps.

Below: five findings read at main that change the work (§0), the entry row and the card grid that lost (§1), the mark and its three rejected forms (§2), the page at full density for a reader at version 0 (§3), the behind and current readers (§4), all three states at phone (§5), the account menu at both widths (§6), and the contract (§7).

§0

Five findings from source, three of which cost engineering time the spec does not budget

The spec calls the menu change “a third row” and “one insertion”. Read at main, account-menu.tsx is hard-wired to exactly two items in three places, and the new row is the first link the menu has ever held. That is still small, but it is not an array append.

1 · the roving focus is written for two
MenuItems holds a two-element ref tuple ([useRef(), useRef()] — hooks, so it cannot be mapped) and wraps with (active + delta + 2) % 2. Both literals are the count. A third row that leaves them is a menu where ↓ skips the new item and never reaches it.
2 · it is a link, and every row is a button
Both shipped rows are <button type="button"> with onClick; About is a navigation and must be a real <Link href="/about"> so it opens in a new tab and shows a URL on hover. Consequences: the ref tuple becomes HTMLElement, disabled does not exist on an anchor (use aria-disabled while busy), and the panel must close on press or it survives the navigation.
3 · inserting first moves a rule
The sheet divides its rows with i === 0 ? "" : "border-t", so the first row is the one without a top rule. Going in first, About takes that slot and Manage account gains a rule — correct by the existing expression, and worth naming so it reads as intended rather than as a regression in review.
4 · row-shell is the card row shell
row-shell.ts declares itself as what five rows standing for a card share, and already excludes the search result row for being “a different object that happens to also be a list item”. An about entry is that same kind of not-a-card-row, so it takes the radius and nothing else: ROW_RADIUS_PX = 7, imported, with padding and gap its own — which is exactly the split that file spends its comment defending.
5 · first-run’s numerals belong to steps
first-run.tsx numbers its items because they are ordered and lockable — a 22px box, step(1, 3), a padlock, --fg-locked on the two you cannot do yet. About entries are neither ordered nor lockable, so numerals go on the four chapters and never on the twenty-six entries. What is borrowed: the chapter’s mono eyebrow, the display-face heading, and the rule that a locked or faint token never carries a fact a sighted reader needs.

why the route carries the accent
A page of links whose titles are all accent is an accent field with paragraphs in it, and accent is this app’s loudest token. Moving the link colour onto the route keeps the affordance unmistakable at a fifth of the area — and the route is the fact the page exists to teach. Titles stay --fg at 600, which is also what lets the mark beside them read.
display type stops at the chapter
Cinzel carries the h1 and the four chapter titles. Entry titles are sans at 600: twenty-six display-face labels at 15.5px is a texture rather than a hierarchy, and first-run spends font-display on three items, not twenty-six.
the “opens” clause, and when it is absent
Where the route is only the way in — a modal, a tab, a panel — a mono --fg-faint clause says what opens once you are there. It is omitted, never empty, when the route is the feature. It is decoration in the accessible name’s terms only: the body repeats it in words, because --fg-faint must not be the only carrier of a fact.

Tempting, because it costs no hue and the family is already taught. But that box was just ratified to mean a mark on a card — $ and P — and an entry is not a card. Worse, a dashed edge reads as provisional, which is the opposite of what this mark says: the thing shipped.
2d REJECTED accent title, no mark
Cards you are proxying /decks
The cheapest possible mark: no new object at all. It fails because nothing decodes it — a reader seeing two accent titles among twenty-six has no way to learn what they share, and the one legend that could say so would be a key on a page that is already all words. A mark that needs teaching should be a word instead, which is how 2a was arrived at.
three carriers, never one
The plate on the entry, the count on its chapter heading (2 new), and the page eyebrow (2 new since you were last here). The accessible name adds New in this version as an sr-only span inside the plate, the way BuyTag’s collapsed density already does.
the mark is never on a shipped surface
It exists on /about and nowhere else — no dot on the avatar, no badge in the account menu, nothing on Home. That is ruling 3 enforced by inventory: the redirect is the notification, and a second notifier is the banner the ruling refused.
and it is gone on the next visit
The mark renders on seen > 0 && entry.since > seen, and dismiss writes seen, so it is a one-visit object. Nothing in the page decays or needs clearing, and seen === 0 marks nothing at all — the spec’s rule, not an edge case.
§3

The page at desktop, reader at version 0 — twenty-six entries, nothing marked, no dismiss

Drawn at full inventory rather than sampled, because density is the thing a reference page is judged on. Chapter 3 has eleven entries — that is the app, not a drafting accident, and a page that pretends otherwise would be redesigned the day it is filled. The eyebrow slot above the h1 is absent here: it exists only when something is new.

What The 99 does

Everything here is scored against the cards you actually own — decks, commanders, set completion and the buy list all read from your collection. Four chapters, in the order you would use them, and every entry links to where it lives.

01 Your collection
Everything downstream is scored against what you own, so this is where the app starts.
Import a collection/collection/replace
A CSV export from Moxfield, Archidekt, Deckbox or ManaBox, or a list pasted straight in. An import replaces what is there rather than adding to it, and you see a preview before anything is written.
Rows the import could not place/collection/replaceopens the issue inspector
Anything that could not be matched to a printing is listed with the reason, in an inspector on the import screen. A pasted list carries no set or collector number, which is why a pasted collection cannot drive set completion.
Browse what you own/collection
Every card in your collection, searchable and filterable, with how many copies you have and how many of them are committed to a deck.
Set completion/collection/sets
How close each set is to complete, and what is missing from the ones you are closest on. It reads set and collector numbers, so it needs a CSV rather than a pasted list.
What a card costs to buy/collectionopens the card detail
Prices come from ManaPool and refresh weekly. A card with no price was never stocked there, which is not the same as free.
The card itself — printings, art, both faces/collectionopens the card detail
Every printing of a card, its art at full size, and the back of a double-faced card. It opens from a card anywhere in the app, not only here.
02 Finding a commander
Two lanes: what your collection already supports, and what it would support if you bought one card.
Commanders you can build today/commanders
Ranked by how much of a deck you already own for them. Filter by colour identity and reorder as you like.
Commanders worth acquiring/discover
The same ranking run over commanders you do not own — the one card that would unlock the most of what is already on your shelf.
Why this commander/commandersopens a commander
The reasoning behind a ranking: which themes in your collection it is using, and how much of each it reaches.
What the deck would be made of/commandersopens a commander
The cards you already own that would go in, grouped by the job they do, before you commit to building anything.
Partners/commandersopens a commander
Commanders that pair, and what the pair’s wider colour identity opens up in your collection.
03 Building the 99
The longest chapter, in the order a deck actually gets built.
Start a deck/decks/new
Name it, pick a commander, and the 99 fills from there.
Import a decklist you already have/decks/import
Paste a list or bring a file. Cards you own are matched to your own copies; the rest arrive as a buy list rather than as a problem.
The deck page/decksopens a deck
One deck in one place: the list by role, what it still needs, its games and its versions.
Adding cards/decksopens a deck’s Add panel
Search your collection or all of Magic, with the cards you own marked, and add without leaving the list you are looking at.
Topping off the last slots/decksopens a deck
When a deck is nearly there, this fills the remaining slots from what you own instead of making you search for them one at a time.
Who holds this card/decksopens a deck
A card you own one copy of cannot be in two committed decks at once. The deck that sleeved it first holds it, and the other deck is told which one that is. A card staged into a draft counts against the supply from the moment you add it.
What you would need to buy/decksopens the shopping roll-up
Every card your decks want and you do not own, priced and grouped, across the whole library rather than one deck at a time.
Shelves/decks
Folders for decks. A deck sits on one shelf or on none, and choosing a shelf narrows the library rather than filtering it.
Versions/decksopens a deck’s Versions tab
A deck has a committed list and a draft you are working on. Committing the draft cuts a version, and you can read any earlier one.
Reviewing a draft before you commit it/decksopens a deck’s pending banner
Every add and cut in the draft, beside the committed list, before any of it counts against your collection.
Export a decklist/decksopens a deck
The list as plain text, in the format the other deck sites read.
04 Playing and tuning
Home is the end of the loop rather than the start of it, so it is described here.
Log a game/decksopens a deck’s Games tab
Who played, who won, and how long it ran. A few seconds after the game, on the deck you played.
What the log tells you/decksopens a deck’s Games tab
How a deck has done, against whom, and how it has done since the version you are on.
Picking up where you left off/
Home leads with the deck you touched last and what it is waiting on.
Where your collection is committed/
How much of what you own is in a deck, how much is free, and which decks are asking for more than you have.

measure, not width
Entry bodies cap at 600px and the lede at 620, inside a column that is wider than both. The column is what the hover ground spans; the paragraph is what a reader’s eye spans, and at 13.5px 600px is about 78 characters. Nothing here is centred.
the page ends on its last entry
One hairline and the page stops. No footer, no “that is everything”, no link back to Home — the rail is right there, and refresh.ts’ voice rule forbids a sentence whose content is that the reader is finished.

§7

The contract

STRUCTUREOne scrolling document. Four <section> chapters, each an h2 with a blurb and a list of entries; every entry is a single <Link> wrapping title, route, optional opens-clause and body. No tabs, no accordion, no jump control.
INDEX216px sticky column on the right at ≥1000px; four 44px rows in the flow below it, with entry counts. Same four strings, one source. Scroll-spy via IntersectionObserver on the chapter headings, desktop only — the phone list does not track.
MARKMono 9.5/600, 0.1em, --bg on --accent, radius 3, padding:2px 5px, flex-none, with an sr-only “New in this version”. Rendered on seen > 0 && entry.since > seen and nowhere outside /about. Chapter heading carries N new; the page eyebrow carries the total.
DISMISSRendered only when seen < ABOUT_VERSION. In the flow at the foot, after the last entry and its hairline, left-aligned at desktop (36px) and full-width at phone (44px). Primary skin: --fg ground, --bg ink. Never sticky, never disabled, never a second copy at the top.
MENUOne row, About The 99, index 0 in MenuItems’ list, as a <Link href="/about"> with role="menuitem", closing the panel on press. The ref tuple and the % 2 wrap both become the item count, and busy maps to aria-disabled on the anchor. No badge, ever.
A11YOne h1, four h2, entry titles as h3 inside the link so the page is navigable by heading. Each link’s accessible name is title + route + mark; the route is spoken, not decorative. The index is a nav labelled Contents with aria-current on the tracked chapter. No live region: nothing on this page changes after load.
CONTRASTBodies are --fg-body, blurbs --fg-muted, and --fg-faint carries only the opens-clause, which the body repeats in words. Nothing on this page uses --fg-locked or --fg-disabled — there is nothing locked here, which is the difference between this page and first run.
MOTIONNone. No entry animation, no scroll effect, no transition on the index’s selection. The menu sheet and popover keep the animations they ship with, unchanged. No reduced-motion state, because there is no motion to reduce.
