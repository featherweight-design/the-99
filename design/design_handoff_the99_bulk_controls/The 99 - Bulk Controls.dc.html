The four calls the brief asks for
§5.4 · is it two buttons
Two ghost controls, stacked, in a fixed column, with the concession on the same line as its own button. Not a segmented control (which makes the pool a mode the player sets, and this surface has already refused the ownership filter), not a primary + secondary (which ranks one concession above the other), not a disclosure (which hides one figure until after the choice is half made).
§5.1 · the two labels
Both name the pool, both state the same count, neither names its own concession. Select 12 from my collection and Select the 12 this deck needs. They select rather than commit, because on this surface nothing is added until Add 12 cards.
§5.2 · zero and absent
A concession that is nil is still stated, in the concession’s own slot: matches every recommendation. A price read that degrades drops the figure and keeps the count — the shipped rule from price.ts, never a dash and never $0.00. A recommendation that cannot be measured says so rather than inventing a gap.
§5.3 · the phone
Nothing new is pinned. The bottom slot keeps one occupant — the commit bar — and the pair stacks in the selection head, in the flow, above the rows it is about. Two 44px rows, each with its concession beneath its own label. Clear all selections demotes to a text-weight control on the head’s own line.
Not redrawn, per §4
The candidate row’s shell and its buy tag · the absent-price case the row already draws · buyCount / buyMoney for the selected set · the fill’s own buying behaviour · the per-pass undo in provenance-line.tsx, which is how a player reverses a bulk fill and is not duplicated here in any form · the pinned commit bar’s geometry · the column header, the cap line, the decline and its restore. Why a fixed 244px button column
The locked part of §3 is that both concessions are legible before either is pressed — which is a claim about comparison, not about visibility. Two labels of different length push their notes to different x positions, and two figures that do not share a left edge are read one after the other instead of against each other.

So the buttons take one fixed column and the concessions form a second. 244px is the wider label at its largest figure (Select the 47 this deck needs, 29 mono characters at 11px) plus the ghost’s padding either side; nothing in the pair wraps up to that figure, and the count cannot exceed the open slots.

Both controls are ghosts at equal weight. A primary would rank one concession over the other, and this pair exists precisely because the app cannot know which the player minds — Hugh’s own reasoning is that both are acceptable once stated. The brass outline in this list is already spoken for by Add N cards, which is the only control here that changes the deck.

Rejected: a segmented control. It makes the pool a mode the player sets and leaves set, which is the ownership filter this product has ruled against, one level down.

Rejected: one button with a disclosed alternative. The second concession then arrives after the first choice is half made — the exact failure §7 says a post-press check would let through.

Rejected: putting the concessions in a caption above the pair. One sentence carrying two figures makes the reader assign each to its own control, and at 390 it wraps to three lines before either button is on screen.

The bottom slot is not where this goes
The quick-add dock is already stood down and its slot already reassigned — while the list is open the commit bar holds it, and the top-off handoff’s rule is that one bottom slot gets one occupant. So the pair has no claim on it, and the question §5.3 asks is answered by where the pair belongs rather than by what the dock is doing.

A bulk selection is a decision about the list, so it sits at the top of the list. Pinning it would put a control that changes twelve rows below the rows it changes, and would make the bar taller at the one width where the bar’s height is the page’s scroll budget. The commit bar keeps its measured geometry exactly: status line, counts line, 44px primary plus Cancel.

Two 44px rows, not two halves of one row. Side by side at 390 each label gets ~175px — Select the 12 this deck needs is 29 mono characters and wraps to three lines in that width, and the two concessions then have nowhere to sit but under the pair, where neither belongs to a button.

The concession sits under its own button, indented to its edge, not beside it: at 390 there is no second column to be had, and a figure to the right of a full-width control reads as part of the next thing down.

Clear all selections loses its box. It is a reset rather than a choice, it concedes nothing, and boxing it would make three equal-looking controls out of a pair and a reset. Text weight, dashed rule, 44px hit area, on the head’s own line opposite the ordering note.
For probe:last-slots
The pair is addressable without reading its copy: data-bulk-fill="collection" and data-bulk-fill="best" on the two buttons, and data-bulk-concession on each note — the same reason data-top-off-type exists a few rows down, where recognising a column by its text let a card called Land pass for a type.

The check §7 asks for is a pre-press read of both notes: assert two concession nodes with non-empty text while zero rows are selected, then press one and assert the selected count. A probe that only read post-press state would pass the shipped defect.

Each concession is also inside its button’s accessible name, so the pre-press read is true for a screen reader too. The visible note carries aria-hidden so the figure is not spoken twice.

The bar’s pinned geometry is unchanged, so that assertion stands as written. What needs adding is the head at both widths — one row per control at 390, two columns at 1300 — and the row’s marks, which are aria-hidden glyphs and must be asserted through the row’s accessible name, never by counting SVGs.
Surface 4 · the cost track and the card mark — both shipped, neither re-specified
The four marks, at row size
{{ k.title }} {{ k.note }}
Nothing is re-specified. Round 1 set the ink (--fact #8FA6BE), the bare-no-container rule, the sizes (14px on a row, 12px floor) and the tooltip; Round 2 added the tutor mark and the solid/stroked split. This surface only says where they go on this row: immediately after the name, before every boxed atom, because boxed is about your copies and bare is about the card.
Why the mark belongs on a candidate row
This list is the last moment a player can decline the card. Everywhere else the mark annotates a card they already own or already sleeved; here it annotates one they are about to take, and a bracket fact learned after the commit is a fact learned too late — it costs an undo, a shopping-list edit, or a bracket the deck did not mean to be in.

And the right-hand control buys. Select the 12 this deck needs can put a Game Changer on the shopping list for real money; the mark is what makes that a choice rather than a surprise.

It costs the row nothing. A 14px glyph rides in the name’s own line, the name column is flex-auto, and Round 1’s standing finding is that no row grows and nothing yields for it. Multiple marks on one card sit in factsFor’s fixed order.

Not drawn: a bulk control that avoids them. “Fill without Game Changers” is a third concession and a third pool, and the brief locks the pair at two. The mark informs the hand-made edit the player makes after the press — which is what the whole review list is for.

Costs eng one join. The candidate pool is SynergyCard, which carries no facts; attachBracketFacts is written and is already quiet on failure, so a degraded read draws fewer marks and never an empty claim. Tutors are Round 2’s detection and may not be on the card row yet — if it lands later, it lands as one more entry in the same list.
The cost track
Shipped CostPips, at the component’s own figures — 12px pips, 3px gap, five slots inside the 72px COST_TRACK on desktop, with the sixth symbol onward folded into a generic +N pip; 13px, uncapped and unshingled, on the phone meta line. The colours are WUBRG_COLOR, which are themed tokens and not this canvas’s literals.

A costless row still holds the column open on desktop, and mounts nothing at all on the phone — the component’s own rule, and the reason the header and the rows stay on one vertical.

The pips are aria-hidden; the cost is spoken once, uncapped, from the row’s accessible name, which is where this list already speaks it.
