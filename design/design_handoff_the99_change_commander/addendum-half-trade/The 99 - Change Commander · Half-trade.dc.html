CHANGE COMMANDER · ADDENDUM the half-trade · held back in the build until this is drawn

When one half of an incoming pair is already in the 98, one old commander takes its slot and the other leaves the deck.

In the example, Thrasios and Tymna lead. Ishai and Reyhan are offered, and Reyhan is one of the 98. The shipped confirm would say The two trade places, which is false: three cards move, not two. Today offerable() hides the candidate, and the write refuses it. This addendum gives it a confirm, a sentence and an outcome. Nothing else in R3 changes.

the new call · which old half stays
The build has no answer to this, only an order. write.ts hands the freed slot to departing[0], the commander column. That's an accident of storage, and either old commander might be the one worth keeping. So the confirm asks, with a two-way choice that defaults to that same half. One tap still confirms. Hugh can rule for a fixed rule instead (note 2).
the labels move to the cards
One side label can't say two things. The out side reads Leave the command zone, which is true of both old halves. Under each card, a mono line says where that card goes: into the 98 or leaves the deck, and on the in side from the 98 or free. The arrow stays → because the leaders change. ⇄ is kept for a whole trade.
the outcome is two shipped shapes, joined
The banner is the trade's (+1 / −1, with its command-zone hints), naming Thrasios in and Reyhan out. The review note is the pair's, with one clause for each fate. Tymna, like any released commander, is named only where a commit will release her: the review note and the pool count.
§1

The confirm

The choice is live in both frames: press Tymna and the card lines and the sentence follow. It sits between the plates and the sentence, so the reader sees what moves before being asked, and reads the result right under the choice. It is a radiogroup labelled by its question. At 390 it takes the full width with 44px options.

In the list, before the confirm

Once it is offered, the pair sorts like any pair: in Yours, because its half from the 98 is owned and Ishai is free. Reyhan carries the shipped in this deck · trades places mark. That is true of that half, and it is how the reader learns, before pressing Choose, that this candidate moves a card out of the 98. No new string is needed.

§5

For engineering and for Hugh

1 · the write
offerable() stops holding the case back, in the loader and in the write. changeCommander takes staysOracleId, which must be one of the departing halves. The write gives the slot to that half, not to departing[0], and refuses a value that isn't departing. The CommanderSection's trade test picks the new note whenever the pair has one leaving and one arriving from the body.
2 · for Hugh
Ask, or rule? Recommended: ask, as drawn. The alternative is a fixed rule, where the commander column always stays. With that rule the choice row goes, and nothing else in the drawing changes. Staging indexes one usable pair, so this case is rare. Rare is also why a reader is unlikely to know which of their two commanders the app would keep.
3 · not drawn, still held back
In the sibling case the new pair keeps one old half. For example, Thrasios + Ravos replace Thrasios + Tymna, with Ravos in the 98. That is a plain single trade (Tymna ⇄ Ravos) under a kept half, but confirm.trade's pair name would still read wrong. It stays held back until it gets its own frame. Say if you want it drawn.
