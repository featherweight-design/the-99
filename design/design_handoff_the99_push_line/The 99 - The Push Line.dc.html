R1

The push line and the upgrade path

Round 4 of 5 · the first round that answers what should I do?

Two objects that share a subject and nothing else. The push line is a property of a candidate card — so it is drawn on the candidate, wherever a candidate can be committed, and never on the analysis tab, which is about the deck you have. The upgrade path is a property of a target — so it is drawn on the analysis tab, under the climb’s lower rung, as the full answer to the question that rung already asks.

The push line belongs to the candidate, so it is drawn on every surface where a candidate can be committed — quick add, Consider, and the top-off list — in one slot with two densities: the sentence where a row has a line to spare, the shape’s name alone where it does not. 1a 2 · Not on the analysis tab. The atom carries no link to the tab either — the verdict it would link to is the one that has not changed yet. 3 · A mark means movement. 1a One atom, three hosts — the round’s biggest call §4.1

The push line fires at the moment of an add, and the moment of an add is a row, not a page. Putting the sentence on the analysis tab would make the player leave the list they are choosing from to read a preview about one thing in it, and then come back — and would put a claim about a card the deck does not hold inside the block whose whole authority is that it describes the deck that it does. So the atom lives on the candidate, in the same slot, on all three surfaces that can commit one.

The row already has the width for a sentence, and quick add is the surface where a player adds one card on purpose — the exact event the push line previews. The slot sits below the row’s own line, above nothing: it never displaces the name, the price or the Add.

168px cannot hold a sentence, and a tile grid that wraps one becomes six tiles of ragged height. So the tile carries the shape’s name and its mark — at most three words, no arrow, no range — and the sentence prints in the card’s own detail modal, which the art and the name already open (`CardDetailTrigger`, shipped since #127). No new door, no tooltip, no hover-only text.

Thirty-eight rows, and most of them say nothing — which is what makes the slot readable here rather than noisy: a push line on a top-off row is rare, so it reads as a flag on the one row that earned it. It never goes on the commit bar. bracketDelta takes one candidate; a bracket preview for a 27-card batch is a computation nobody has written and a sentence nobody could defend.
Not a third host, and the reason is arithmetic. /commanders/[slug]’s synergy rows toggle a selection that a Build link carries into deck creation. There is no deck and no profile, so bracketDelta has nothing to evaluate against — drawing the slot there would specify a preview the flow cannot compute.
It does not persist after the add (§4.5). The card is in the deck now, and the verdict block is the record. A preview that outlives its subject becomes a claim about a deck that has already changed — and it would be the app’s only self-congratulating sentence. No toast, no afterglow, no badge on the tab.
No link to the analysis tab. Tempting, and wrong: the tab still reads the deck without this card, so the link would promise an explanation of something that has not happened. The atom is self-contained; the rule it names is the rule the climb already spells out.
1b Five shapes, five sentences — and one deliberate absence §3.1

One rule orders the whole set: a bracket mark is the mark of movement. moves and a counts that moved draw marks on either side of an arrow; a fact draws none, and is inked --fact under a label naming what kind of fact it is. That is how a reader tells “this changes your bracket” from “this is true and changes nothing” without reading either sentence to the end.

Round 2’s five fact marks ride the card names, here as everywhere. Bolt, stripped peak, turn again, the knot and drawn up, bare and in --fact at 12px beside a name — on the host row, inside the sentence and in the upgrade path’s candidate lists alike. A card carries every mark that is true of it (Underworld Breach is bolt and knot; a loop combo’s halves are knot and turn again), and a card that fires no rule carries none — which is the same rule as the atom’s: presence is information. The marks are aria-hidden; the facts are in the row’s accessible name.

Two brackets on both sides — the widest the grammar gets, and the reason the sentence names ranges and never a slot. Violation.bracket is not printed as a number: the rule is stated, the numeral stays in the mark.
moves · extraTurns at bracket 1 · [1,2] → [2]
If you add this
→ Moves what your cards permit from Exhibition or Core to Core. Exhibition is the one bracket that counts extra-turn cards, and it allows none.
A range collapsing to one of its own members — the case that breaks any sentence built on from N to M. Core is on both sides and the sentence stays true, because it is about what the deck permits, not where it sits.

The loop leads, not the widening. delta.ts attributes this to the extra-turn clause on purpose — a completed infinite-turns combo breaks Upgraded outright, where a generic infinite is decided by tutor coverage. The sentence names the combo, never the clause number.

Never a move, and it says so. The figure is stated because the findings block states it too; what the copy may not do is imply a ceiling. Upgraded allows none would be false and you may be pushing it would be us scoring what we just refused to score.

The clause deleted in October 2025 stays deleted — and the sentence still can’t say tutors don’t matter, because since 2026-09-20 coverage decides the 3-vs-4 boundary. — and yours can’t, yet is the whole reconciliation: no limit, real consequence, stated as coverage rather than as a count.
none · drawn as an absence
Swords to Plowshares 86% of decks
No slot, no line, no reassurance. A nothing changes note on thirty-eight rows is how a reader learns to skip the slot, and then misses the one row that moved their bracket. The atom’s value is that its presence is information. The dashed edge here is the canvas saying nothing is drawn; the shipped row has no edge at all.
1c The two-clause sentence, and the count leads §4.4
the call — count, then consequence, then evidence
If you add this That would be your 4th game changer, and Upgraded allows 3.
→ It moves what your cards permit from Upgraded to Optimized or cEDH.
Combo changes Completes Underworld Breach + Brain Freeze.
Two lines, and the count is line one — a limit with a number is the only thing here a player can act on, and delta.ts routes a Game Changer through counts even when it moves precisely because the count is the sentence. The combo evidence is a third line, not a clause: it is what the count cannot explain, so it is marked as a different kind of statement (--fact) rather than folded into the same breath.
the same shape when it does not move — one clause
If you add this That would be your 2nd game changer, and Upgraded allows 3.
No marks, because nothing moved — and no your bracket stays at Upgraded either, which would be the reassurance 1b refuses. One clause, one fact, done. The test in code is to.join() !== from.join(), which the client must compute against the profile it already has: counts carries to and no from.
rejected — one line, both clauses Your 4th game changer of 3 allowed moves you from Upgraded to Optimized or cEDH. Four figures and two claims in one breath, and the phrase 4 of 3 prints a fraction whose denominator a reader will try to satisfy by ticking something off. Two sentences cost one line and read at a glance.
1d The combo that moves nothing — the sentence this round owed §6 · the hole

Silence is wrong here, and the reason is not politeness — it is that the player is about to be surprised by their own deck. A pod asks what is in it; the answer includes a two-card infinite the app watched them assemble without a word. So the shape speaks. What it may not do is dress the fact as a consequence: nothing moved, so it wears no mark and it never says this pushes you.

the sentence — a deck already at Optimized or cEDH
If you add this
A combo, and no change This completes Heliod, Sun-Crowned + Walking Ballista, a two-card infinite. It doesn’t change your bracket — this deck already permits Optimized or cEDH, and there is no lower bracket left for it to rule out.
Two clauses: the event, then why the verdict is unmoved. The second clause is load-bearing — without it the first reads as an accusation with no consequence attached, which is the tone the whole surface is built to avoid.
the other entrance — a deck at Upgraded that already holds one
If you add this
A combo, and no change This completes Heliod, Sun-Crowned + Walking Ballista, a two-card infinite. It doesn’t change your bracket — nothing in this deck searches for either half, so Upgraded still permits it as a late-game finish.
Two reasons, two clauses, one shape. Which second clause prints is decidable from data the client has: already at the top when to is [4,5], nothing searches for it when the deck sits at 3 and coverage is absent. Both are quotable from the rules and neither is a judgement.
the ask — a sixth shape, not a field
| { kind: "inert";
    fact: "combo";
    combos: ComboRef[];
    to: Bracket[] } Why not none with a completes array. none is the shape a caller renders nothing for, and every consumer that ever ships will branch on kind before it inspects a field. A speaking none is a shape that reads alike and means differently — which is the exact thing delta.ts’s own header says the union exists to prevent. Precedence: after ruleless, before none. A tutor that also completes a combo keeps its tutor sentence and gains nothing — one atom, one sentence, and the combo is in the deck’s findings either way.
This is the round’s only data-shape change, and it is one branch in a pure function with a test file that already records the case as unreachable-by-construction. to is carried so the client can pick the second clause without re-deriving the slot.
1e The upgrade path — under the rung that already asks the question §3.2 · §4.2

Round 2’s climb already draws a lower rung captioned what would take you down, with one sentence of reason on it. The upgrade path is that rung’s full answer, so it opens from it rather than becoming a fifth block on the tab — and the lower rungs become the target selector, which means no new control invents a target the rules do not offer (there is no upgrade to 4 or 5, and the chips only ever show brackets below this deck’s reading).

It reads; it does not act. No tick boxes — a checklist claims the app is tracking progress toward a target the player never set, and it would make the overlap between two items (the same two cards appear twice) into a broken counter. No remove control — it would be the only write on a surface built to report, and the removal belongs on the decklist where the card and its other four hundred consequences live. Card names carry the dotted underline that opens a card everywhere else, which is the one affordance this block needs and already has.
the grammar, and the one place it bends Cardinality picks the phrasing, and the app never picks a card. candidates > removeCount reads any 2 of these 5; candidates === removeCount names them and drops the any, because any 1 of these 1 is a choice dressed on a certainty. Naming a forced card is not the app choosing — the rules chose. The combo item never says remove a piece. Either half breaks it states the requirement at the combo’s own cardinality; an item listing two cards under remove any 1 of these 2 reads as if the pair were interchangeable with the count rules above it. The overlap is stated once, in the foot. upgradePath lists a card under every rule it violates — correct, and confusing on a first read. One sentence naming the overlap costs less than de-duplicating the items, which would hide a rule.
rejected A checklist with persisted ticks. Storage, a progress figure, and an implied goal the app assigned itself. The path is derived; ticking it would make it state. Rows that remove. The first write on the analysis tab, on a surface with no undo, next to five candidate names the player has not finished reading. A separate block below the findings. It would restate the climb’s lower rung, and on a deck at bracket 2 it would sit there with nothing to say. A target dropdown 1–5. There is no upgrade to 4 or 5, and offering one would ask the player to aim at a bracket the function refuses to compute.
1f Blocked, and already satisfied §4.3
blocked — the excess sits on a commander
Target Core — not reachable by removing cards
Game changers Core allows none, and you have 3. Two of them can come out. Mystic Remora Cyclonic Rift Thrasios, Triton Hero your commander
The third is your commander, and a commander can’t be cut. Removing the other two still leaves one game changer in the command zone, so Core isn’t reachable by removing cards — it would take a different commander. Upgraded is reachable — two cards would have to go.
Three things keep this from reading as a scold or a dead end. The candidates that can come out are still listed, so the block is still useful. The commander is drawn in the list on a dashed edge rather than omitted — upgradePath excludes it from candidates, and showing why the count is short beats a sentence about an invisible card. And the nearest reachable target is named and one tap away: the function is callable per target, so the exit is derived and not a consolation. No --danger, no warning glyph, no impossible. It would take a different commander is the plainest true statement available, and it is not advice.
satisfied — items is empty
Nothing would have to go. Your cards already sit inside Core — whether this deck is Exhibition or Core is yours to say.
Reachable on a deck whose reading is a range. A deck permitting [1,2] can ask for the path to Core and get an empty one, so the state is drawn rather than assumed away — and the second clause hands the reader back to the declaration, which is the only thing left that can answer it.
two sentences nobody may write “Remove Cyclonic Rift to reach Upgraded.” One of five equally valid answers, dressed as the answer. The locked ruling in upgrade.ts’s own header: determined in cardinality, undetermined in identity. “You’re 2 cards away from Upgraded.” A distance implies a direction the player agreed to travel. The path answers a question the player asked by tapping a rung; it never volunteers a gap.

Three placements tried and dropped
A preview block on the analysis tab. One sentence about whichever candidate is “current” — and nothing on that tab has a current candidate. It needed a selection model the tab does not have, and it put a claim about a card the deck lacks inside the block whose authority is that it describes the cards the deck has.
A confirm step on the Add. The honest version of a preview — and it taxes every add in the app to warn about the one in forty that moves a bracket. The product has no confirm dialogs, and a player topping off does not want twenty-seven of them.
A running total on the top-off commit bar. Attractive — twenty-seven cards is where a bracket really moves — and uncomputable: bracketDelta takes one candidate against a fixed profile, and a batch preview needs a profile that changes as the batch lands. Named in READINESS as the one thing to want next.
