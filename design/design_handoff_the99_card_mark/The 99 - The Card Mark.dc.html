Three solid glyphs, bare, in one shared ink — and the key already on the page says what they mean.

Adopted: 3d. Bolt (Game Changer) · peak stripped (mass land denial) · turn again (extra turns), drawn as mass rather than outline, with no container, in one theme-independent ink --fact #8FA6BE, lit by a static 45% drop-shadow glow. 16px in the modal, 14px on rows, 12px on the tile; 12px is the floor and below it the mark is not drawn at all.

The ink says a bracket fact is present; the glyph says which one — so colour is never the only carrier, and one shared token replaces the three-hue set Round 0 ruled unsafe across six themes. Ordering is copies first, mark last on every surface: the row reads Armageddon ×2 ⛰, and the tile’s meta line reads short 1 ⛰ with the count moved down beside the name. Each mark also carries a one-sentence hover tooltip on pointer-fine devices — an enhancement, never the gloss, since it does not exist on a phone.

The written handoff is final and rebuilt around 3d: README.md (the four calls and what each rejects), STATES-card-mark.md (six state matrices), CONTRAST (§3’s measurement — weakest 6.7:1, plus the distinctness pass), copy strings, READINESS.md (one data dependency).

Turns 2 and 1 below are superseded history, kept for the audit trail: Turn 2 explored the nature register and the three ink treatments, Turn 1 the three symbol groupings (letters / signs / an ink each) and the word-based direction they replaced. Read them for why, not for what ships.

the adopted mark, in one paragraph
No container, and that is semantic rather than stylistic. Every other mark on these surfaces — $, P, short 1, ×N — sits in the shipped slot box, so boxed means “about your copies” and bare means “about the card” · a glyph is ~14px, so nothing yields anywhere: the tile keeps SET · rarity, the availability chip never outranks the mark, no row grows and no meta line is touched · three key entries, the words quoted verbatim: Game Changer (a proper noun), mass land denial, extra turns · each mark carries its own word as its accessible name, so a screen reader never depends on the key · absence is drawn as nothing, on every surface, at every size.

Why it works. Colour is the fastest channel there is, and here it is genuinely a second carrier: a reader scanning a decklist sees how many of which kind without resolving a single glyph. The forms still work alone, so nothing depends on it.

What a pick unblocks. The mark is ~16px in every set, so every yield rule the word version needed disappears — the tile keeps its rarity, the chip no longer outranks anything, no row grows, and a card carrying two facts draws two marks.
