Add panel overhaul · #158 · R1

Retrieval is a bar. Discovery is a room.

The 340px rail asked one column to be both. It is neither: search is fast and needs no pictures, suggestions are slow and need all of them. So the rail is retired and the two jobs get the shapes they want. Quick add becomes a bar pinned to the deck at both widths — type, ↵, done, no view change. Consider becomes a full-width peer view of the decklist — art-led tiles grouped by the coverage the deck is short of, one tap from the deck and never buried under it.

Every state leads with the phone; desktop sits beside it. Both widths use the same two constructs — no mobile-only sheet, no desktop-only rail.

1 · One surface or two
Two. The tab bar goes. Search is always-on chrome; Consider is a view.
2 · Where discovery lives
Main column, full width, as a peer tab to the decklist. The rail is deleted; so is the sheet.
3 · Tile hierarchy
Art → name+cost → type → evidence → Add. Role is the shelf, never repeated on the tile.
4 · Detail invitation
The art and name open the modal; Add is a separate target. The shelf is the sibling list, so Prev/Next pages it.
5 · Owned filter
Belongs to search only — suggestions are already owned-only. Drawn in two positions; A recommended, default Owned.
6 · Replaces the reason chip
EDHREC inclusion % as bar + figure. Role picks quote the coverage gap instead. Lift requested, not assumed.
7 · Role "other"
→ Threats & synergy. The five roles are coverage, not a taxonomy; this is the gameplan, not the residue.
Staleness
An accepted tile stays put, marked ✓ In deck. The shelf count and coverage cell tick instead of the grid reflowing.

The call: the CTA dies
“Open suggestions” existed to open the sheet. The sheet is gone, and the jump it performed is now a tab sitting 40px above the mark with the count already on it — Consider 99. A brass primary button pointing at a visible tab teaches that the tab is not the way, which is exactly wrong on the one screen where a new builder is learning the surface.
It is also no longer the only door. The quick-add bar is present and focusable at the bottom of this screen — the empty builder now has two live entrances where it used to have one button. Adding a third control that duplicates one of them is the rail’s crowding problem in a new place.
The body copy carries the direction instead. It names both doors in the order they sit on screen — type below, or take the ninety-nine — so the instruction survives the button’s removal rather than being lost with it. Kitchen Table: the joke is untouched.
The role hint is promoted to both widths. It was desktop-only because mobile spent that vertical budget on the CTA. That budget is now free, and the hint is the only line telling a first-time builder what to fill first — the thing a new player actually needs on a 0/99 screen.

One caption, not two
The desktop caption breaks too. captionFor says suggestions are “primed on the right” — verbatim from the richness mock, and true only of the 340px column R1 retired. There is no right column now. That string was not on the addendum list, and it is the same class of bug as the CTA: copy naming a construct that no longer exists.
So captionFor and captionForMobile collapse into one string. The breakpoint split existed only because the two widths pointed at different constructs; they now point at the same two. This also closes the “flagged for Design ratification” note on captionForMobile — by deleting it rather than ratifying it.

quick add · pinned
Quick add is chrome — pinned to the bottom of the deck column at every width, live in both views, never a mode. Consider is a view — full width, art-led, grouped by coverage. On phone the identical pair: the sticky + Add cards footer becomes the quick-add bar, and Consider is the same tab.
Why not both in one panel
A tab bar makes two unlike things trade places. Every quick add would cost a tab switch back, and every browse would sit behind a control the player must first notice. Archidekt ships a pinned text-only quick-add and a separate visual search; Moxfield splits inline autocomplete from an advanced page. Two speeds, two shapes, no toggle between them.
Consider is a tab, not a section below the decklist. Spotify's append-below pattern works on a 30-row playlist; below 99 rows it is a scroll no one takes. A peer tab is one tap at any deck length.

Reading the shelves
Order is the argument. Shelves sort by how far under target the deck is, so the first thing on screen is the thing the deck most lacks. The header quotes the same numbers as the coverage cell above it — 4 of 10 in two places, never two different framings of one fact.
The strip becomes navigation. A cell under target is a link to its shelf. That is the whole fix for finding 1 and the strip's isolation at once: the thing that names the gap now reaches the cards that fill it.
Shelves cap at 12 with Show all; a 99-tile wall is the rail's problem in a wider frame. Sort also offers EDHREC first and Cheapest first.
Two honest omissions, drawn. A card with no imageUri gets the flat plate and says so; a card EDHREC has no figure for prints no EDHREC figure rather than a 0% bar, which would read as a verdict.

Two different absences
Empty gets the wink. Zero suggestions on a 99-card deck is a win, not a failure — an expressive moment, so it takes the voice. The CTA points at the collection because that is the only thing that changes the answer.
Degraded stays straight. Same reasoning as the Commanders counter-example in the richness pass: a service outage is not a place to be charming. It says what is missing, what the ranking fell back to, and offers Retry — reusing the shipped DegradedBanner shape rather than a new one.
Degraded tiles drop the bar, not the row. Where a percentage would sit, the coverage reason sits instead. An empty track would read as 0%.
The caption is no longer a permanent sub-header under a tab. Non-degraded, the provenance line lives once in the toolbar as 99 cards you already own; the EDHREC attribution moves to the shelf that uses it.

Why the bar has thumbnails after all
Archidekt's quick-add loads no images on purpose. Ours loads a 30×42 one because Magic has a thousand cards whose names differ by a word, and a 30px crop settles which Study in less time than reading the type line. It is one column added to searchCards's select, at four rows on screen — not a grid of art.
↵ adds the top match. That is the whole retrieval promise — type three letters, hit return, keep typing. The active row carries the brass ring and the ↵ add hint so the shortcut is stated, not assumed.
No overlay, no dim. The results panel rises off the bar and the deck stays lit behind it — you are watching the deck grow. Esc dismisses results and keeps the query; a second Esc clears it.
No-results offers the one honest escape — drop the owned filter, not "check your spelling". The premise is building from what you own, so the most likely truth is you don't own it.

Drawn so the tradeoff is on the table; A is the call.
Default: on
The product premise is building from what you own, and the deck already carries a shopping list for the rest. Today it ships useState(false) — off. This is a behaviour change, and it is the one item here that touches shipped defaults.
Consider has no such filter — and should not. suggestDeck() iterates ownedPool only, so every suggestion is already owned. A toggle there would be a control with one setting. The toolbar says 99 cards you already own instead, which is the same information without the lie.
Finding 4 therefore resolves as: the filter exists, it was buried inside a tab nobody opened. See the gap report for the "suggest cards you don't own yet" question, which is a different feature.

Scope of the rename
One string in grouping.ts:65 plus the shelf header. The role value stays "other" — no migration, no classification work, and the label is free to change again.

H · Accessibility, motion, tokens
Roles & keyboard
div[role=tablist]
  button[role=tab][aria-selected]
section[role=tabpanel][aria-label="Cards to consider"]
  section[aria-labelledby=shelf-removal]
    ul > li > (button.art + button.name + button.add)

input[role=combobox][aria-expanded][aria-controls=qa-results]
  [aria-activedescendant]
ul#qa-results[role=listbox] > li[role=option]
Quick add: focus never leaves the input. ↑↓ move aria-activedescendant, ↵ adds the active row, Esc dismisses results then clears. Not a dialog, not modal, no trap.
Consider: Tab walks tiles in DOM order — art, name, Add, next tile. Shelves are landmarks, so a screen reader can skip a whole shelf.
Adds announce on the builder’s existing live region: Sol Ring added · 48 of 99 · removal 5 of 10. One region, so five fast adds read in order.
Tap targets: mobile Add 44px, result rows 56px, quick-add field 16px text so iOS doesn’t zoom.
Focus is the global :focus-visible — 2px var(--brass) at +2px. Not overridden anywhere in this flow.
Contrast & motion
Tile name #EDE6D8 on #1E1A15 ≈ 12.4:1. Type line #8E8269 ≈ 4.6:1 — AA, and supplementary to a name that already carries the meaning.
The inclusion figure is #C6B79A ≈ 8.1:1; the bar is decoration for it, never the only carrier. Colour is never the sole signal — ownership is a dot and a word in the row, art state is greyscale and a ✓ label.
“In deck” tiles sit at opacity .5 on the art only; the name lifts to #A89B84 rather than dimming, so the row stays readable.
One new animation: the quick-add results panel, .anim-quickadd-in — 140ms ease-out, opacity + 6px rise, joining the explicit list in the prefers-reduced-motion block. Reduced motion: appears in place, opaque. Dismissal is instant in both modes.
Tiles do not animate on add. A grid of 12 cards easing at once is a light show, and the optimistic flip has to be instant to be believed.
Every hex on this canvas is a Palette A value shown for theme-independent reference; production reads var(--*) from globals.css. No new token is introduced. No new dependency: the grid is CSS, the combobox is useState plus key handling, and 99 tiles need no virtualisation.
