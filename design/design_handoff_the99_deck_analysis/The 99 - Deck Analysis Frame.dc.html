§6 — locked by the spec, drawn as written
A verdict is sometimes a range. Brackets 1 and 5 are declared, never inferred, so permits 1 or 2 is a finished answer — same slot, same rank, same ink as a single number, and the chip carries it out to the decks list unrounded · every figure carries its denominator in its own cell, never a tooltip and never a footnote · nothing is blended into a score · both widths ship together.

Drawing bracket 2 turned up something worth knowing: the cards can never produce it. Brackets 1 and 2 have identical card limits — no game changers, no mass land denial, no chaining, no two-card combos — so no count can ever distinguish them. The difference is intent, which is why the verdict reads 1 or 2 and never 2. The same is true at the top: 4 and 5 share their limits too, so the cards say 4 or 5 and the player says which. Both charts above therefore show two kinds of case — a floor the cards set, and a bracket the player declared — and 2 and 5 only ever appear as the second kind.
My pick is now 3d. It keeps the thing 3b was for — the silhouette, visible before any word is read — while spending no width on empty columns and stating no barometer twice. It is also the only one of the four whose shape is unchanged at 390px. The one thing 3a still does better is spelling out, on every rung, exactly what is standing in the way; if that turns out to matter more than compactness, 3a is the fallback and the cost is height plus a repeated line.

Color board · six palettes × six themes, on the real deck tile
Your four reactions share one principle: the mark should get hotter as the bracket climbs, and 5 should be the brightest thing there. That is the note in all four — 2a is liked because 5 burns, 2c and 2d are faulted because 4 and 5 fade out, 2b is faulted because nothing escalates at all. So escalation is the requirement, and the palette is just how it gets delivered. None of the four was designed to that; they were designed to be distinguishable, which is a weaker goal.
A rule of the mark, not of any one palette: every rung owes 4.5:1. The numeral sits inside the diamond in the bracket's own color, so it is text on the tile ground — in every palette, at every rank. Measured against all twelve real grounds (--surface-inset and --surface-sunken × six themes), three of the six fail, and the badges below carry the numbers. This is the measurable version of your own note. “4 and 5 get lost” in 2c and 2d is not a matter of taste — those ranks are literally under the legibility floor, at 1.68:1 and 2.82:1. A ramp that darkens as it climbs cannot be fixed by nudging, because the direction it travels is the direction the floor forbids. The whole descending family is out, which retires 2c and 2d together. And 2a — the one you liked most — fails too, at rank 5 (3.27:1) and at rank 3 on the accent (3.95:1); 2f is exactly that palette with both faults repaired.
One constraint survives from the last round and still binds. The six accents are #CF8745 brass, #FF4D9A magenta, #A3C445 yellow-green, #52827A teal, #AE88E6 violet and #5C86C9 blue, so a bracket ramp must be one constant set rather than six tuned ones — the precedent is in the token block, where --w --u --b --r --g hold identical hexes in every theme because mana colors are the format's vocabulary, not ours. Brackets are the same kind of thing. That is the one real fault in 2a, and the only thing 2f repairs.
And here is why escalation kept failing. The hot end of the wheel is already spoken for: --danger owns red-orange, --warning owns amber, and Low Sun's accent owns brass. A five-hue ramp that ends hot therefore lands on a semantic or on an accent — which is exactly why I steered to 2b, and why 2b came out inert. The way out is to escalate on brightness inside one family instead of moving around the wheel. Rank 5 can be the brightest mark on the page without being red.
So: two new palettes, and no fifth direction. 2e Ignition is that idea — warm ash, warming, brass, bright gold, white heat, one family, brightness doing the work. 2f is the minimum repair of the one you actually liked: 2a with rank 3 pinned off --accent so it stops re-theming, and 5 brightened. All five clear 4.5:1 on all twelve grounds — inset and sunken, six themes.
Which changes where the escalation comes from, and improves the idea. The bottom of the scale is floored by contrast, so quietness at rank 1 has to be bought with chroma rather than darkness — near-neutral ash, legible but colorless. All the escalation therefore happens at the top, where there is headroom: rank 5 is 3.65× the luminance of rank 1 and carries almost all the chroma. That is the read you asked for, and it is now the only place the ramp can put it.
Why 2f was close, and what taking 2e gave up: it is the palette you responded to, it now clears the floor at every rank, and it keeps five distinguishable hues where 2e has one family — which genuinely is more interesting at a glance. The case against is the company it keeps: 2f is honest but carries real costs I would not hide — rank 5 sits close to --danger, and ranks 3 and 4 sit on Low Sun's brass. On a tile whose detail row can print 4 over in --danger, a cEDH mark that looks like a warning is the one confusion worth avoiding. 2e gets you the same escalation without spending the danger color to do it.

A2 · the bracket mark — one diamond, ornamented by rank
The numbers and the names are Wizards', and we say them verbatim. WotC and the Commander Format Panel published the five brackets in February 2025 so a deck's level reads the same in a playgroup, a tournament and an app. Every app showing those five words is quoting the same source we are.
The mark is a single diamond carrying the numeral, and it gets more ornate as the bracket climbs. The five-rung crown was honest and too wide — a chip has to survive a deck tile, a filter row and a phone band. One diamond is a fifth of the width and says the same thing, because the elaboration carries the rank where the count used to: the open diamond alone at Exhibition, the full filigree at cEDH. The diamond itself is the logo's, and the accretion is how the logo already works — marks.tsx builds its crown out of three of them.

The numeral sets the floor, and the floor is 20px. A numeral has to fit the inscribed square of a rotated diamond — about 70% of the core — so the mark cannot hold a readable digit below roughly 20px however the ratios are tuned. So we do not draw one. Under 20px the bracket is the numeral alone in its own color (drawn leftmost above): no ornament, nothing to squint at, and the digit still carries the rank.
