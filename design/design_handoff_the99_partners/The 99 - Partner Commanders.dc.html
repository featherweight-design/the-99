Two commanders, and no surface draws two portraits

Four surfaces from spec §6, and nothing else. The §4 ceiling is assumed throughout: a pair deck is 98, and every count drawn reads against 98.

R2 · what #473 changed under R1
R1's decisions 16 and 17 shingled a thumb that no longer exists. builder-spine.tsx is now spine-desktop.tsx (left-portrait, band={168}, type at a 254px inset) and spine-mobile.tsx (cover, 2.2:1). 16 is withdrawn and replaced by 22; 17 narrows to the picker, the one surface that still has thumbs. 18's caption work survives and is re-anchored to the new insets. 20 and 21 are untouched. 23 is new, from the resume count's second form.
The ordering constraint — still nothing to flag, and now trivially so
R1 carried order three ways: reading order, z-order and size. Two of those were properties of the shingle, and the shingle is gone — so R2 carries order one way, in words, and a sentence cannot be symmetric. {primary} + {partner} states the order at every width, in the accessible name, and in the one place the pair is ever drawn as art — which is nowhere.
1 · 22 · The header carries no portrait-level pair signal — the argued case
The band is the deck's art, not its roster
§6 asked for two portraits in the spine. The ruling is one, and the reason is the same one that closed 21: ArtWash's portrait width is band × 1.37, and at band={168} that is 230px, with the mask end at 58%, the four scrim stops at 0 / 134 / 230 / 240px and the type inset at 254 — all in px, all keyed to that one number. If a diptych was a third strategy on a 72px band, it is a third strategy on a 168px one. The argument did not change scale; it got stronger, because the desktop band also carries a vertical scrim keyed to where type sits over the art.
And the header now says what the thumb used to: spine-desktop.tsx's own first line is the commander's art is the band now. A band is a single field of one image. The pair is stated in the caption, in full, and in the / 98 — the same two carriers the desktop rail uses, on a surface where the caption has 644px instead of 200.
What the partner gets instead: its printing
Declining the portrait leaves one real gap, and it is functional rather than decorative. FullCardTrigger opens the commander's printing from the band; a pair has two printings and one band. So the partner's name in the caption is its trigger — the band stays the primary's, and the second card opens from the word that names it.
That keeps the affordance where the information already is, costs no geometry, and is the app's own vocabulary: the removed thumb was a card trigger too. It is drawn below as an underline on the partner's name only — not the primary's, whose trigger is the 230px of art beside it.

18 · Re-anchored: the label leaves on mobile only, and the row wraps rather than truncates
The mobile caption is no longer beside a thumb — it is centred on the wash inside left:18 right:18, so it has 354px, and metaRow is flex-wrap. Two names at 10px mono are ~336px: they fit on their own line, and the pips and pill wrap below them. Add · Jeskai and it is ~390 > 354, so the label still leaves the mobile line — 18's conclusion survives, on new arithmetic, and the pips it leaves behind are in the same row rather than a row below.
The wrap costs ~22px inside a 177px band whose caption block currently ends ~90px from the top, so nothing is pushed off the art and the 2.2:1 ratio is untouched. Single-commander captions are unchanged at both widths.
The ordered shrink still applies — between 768 and ~1085px
At 1180 the desktop caption has room to spare, but at 768 the type column is only 232px against the pair's ~370. flex-wrap spills the pips and pill to their own lines first; past that the names must give. So: the group is overflow-hidden, both halves are min-w-0 with an ellipsis, and the partner carries a large flex-shrink so it gives first.
Do not write it as a flex-none primary, which is the obvious reading of “only the partner shrinks” and is a bug: a non-shrinking span past its own width overflows the group and draws under whatever follows. This document shipped that version for one round and the primary's last glyphs sat 4px beneath the count.
The joiner is +, and it now shares a line with ·
R1 chose + partly because · is this app's fact separator. On the desktop caption the two are now three words apart — Ishai + Bruse Tarl · Jeskai — which is the strongest possible test of that choice and it passes: + joins two things of the same kind, · separates two kinds. Had they been the same glyph the line would read as three commanders.

20 · Absent, never disabled
§6 lists the first state as “disabled”. Ruling: it is absent, and the brief is right to have re-scoped it. Nearly every commander in the game has no partner ability, so a disabled second slot would be permanent dead furniture on the app's most-travelled create path, advertising a mechanic the chosen card does not have and inviting the one question the surface cannot answer. The slot appears the moment partnerAbility(oracleText) returns non-null for the primary, and never before — which also means it is absent before the first pick, with no separate state to draw.
Dashed means not settled
The empty plate and the import proposal share one border style, and it is the one the app already uses for an unsettled claim: --border-dashed on soft claims, and Building's dashed pill. A dash is a line style, not an alpha fade, so it survives the art-wash readability retirement and reads in greyscale. The proposal is not accent-marked: refresh decision 3 reserves accent for in use and to buy, and a proposal is neither.
A pre-selected pair the player did not choose must be reversible in one tap and legible as a guess, which is what the dash and the word Proposed buy together. Spec §3: a proposal, not a decision.

The rail's pair signal is / 98, and it costs nothing
§6 asks for a ruling because the rail is where a pair deck is least distinguishable. It is — the art spine is 26px wide, and half of that is 12px of card frame with no face in it. So the image stays primary-only, and the pair is stated by the denominator the §4 ceiling already changes: 98 appears nowhere else in the app, needs no new node, and sits on the line above the two rows drawn here for comparison.
Desktop rail rows carry no pair signal, and that is accepted. That row prints the deck name and the status word — no art, no count, no commander — so any pair mark there would be the only commander fact on a row that has deliberately never had one. The rail is a directory; the deck page is one tap away.
Rejected · the diptych, drawn at all three real sizes
rail · 2 × 13px
resume · 2 × 49px
header · 2 × 115px
The argument is structural rather than aesthetic, and it is the same one at every size. ArtWash's portrait width is band × 1.37 — 99px at band 72, 230px at band 168 — and the mask end, the four horizontal scrim stops and the type inset are all keyed to that number in px. Halving it does not change a parameter; it invalidates every stop, and the art-wash handoff's own regression note is that a fade not keyed to the portrait shows as a hard seam. A diptych is a third strategy, not an option, and that document closed at four surfaces, two strategies, one <ArtWash>. The crop is also 0.68:1 halved, against art_crop's 1.37:1 — half the composition gone, which is the exact failure that moved the band to 2.2:1 in the first place.

23 · new — the count has two forms, and the long one does not fit any name at all
The span R1 pinned has a second form: 86 / 98 · 3 to buy when the roll-up has an entry for the deck. Measured, that is ~113px against the short form's 44, and the arithmetic is brutal: 62 pill + 28 gaps + 113 = 203 > 200. The long form leaves negative room for a name — not a short name, not a single name, no name.
So the rule is about what may be cut, not about what shrinks first: a figure is never shown partially truncated. A clipped number reads as a different number — 86 / 9… is a lie in a way that Bruse Ta… is not. The count splits into a pinned figure and a droppable clause.
Pair decks never render the clause — deterministic, no measurement, one boolean. That is not a new state: the component's own comment says a deck with no roll-up entry draws the count alone, so count-alone already ships. Single decks keep the clause, and it becomes the only shrinking child there, so what gives is the clause's words and never the figure. This also fixes a latent bug: today the count is the shrinking child, so a single deck with a long name already renders 86 / 98 · 3 t….
The clause is the right thing to lose because it is the only item on the line that is printed elsewhere: the deck tile and the rail both carry to-buy, and Shopping owns it outright. The pair is printed nowhere else on Home.

19 · Full names where the deck is opened, short names where the line is shared
The short form is the substring before the first comma — the identifying half of a legend's name, and the half a player says out loud. shortCommanderName is exported and pure at decks-builder.ts:17, so applying it to both halves needs nothing new. A name with no comma is already short and returns whole.
Shortening is pair-only. A single commander keeps its full deck.commanderName exactly as shipped — the title is what disambiguates one name, and the pair is what disambiguates two — so no existing row changes.
The partner is the only shrinking half
Two spans, not one string, and the mechanism is an ordered shrink: the group is overflow-hidden, both halves are min-w-0 with an ellipsis, and + Bruse Tarl takes a large flex-shrink so it gives first — Ishai yields only once the partner is gone. Because the joiner leads the shrinking span, the + can never be the character that is lost.
The accessible name carries what the pixels drop: the row's sr-only text names both commanders in full, primary first, and states the to-buy clause the pair row no longer prints. Truncation is a layout compromise; it is never an information one.
