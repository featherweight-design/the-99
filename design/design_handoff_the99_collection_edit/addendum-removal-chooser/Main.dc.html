Pick which deck goes short

Hugh lowered Counterspell by one, the warning named Mutant Menace, and tapping it left the installed app. In R1 the warning's deck rows stop being links, and when more than one deck lists the card, the rows become the choice.

1. The rows are the controlNo new button. Each deck that lists the card is a row with a mark, as the contested-copy chooser does it. The marked row is the one that goes short, because that's the question you came with. One copy: round marks. More than one: square marks.
2. The default is marked before you touch itThe deck that committed the card last goes short (today's seniority rule). Its mark is neutral. A mark you set is accent, and the footer says what you picked, with Back to first committed. Same four signals as the contested chooser.
3. No links out, and no tap to learn why (#2982)The deck name is plain text in all seven places the warning shows. What you'd open the deck to learn is on the row: how many it lists and when it committed the card. Rows with several cards (Sync, Replace, Revert) still open onto their cards, as shipped.
4. What a pick writesCopies go to the decks that keep the card, in the removal's own transaction. No list or version changes. The deck you picked shows the card short and puts it on its shopping list. Undo puts the copies back where they were.
5. Two copies, three decks (P3)You pick as many decks as copies are going. When that many are already picked, tapping another tells you to untick one first. Nothing is locked, and the reason is said out loud.
6. Only on removal, only with a real choiceThe chooser shows on Edit copies' removal and lowering when two or more decks list the card. One deck (P4), or Sync, Replace, Revert, Undo and I got it: today's warning, minus the link.
