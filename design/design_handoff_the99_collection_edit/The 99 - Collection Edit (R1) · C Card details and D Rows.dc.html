Art is a grid of tiles you pick from. Copies are a ledger of rows you count. They never share a screen.

Design's call — two places, and a mode between them. The shipped PrintingRegion stays exactly what it is: a radiogroup of tiles that sets the art, which saves on pick and changes no quantity. Ownership gets a new block, Your copies: one row per holding (printing × finish), each with a stepper, behind an explicit Edit copies on the owned line. While it's open the printing region collapses to its trigger and is inert. Close it with Save or Cancel and the region comes back. So the two meanings differ on four axes at once: shape (tiles vs rows), control (radio vs stepper), commit (on pick vs on Save), and presence (never open together). One list with two controls per tile would put a stepper on a tile that's also a radio, and a mis-tap on the art would cost a copy.

Raising saves directly; lowering goes through §E. Edits are staged in the block and written by one Save, which makes one history entry, so a move (one printing down, another up) is one entry by construction. A save that only raises writes with no check. Any lowering puts Checking decks… on the button first. The owned line's marker never moves. The staged total appears beside ×5, and the marker re-reads only after the save lands.
