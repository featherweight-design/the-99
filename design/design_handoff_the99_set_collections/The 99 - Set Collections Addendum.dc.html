One number sizes every cell: 146.

Findings 1 and 2 are one ruling, as the brief suspected: the grid becomes a real CSS grid of 1fr tracks with a minimum, so the row distributes by the cells growing — one mechanism, not two. R2 raises the minimum to 146px and drops the per-width exception: 146 is image_uri_small's own width, so a cell is never narrower than the art inside it — one constant, one declaration, every width. The name search takes the chassis's field slot at the head of both rows (§3), and the extras figure leaves the index row's code line for a second line under the base ratio (§4).

The constraint, held in three places
Owned and missing keep identical geometry. Stronger than before, not weaker: after this round the cell carries no width literal at all — the track sets the width and aspect-ratio: 63/88 sets the height, so the two states cannot differ by construction. Border style and art opacity remain the only difference.
The grid must not reflow when a filter flips. One engineering line decides it: auto-fill, never auto-fit. auto-fit collapses empty tracks, so a filter leaving three cells would stretch those three across the whole column — the exact reflow the constraint forbids, one keyword away.

§1 · findings 1 + 2 — one ruling, and now one constant
The two questions are one question. A fixed-width child in a wrapping row and a 1fr track in a grid differ in exactly this: the grid gives the leftover width to the cells instead of to the right margin. So “does the row distribute” and “do the cells grow” are the same sentence read from either end — and the answer to both is grid-template-columns: repeat(auto-fill, minmax(146px, 1fr)). The minimum is the whole design — it sets the column count, and the column count sets the size. R1 shipped two minima, 116 desktop and 160 phone; R2 has one. Desktop at 116 was still too small, and the moment the minimum rises to 146 the phone exception stops being needed: 146 puts two cells on a 390px screen by itself.

Setting the minimum there means a cell is never narrower than the art it holds, so nothing on the page is ever downscaling its source pointlessly, and the ceiling is structural rather than declared: a track cannot reach 2F + gap = 302px, because at that width another column fits instead. Three alternatives drawn and rejected
Keep 116 and give the remainder to the gaps (justify-content: space-between). Flushes the edge and answers nothing about size. Rejected twice over: the column gap becomes 24.6px against an 18px row gap, so the grid stops reading as a grid — and a wrapped final row of three cells spaces those three across the whole width, the classic last-row defect, on the one row every reader reaches.
Centre the fixed grid. Halves the dead space and costs more than it buys: the grid's left edge is load-bearing. The sticky ratio, the cost strip, the section headings, the result line and every cell share one left margin, and centring puts the cells 51px inside it — a misalignment on the page's own spine, for symmetry nobody asked for.
F = 160 everywhere, the next step up. Six tracks of 175px at 1180 — 21% more art than 146 and a 21,500px desktop page, against 16,500 at 146. Rejected on the scroll, not on the size: six columns is a two-and-a-half-screen base run, and 175px is past the point where the art gets better, because the source runs out at 146.

§2 · finding 3 — the rack conforms to the chassis, both widths
The brief asked for Clear to conform. Read against design_handoff_the99_filter_chassis R2 the answer is that Clear is the third of four things on this rack that do not conform, and fixing it alone would leave a chip line under a control row the chassis does not recognise. So all four land together: the pips become mana symbols (chassis decisions 7 and 8), rarity becomes a trigger with the shared panel (decisions 4 and 19), Clear becomes three scopes (decision 12), and the bar becomes three rows that never change height (decisions 1 and 2). Each is drawn at both widths below.
1 · The pips are art, not palette. Shipped, color-pip.tsx draws a token square at opacity .34–.40 when unlit, which measures 1.38:1. 2 · Rarity is a trigger and a panel. Four values, single-select, with an off state — the chassis's own test makes that a panel dimension, not a segmented run and not four buttons. The copy for it already exists in the shipped file, unused: rarity.all = Rarity · all and rarity.value(r) = Rarity · uncommon. The trigger was always the intent; the letters were the shortcut.
3 · Clear leaves the row. It is the only GhostButton in a row of hairlines, it is inside a sticky head whose height it changes, it does not say which filters, and it appears at one filter where the lit control beside it is the shorter route off. It becomes Clear all filters on the chip line's trailing edge, past one active control.
4 · Three rows, always all three. no filters on prints at rest rather than collapsing, which is what makes the fixed-height promise cheap — and it is worth more here than on any other route, because this bar lives inside a sticky head and every height change under a reader's thumb moves the grid they are reading.

The compact form, and what it must not do
A control never changes type between widths — only its target size, and whether its panel is anchored or a block in the sheet. So the pips are the same circles at 44px and lead the sheet (five circles of full-strength artwork are the anchor at the top and an interruption in the middle), and rarity is a chip group, not a nested list: four short values is exactly what a chip group is for, unlike Set with its 41. Four 44px chips wrap to two rows at 358px, which a sheet may do and the control row may not.

Missing-only stays on the persistent row, and stays out of the sheet — one control, one accessible name, which is the reason MissingOnlyToggle is mounted once today. The badge counts dimensions only: never the search, never missing-only, both of which are visible on the row beside it.

Chip target is 34px, not 44 — the refresh's granted exception on its own test: a chip is a shortcut, never the only route, and every value it removes is also removable at 44px in the sheet or on the row.
Three amendments back to the chassis, from drawing it on this page
1 · Clear all filters must clear the search. The chassis excludes sort and says nothing about the field. The reason it excludes sort is that sort does not narrow. A search does, so a Clear that leaves it on leaves the reader looking at four cells under a control that claimed to have cleared everything. The field's own × stays the scoped route. Applies to all four routes.
2 · There is a fifth control type: the toggle. The chassis names four — field, pip row, segmented, trigger+panel — chosen by the shape of the values. Missing-only's whole value is on or off, so it is not segmented (which is for dimensions with no off state) and a panel for one boolean is absurd. Drawn as the shipped hairline carrying --accent border and label when on, which is the same twice-carried rule as an active trigger.
3 · A trigger's value may be a word the page does not otherwise print. Rarity · uncommon is lowercase because the cell caption below it says 042 · uncommon in the same register — the trigger and the thing it filters read alike, which is why the shipped rarity.value() string is right as written and the sheet's chips are Title Case, because a chip is a control and not a caption.
The pips, drawn honestly — and the one exception to this package's no-literal rule
Each pip above is svgs.scryfall.io/card-symbols/{W,U,B,R,G}.svg, drawn as an image. The canvas stands the glyph in as a mono letter on the symbol's own ground, which is the one place in this package where a colour is a literal rather than a token — deliberately, because by the chassis's ruling these are artwork, not palette: the files open with a filled <circle>, and a single-colour mask would flatten every two-tone hybrid to one hue. They are never tinted, never dimmed and never token-coloured, and the set page's --w … --g tokens keep doing their real job one layer down, inside the cell art. Lit is a 2px --accent ring at 4.42:1 against the sunken well; unlit carries no rim at all, because --border-strong there measures 1.24:1 and could never be the carrier. The symbols themselves floor at 9.51:1.

What it matches, and what it is called
Substring on the printed name, case-insensitive and diacritic-folded (aether finds Æther, jotun finds Jötun), matching either half of a // name. Not the type line, not oracle text, not the collector number — the control is named for the name, and a field that quietly matched rules text would make the result line's count unexplainable.

Placeholder Find a card in this set; accessible name Search cards in Edge of Eternities. The set has to be in the name: this field and the index's Find any set are one tap apart, look identical, and search populations that are opposites.

It reaches nothing. Every cell is already in hand, so this is a presentation change over loaded data: history.replaceState like the other three, 150ms debounce (the shipped card-search window), no request, no router, no dropdown of names, no Enter to submit.

Never in the sheet. A second search field behind Filters would be two controls with one accessible name — the defect MissingOnlyToggle already exists to avoid on this page.
The parameter, and a note on decision 15
The key is q, matching the index's own search, so /collection/sets/eoe?q=plains&m=1 is a linkable page.

R2's decision 15 wrote “no shared key names” with /collection. Read literally that bans q — but the shipped surface already shares c for colour with browser.tsx's owned-params list, and no bug came of it. Narrowed: the rule is that no value carries, and that no one name means two different things. q means “what I typed” on all three routes; it just types into different populations, which is what the route boundary is for. What would still be the bug decision 15 was written about is carrying /collection's q into this route on the By set link. The pill still carries nothing.

The four rules in the move
1 · It takes --fg-muted, not --fg-locked. It is a figure, so it is text and owes 4.5:1; --fg-locked measures 3.16–3.44:1 and is only legitimate on the cell's rarity word, which is a redundant restatement of a filter. --fg-muted floors at 6.53:1.

2 · So the base ratio brightens to --fg-body. With both figures at --fg-muted the only ranking left is 1px of type size. One token up on the primary restores the step and raises the row's most important string at the same time. Complete rows keep --success on the base ratio and leave the extras line muted — a finished base run with 119 extras outstanding must not read as a finished set.

3 · Never 0 / 0 extras. The shipped extrasTotal > 0 condition moves with the line, and an unindexed row has no ratio so it has no second line either — the tag stands alone.

4 · The accessible name has to grow with it. The row is one <Link> carrying an explicit aria-label, which replaces everything its descendants contribute — so a second visible figure that is not in the label is invisible. Exactly the defect set-cell.tsx already fixed for its price tag and its two marks. One clause appended: …and 4 of 123 extras.
Geometry, and the phone
The figure column goes one line to two: 11px mono (15) + 2 + 10px mono (13) = 30px against the name-and-bar block's 24, so the desktop row grows 4px and the phone row does not move at all — it is already pinned to its 44px target floor, with 14px spare. The code line loses ~74px, which is measure the set name gets back on the width where it truncates.

Not drawn, because it is not on offer: a third line, or the extras ratio getting its own completion bar. One set is one figure with one bar; the extras are a second count on the same subject, which is what one type step down means.

Severability, and the one sequencing constraint
§2 is now one PR, not four. The pips, the rarity trigger, the chip line and the three-row bar are one conformance change on one component, and splitting them leaves the page in a state that is neither the old vocabulary nor the new one. Finding 4 lands in that PR or after it, never before — the search is the chassis's field slot, and adding a field to today's row puts it beside a GhostButton at 358px with no width to give it. Findings 1+2 and 5 are independent of both, one file each. Nothing here touches the sticky head's contents, the cost strip, the delta strip, the modal, or any token.

The dependency worth naming: §2 mounts filter-panel.tsx and the chassis's pip component. If the chassis package lands first, this page adopts both and the PR shrinks to a chip line and a trigger. If this page lands first it must not fork either — a forked pip is how six themes of measurement get re-derived by hand, and a forked panel is the component the chassis's own flagged decision is about.
