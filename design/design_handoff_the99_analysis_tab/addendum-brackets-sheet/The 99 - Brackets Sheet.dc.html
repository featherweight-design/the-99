The last surface owed from the verdict round (verdict READINESS item 8). One sheet, opened from the climb's foot, and the only place all four rules and the first three brackets appear together. Built on rules.ts and sources.ts as they stand on main; every figure in the grid is a constant there.

Called · which three 1, 2 and 3 as columns. 4 and 5 as one row under the grid.

They are the only brackets with card limits: verdict.limits covers 1 to 3 and nothing else. A column for 4 and one for 5 would be eight no cap cells saying one thing. They are one row instead, so a deck at 4 still finds itself on the sheet, and the row says the one true thing about them: only the banned list, and the difference is how you play.

Three reasons. The readings are ours: “no cap” at 2 and 3 and the combo question at 3 are how the app applies the rules, and a WotC quote above them would claim Wizards said it. 1c

The full grid, unrolled

A “no cap” cell

Extra turns at Core and Upgraded, and two-card infinites at Upgraded. These are the rules that report a figure and give no verdict (counted: false and earlyGameOnly). Same words as the climb, in --fg-faint on a dashed underline that points to the reading below the row. Every no cap cell has a reading, and a row with none has none: mass land denial is the example.

The tutors row

Set off by a dashed rule, with one cell across all three brackets, so it cannot be read as a column of zeroes. The findings' no limit chip, never no cap. No cap means a rule exists and sets no number. No limit means there is no rule at all. The reading says Wizards removed the rule, and says what tutors still do here: decide whether a combo is found or drawn.

Nothing in a cell weights

No totals, no score, no strength words, no ink that goes up the scale. Bracket inks appear only on the marks and on the reader's own column. 3 allowed is the one figure in --fg at 600, because it is the only allowance above zero. It is an allowance, not a rank.

1d

How the reader's own bracket is marked

Marked, with the climb's own treatment, because the reader arrives from the climb and should find themselves on the grid. The column is tinted YOURS_TINT (12% of the rank's ink over the ground), the header has a 2px ink rule along its top, the name is in ink, and where your cards are / your cards sits under the name (climb.roleHere / roleHereShort). Figures keep their neutral ink, so the mark shows position and not a result. A range marks both ([1,2] tints two columns). [4] and [4,5] tint the 4 · 5 row and add a 2px ink rule on its left edge. The sheet only opens from an analyzed climb, so there is always a bracket to mark.

1e

Stacked over a card: not drawn, because no cell names one

The sheet is about rules, not this deck, so it names categories and not cards. An example card in a cell (an Armageddon beside mass land denial) would be us picking the card that defines the category, which is a kind of weighting, and it would have to be checked against Wizards' text we do not quote. The reader's own cards are in the findings, one tap back. Depth stays at one. The only control in the body is the Wizards link, which leaves the app. If Product wants example cards later, they stack exactly as the receipt does (card sheet from 96, this sheet on --surface-sunken behind it, header at 0.55, body at 0.35), and nothing new needs drawing.
