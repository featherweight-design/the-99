Move a copy to another deck later

A copy went to the wrong deck and, once the receipt was gone, nothing offered the move. The shipped chooser (“Where this copy goes”) gets two more doors: card details and the collection History entry. Every frame is the shipped component rendered from main with the shipped CSS (amonkhet), with this round’s proposal applied. Example card: Swords to Plowshares, one copy, two decks list it.

Calls

1

Card details: “Change” at the end of the In your decks heading (C1), only when you own the card and no copy is free, so some deck is buying it or every copy is held. With a copy to spare there is nothing to move, so no Change. It opens the shipped chooser over card details: sheet on a phone, 520 modal on desktop.

2

The chooser is the shipped one, title “Where this copy goes”, sub “Swords to Plowshares · ×1 in your collection” (C2). Deck names are the control, gets it / buys it, tap the pick again to go back to first committed (C3). It writes the reservation (assignCopyFor) and changes no deck list or version; every claimant’s marks and buy state update.

3

R1b · “Other decks · N”, closed by default under the decks that list it (O). One row that opens in place, however many decks. Open, it lists only decks whose colors allow the card, in the same rows as above: the mark, the deck, its card count, adds it. A deck at 99 or more is still offered and says so (“99 cards · makes 100”). Decks outside its colors are left out, with one faint line saying so.

3b

One choice across both groups. Picking another deck works like picking above: accent row, “adds it · gets it”, and the decks above flip to buys it. The card goes into that deck’s draft and gets the copy when you commit. Tap again to undo. Closed, the header names the pick (O3). With more than 6 decks, a Find a deck field tops the open list (F1, F2).

3c

More decks than copies (M) is the shipped row’s own case: one copy uses round marks, two copies use square marks with “2 of 2 given”. A pick in Other decks counts toward that cap (M3).

4

History: “Change” at the end of an add’s “Went to …” line, same rule as call 1, opening the same chooser (H1, H2). The record is never rewritten: after a move the line reads “Went to Giada’s Angels. Now Heroes of the Plains has it.” (H3).

4b

R1c · a large entry (L, your 62-card add). Change sits on each card row’s own “Went to …” line, never on the entry’s summary line, which names many decks. The summary line adds “Change where 3 cards go”, which opens the entry on a new last tab, Can move · 3, holding only those rows. A card listed on several printing rows carries its line, and its Change, once, as shipped.

5

Matches the removal chooser (approved R1): the same row grammar (round mark, neutral default, accent pick, Back to first committed) and the same rule that a deck that can’t do something says why in its row.
