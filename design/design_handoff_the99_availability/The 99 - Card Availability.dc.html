4 surfaces
Card availability · #43 · R1

One diamond. Four states. Mostly nothing.

A physical card is in one deck at a time, and the app has never known it. This pass gives that fact one marker — a diamond and a number — and puts it on four surfaces at two densities. The diamond is hollow when a brew wants the card, solid when nothing is free, and doubled and red when more finished decks want it than you own. One shape means one concept; the fill carries confidence.

Everything else is silence. A player whose collection covers their decks sees nothing new on any of the four — no empty slot, no zero state, no reserved gutter. Noise scales with scarcity, not with collection size.

Every state leads with the phone; desktop sits beside it. Mock quantities are real arithmetic over a fabricated collection — a Jeskai player with four decks. Nothing here asserts where a card physically is.

1 · What the marker is
A diamond glyph + a count + a word. Chip on art, inline in rows. Never a treatment on the whole row.
2 · Two severities
Same glyph. Hollow = soft, solid = hard. Outline is the universal “provisional”; it needs no legend.
3 · Over-committed
On the card, everywhere — plus one summary line inside the deck it is in. Not on the deck index (out of scope).
4 · The toggle
Segmented, under the label Score against. Default Everything I own. Persists via ?avail=.
5 · Two ownership controls
They survive — because they never meet. The builder gets the marker and no toggle. Drawn as proof, not asserted.
6 · Naming locked-up cards
Annotation on the cards already shown, reached by a count-with-disclosure. No second list.
7 · The receipt line
The line always renders; only the second clause appears. Row height never changes, so list rhythm is safe.
Vocabulary
Kept free / in use. Available collides with the shipped buy tag on the very same tile.
Locked decisions — all six drawable, none worked around
Both severities surface (§1) — hollow and solid. Completeness is the severity source (§2) — the Draft/Building/Playable/Complete pill is reused verbatim and is the only place a deck’s maturity is ever named. Adds proceed (§3) — the over-committed row below is drawn after a successful add, with no dialog anywhere in this file. Two ranked states behind a toggle (§4). Counts, not booleans (§5) — every marker carries a number. No global setting (§6) — one control, one surface, and §5 of the open items reports on how that lands.

The call: one glyph, three fills
A dot was the obvious answer and it is already taken. The ownership dot sits top-right on every tile since #158. A second dot on the same 168px tile is a puzzle, not a signal — so availability gets the shape the brand already owns: the crown diamond, rotated 45°, at 6px.
Hollow → solid → doubled is a single legible progression. Two unrelated badges would make soft and hard read as different features; one indistinguishable badge would make a sketch and a sleeved deck read as the same claim. Fill is the confidence axis, and it is the axis that survives being 6px.
Never a treatment on the row. Dimming or striping a row says “unavailable”, and nothing here is unavailable — every one of these cards can still be added. A chip annotates; a treatment forbids. We are not allowed to forbid.
Colour is never alone. Every marker is glyph plus number plus word. Greyscale the file and all four states remain distinguishable by shape and text.

The call: segmented, and it says what it does
Segmented, not a switch. A switch implies a default-off setting with one true state; these are two equally valid questions. Both labels stay visible so the player can see the alternative exists — the whole point of a re-rank they have never met before.
Default: Everything I own. A first-deck player has zero hard claims, so What’s free would produce an identical list reached by a scarier road. Defaulting to the scarcity view also breaks “silence is the default” on the surface with the most rows.
Persists, but visibly. The choice writes ?avail=free and is restored on return — the same 1:1 query-param contract the colour filter already ships (?c=B, commanders decision 6). Shareable, back-buttonable, and it cannot silently outlive the session that set it.
It sits beside the colour filter, and the two do not compete. The colour filter narrows the field; this changes the score. Showing N of 24 is identical in both segments, and Order: Fit ↓ · fixed stays true — the order rule never changed, only what Fit is measured against.
The delta is the argument. ↓21 next to a re-ranked score is why the toggle exists. It appears only in state B, only where the score moved.

The call: the count is the disclosure
A separate “in use” list would duplicate the matched-cards list. Twelve of the thirty-four are already on this page; a second list means the same card in two places with two treatments, and a player scrolling past both cannot tell whether they are looking at twelve cards or twenty-four.
So the receipt’s second clause is the control. 12 already in use is a disclosure button that filters the list below it to those twelve and adds the claiming deck’s name and status pill to each. Closing restores all thirty-four with the annotations still in place.
The status pill is the evidence, not a new badge. Complete beside Atraxa is why Smothering Tithe reads solid rather than hollow. Locked decision 2 asked for the pill to be the severity source; here it is doing that work visibly instead of only in the query.
The expanded copy is where we refuse to overclaim. “Sleeved into decks you’ve finished” describes a list, and “you’d be taking them out of something” is about the list too. Nothing says the card is in a box, a binder, or a sleeve on a shelf. This is the sentence most likely to drift in a copy edit.

Parity note — the one structural delta in this handoff
Every other construct here is identical at both widths. This one is not: desktop splits, phone filters. Both are the same disclosure with the same two counts and the same annotations; the phone simply shows one half at a time with a show all 34 return.
Flagged rather than hidden, because “parity” in this repo has meant same constructs, not same pixels — and a two-column split at 390px would be four-word rows.

The call: the builder gets no second control
Yes, two adjacent ownership controls would collide. Owned only and a hypothetical Free only in the same 390px bar are two checkboxes about the same noun, differing on a word the player has known for four minutes. It is exactly the collision the workshop flagged.
So the builder does not get one. On this surface availability is annotation only. Every state above is drawn, nothing is filtered by it, and Owned only keeps its exact shipped meaning and position. Per-surface controls (locked decision 6) does not mean one control per surface — it means the control goes where the question is asked, and “what can I still build with” is asked on /commanders, not mid-build.
Filtering here would also break “never block” by the back door. A Free only filter that defaults on is a block wearing a filter’s clothes: the card you wanted is simply not in the list, with no marker to explain why.
If it ever does land, the rename is the fix, not the layout: Owned only → In my collection, which separates the nouns before the toggles have to. Gap report §1.

The call: on the card, plus one line in the deck
On the card, everywhere the card appears. Over-committed is a fact about a card, not a deck — the same physical copy is over-subscribed whether you are looking at it in Kykar, in Consider, or on the commander detail page. A card-level fact that only rendered in one place would be a fact the player could not act on.
And once at the top of the deck — because a 99-row list will not surface a single red glyph on row 61 by scrolling. The summary line is a count and a name, it appears only when the count is above zero, and it never renders on a deck with no over-committed cards. Silence still holds.
Not on the deck index. The per-deck roll-up is explicitly out of scope, and the status pill is not touched: this deck reads Building because it has 48 cards, and over-commitment does not change that. One status vocabulary, as ruled.
The banner is not an error. No icon-in-a-circle, no dismiss, no retry — it is a tinted strip with the marker glyph as its bullet, and the copy closes on “it’s a shopping decision.” The refusal pattern from #140 appears nowhere in this handoff, because nothing here was refused.

Contract · a11y, tokens, motion
Accessibility
Never colour alone. Glyph fill (hollow / solid / doubled) plus a number plus a word. Greyscaled, all four states stay distinct.

The marker is text, not an image. It reads inline in the row’s existing description — “Smothering Tithe, ×1 owned, 0 free, in Atraxa”. The diamond is aria-hidden; it decorates a string that is always present.

The disclosure is a real button — aria-expanded, aria-controls the matched-cards list. Toggling announces the new count on the page’s existing live region.

The ranking toggle is a radiogroup, labelled by the Score against heading. Not tabs — it switches data, not panels.

Contrast, against the real row and tile background --surface #1E1A15. #E8CE93 ≈ 11.8:1. #E9917A ≈ 7.6:1. #C6B79A ≈ 9.2:1. Brass #C9A24B ≈ 7.5:1 — all four AAA for normal text. The shipped #C6533F is used for glyph and border only — never for text — per the richness pass’s contrast rule.

Targets. The disclosure is 44px on phone. Nothing else in this pass is interactive; the marker itself is never a target.
Tokens & motion
Four new tokens, all derived. --avail-soft = existing --fg-muted. --avail-hard = --brass. --avail-hard-fg = #E8CE93 (new; brass is not AA as small text). --avail-over-fg = #E9917A (new; the shipped danger red lifted for text). No new hues.

Theme-aware. Every value above is a var(--*) reference; the two new literals go in the same block as the existing danger colour and get a light-theme pair there.

No motion. The marker never animates — not on appear, not on add. A card becoming committed is a fact arriving, and a fact that moves reads as an alert. Nothing joins the prefers-reduced-motion block.

No new dependency. The glyph is two rotated <span>s. The chip is flexbox. The disclosure is useState.

Copy lives in src/lib/copy/ — see availability · copy strings.ts in this folder, keyed and ready to paste.
