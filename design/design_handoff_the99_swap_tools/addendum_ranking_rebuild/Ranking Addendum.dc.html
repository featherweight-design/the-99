The 99 · swap tools · addendum, 2026-09-25 · ranking rebuild

What the smarter ranking shows: the mana line, type groups, and the reason a deck fits

This amends the parent handoff and the staples addendum, and replaces neither. It follows Hugh’s rulings of 2026-09-25. Nothing new is modelled: every figure is a plain share of decks, and rows stay as tall as they are today. Staples keep their treatment: the clause staple, any commander and no figure.

1a

A land row says what it makes

The mana line leads, and the drawback follows it

The fact line under the name now starts with the mana line, in body ink: makes white and red, untapped. If there’s a drawback, it follows after a dot, in muted ink: · 1 damage for either. The row keeps its two lines. The drawback isn’t replaced, because it’s the one fact the price can’t tell you.

A condition joins the mana line

If the land comes in untapped only sometimes, the condition finishes the clause: untapped if you pay 2 life, untapped with a Mountain or Plains. A shockland then has no drawback segment, because the life it costs is the condition. The four forms are untapped, untapped if / with …, tapped, and a color made once.

One-time mana is named, not counted

A tap-and-sacrifice ability no longer counts as making that color. The mana line lists only the colors the land makes every turn (makes red, tapped). The one-time color sits in the drawback segment instead: white once, by sacrificing it. Such lands fall to the last rung.

1b

Stand-ins grouped by card type

Same type, then other types, three of each

The yours, free and to buy groups give way to same type · creatures and then other types. Each group shows its top three, followed by a text row, Show all 5 other types ▾, that expands the group in place and becomes Show fewer ▴. A group of three or fewer gets no toggle. Ownership stays on the row as the green dot and the free tag.

Jobs go in the lead. Synergy stays two plain shares

Every stand-in does every job the card does, so the lead says so once (ramps and fixes) and the rows don’t repeat it. What orders a group is how much more Gishath decks run a card than other Naya decks. The row shows both shares, in 58% of Gishath decks, 41% of Naya, and never the difference as a score. An other-types row starts with its type (sorcery ·).

For lands, the order is implied, not labelled

Lands get one group, same type · lands, headed best mana first. The rungs have no labels or dividers, because the first words of each mana line already show the order: untapped, untapped if…, tapped, once. Within a rung, synergy comes first and then price, so free copies lead. Design’s readings, which Hugh may overrule: a land isn’t offered non-land stand-ins, and a staple sorts after the synergy-ranked cards in its group.

1c

Find a deck: the reason line

The reason replaces the commander chip

The row’s second line is now one mono line. It gives the reason, then · this deck ramp 7 of 10 when the card has a role. The two chips become one run of text, so the row isn’t taller even with a long card name. At 390 that run wraps between its segments. The N% of X decks figure is gone, because it’s no longer what orders the list.

One reason, the strongest there is

If there’s a combo, the line reads Completes a combo with {card}. The text is in full ink, and the card name is dotted, which opens its details. Failing that, Joins {n} other {theme} cards, in body ink. With neither, the line falls back to the two plain shares the list is ordered by. A staple still reads staple · any commander, as in the staples addendum.

Ordered by synergy, not by name

The group’s order note changes from by name to by each commander’s synergy. A combo can include the deck’s own commander, as with Kinnan here. It counts only if every other piece is already in that deck’s draft. Already holds it is unchanged, and staples keep their by-name order.
