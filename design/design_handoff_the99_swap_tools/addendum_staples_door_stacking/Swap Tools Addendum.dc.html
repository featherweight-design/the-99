Three undrawn pieces: the staple read, the door on the shipped modal, card → sheet → card

This amends the parent handoff and replaces none of it. Where they disagree, this file wins for these three questions only. Locked and unchanged: Undo swap everywhere; every stand-in names what it gives up; staples come from the shared definition; no single score.

1a

The staple read (audit F1)

The figure is withheld, and the slot says why

For a card on the shared staples list, the commander chip keeps its place but drops the percentage. It reads staple · any commander in italic body ink. A 91% beside Gishath would say "suits Gishath". The true reading is "suits anything", so no number is shown. The chip stays, so every row still has two slots and the need fact still sits beside it.

One sentence up top, stated once

A dashed note under the card plate explains the chip once rather than on every row. Dashed and neutral, it isn't a warning. Row order stays by name. When the staple has no role (Reliquary Tower, Rogue's Passage, Swiftfoot Boots), the need chip is omitted as usual. The row is left with the name, the staple chip and Add, and the note says that's all there is to say.

Stand-ins: same words, and their own group

Yes, the stand-in row carries the same treatment. Its fact line ends staple, any commander instead of in N% of Gishath decks. Because spells are ranked by that figure, a staple can't rank by it either. Staples to buy get their own group, staples, cheapest first, between the free copies and the commander ranking. A free staple stays in yours, free with the same clause. Ordering is Design's reading; Hugh may overrule.

1b

The door, on the modal as it ships

Next to the price, where the price already is

This supersedes the parent's R2 placement under a buy tag. On desktop the door sits in the art column, under the shipped price tag: a mono limit line, then ⇄ See cheaper stand-ins →. On the phone it's one 44px strip right under the art header, which holds the price. It's the limit line on the left and the link on the right. The content column and the fixed footer don't change.

The over-limit color is in the words

The shipped price tag is --warning-outlined on every card, so the ring can't mark "over" here the way BuyTag's does. Instead, the limit line carries it: over your $10 limit in --warning, or under your $10 limit in faint ink. The link is accent in both states, and ⇄ uses the row's glyph so the two doors read as one tool.

Only when opened from a Shopping row

Unchanged from the parent: the door needs a deck and a card to replace. Opened from the collection, search or a set page, the art column is as shipped. Prev/Next page through the Shopping list, and the door follows each card's own limit state.

1c

Card → sheet → card

Does the original stay under the sheet? Yes.

The sheet stacks over Plateau's card, the same way Find a deck's picker stacks over Card Details. The card stays mounted, dimmed, with its scroll kept. On desktop its 824px edges show around the 640px sheet. The sheet's × becomes a back pill, ← Plateau, on desktop. On the phone the × stays, labelled Back to Plateau. That's the picker's pattern.

What a stand-in stacks over: the sheet

It's exactly the Shopping path: CardModal stacked, ← Cheaper stand-ins, the Take footer and Prev/Next through the stand-ins. The only difference is one more layer underneath. The stacked card's footer replaces Buy and Find a deck with Take, so there's no fourth door. Three layers above the page is the cap: page · card · sheet · card.

Phone: each layer starts 44px lower

Shopping's path already steps by 44: the sheet at 52, then the card at 96. From a card it's the card at 52, the sheet at 96 and the stand-in at 140. Each layer's header stays visible above the one on top, as the tap-back target. A tap on the scrim works the same as Escape.

Why no Undo on the card

Undo swap already lives in two places: the sheet's taken row and the Shopping receipt. A third copy on a card that may no longer be in the deck would be one more thing to keep in sync. The success-ink line says what happened, and one more Escape lands on the receipt.
