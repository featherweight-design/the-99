34px, not 32 and not 44. The row is one 32px hairline tall, plus the 2px a lit pip's --accent ring adds outside the control's own box — a 32px row clips it, and clipping the one carrier of the lit state is the pip defect arriving by another route. 44 is the phone's target and has no business here: these controls are 32px at md in the open head, and a bar that grew them would be a second skin for the same rack.
Why the phone's 52 and the desktop's 56 differ by four pixels and not by forty. They are the same row twice, sized by their own width's target: 44px controls in a 52px bar on the phone, 32px controls in a 56px bar at md — the desktop bar is taller because it keeps the head's real 10/12 padding rather than the phone bar's flush row, and because it carries five controls where the phone carries two. Neither figure is declared as a constant in code: the bar is pt-[10px] + a 34px row + pb-[12px] and measures itself, which is what the threshold reads.

The bar does not: it truncates at one line by design, so the collapsed height is the same 56 for every set in the catalog — which is exactly why the reserve is measured off the head and the threshold off the reserve, not off either constant.
§4 · call 1 — the rack survives by not being redrawn
“Survives, reduces, or goes” has a fourth answer, and it is the cheap one: the same nodes, one row fewer. The phone's collapsed bar had to be its own row because its controls change identity across the threshold — the field becomes a button back to the head, and Filters is a sheet trigger that does not exist at md. At desktop nothing changes identity: the rack's row 1 is already one 32px line of five controls and it already fits with room. So the collapse keeps that row mounted and hides the members around it, which buys three things a redrawn bar cannot. One: no second field. §29 forbids two controls with one accessible name on this page, and a redrawn bar would mount a field behind a display:none twin. Two: nothing to rescue. Focus, the caret, the 150ms debounce and the open rarity panel all survive the threshold because the element under them is never unmounted. Three: no desktop badge. The lit controls are on the bar, so the filter state is carried where it is applied — the phone needed a badge only because its controls were behind a sheet.

Same rule, and deliberately so. The shipped hook already derives both offsets from measurement rather than constants — the grid's own top for the collapse, the reserve's edge for the restore — and that derivation is width-agnostic: it asks “has the open head gone past” and “would the reserve show”, which are the right questions at 1440 as much as at 390. A desktop-specific rule would mean a second state machine for one number, and that number is already a variable.
The one change: HYSTERESIS_PX 80 → 120, at md only80px is inside one wheel notch. A mouse wheel step is ~100–120px in every major engine, so a desktop band of 80 can be crossed in both directions by two consecutive notches — the flutter the band exists to prevent, arriving on the input the phone does not have. A thumb scrolls continuously; a wheel quantises, and the band has to be wider than the quantum. 120 is one notch plus headroom, and it is a term in the existing expression rather than a new branch: HYSTERESIS_PX becomes a function of isDesktop, which the hook already takes.
Two gates the desktop adds, and one it must stop applying. Adds: an open rarity panel freezes the head exactly as the phone sheet does — the panel is anchored to a trigger in the row, so collapsing under it would move its anchor 220px while it is open; and a collapse is forbidden while the cost strip has focus inside it, because the member the collapse hides would take the keyboard with it. Stops: mayCollapse's !isDesktop term goes, and with it the breakpoint reopen — but the crossing must still not animate (the two forms are different designs, not two heights of one), and the bar height recorded at one width must still be cleared at the other.
§6 · call 4 — the CostStrip stays whole, and the bar carries no figure
There is no desktop expanded state to design, because the desktop form has no collapsed one. cost-strip.tsx ships the full card at md:flex, always open and never closable, with the disclosure buttons md:hidden on both sides of the swap — so the phone's “expands in place on tap” has no desktop counterpart to rule on. It stays that way: the caveat lines are the audit trail for the figure, up to four of them, and at desktop there is room for all four beside a 32px button. This round changes nothing in that file.
And no, the bar does not carry the cost — the desktop case is stronger than the phone's, not weaker. The figure re-scopes with the filters, and the collapsed bar is the one place where the filters can be changed with the figure's own denominator off screen: $188.66 over 45 of 46 becoming $12.04 over 3 of 3 under a reader's cursor, with the caveat count that explains it hidden, is a money figure that cannot be audited where it is read. The consequence, named: the collapse also hides Send N to ManaPool, which is this page's one primary action. Accepted — it is 120px of scroll from anywhere on the page, the reserve means it lands whole rather than half-drawn, and a commercial action that follows the reader down a 16,500px grid is the pattern this whole finding exists to remove.
