One rule decides four of the five
At 390px the pinned bar has one mono status line, and its tenant is whichever of three facts the player most needs right now: the cap, the slots that stay open, or the retired dock — in that order.
Gaps 4 and 5 are both “this line has nowhere to go at 390px”, and the shipped bar already has a line of exactly that register sitting on the dock notice. The three are mutually exclusive in practice — at the cap no slots stay open, and below it some do — so a ladder costs no height at all. It is also the only arrangement where the cap is explained beside the button rather than 167 rows above it.

Why a word, and why that word
The handoff drew the declined row in full and never drew the way into it, which is the defect: a state with no trigger is a state that does not exist. Eng was right to invent one and right to flag it.

But removal is a glyph everywhere in this app, so the trigger cannot be one. The rule this addendum adds: no glyph-only control on the candidate list. A glyph on a list of things you have not bought is one surface away from the glyph that deletes a card you have.

The declined row already ships the register. Show it again is mono 10.5px --fg-faint on a dashed underline, and the decline is its exact inverse — so it wears the same clothes and the pair reads as one toggle rather than as an action and its apology.

Four carriers now separate it from removal: a word not a glyph; a dashed underline, which is this app's reversible / unsettled vocabulary and which removal never wears; the mono figure register rather than the row's chrome; and at phone a different position entirely — line 2, not the right edge.

Rejected: a bordered plate like the game-log delete. That box is a confirm trigger for a destructive act, which is the association being severed.

Rejected: hover-only at desktop. Unreachable by touch, invisible until found, and it would make the phone and desktop affordances different in kind — the parity defect one surface over.

Rejected: swipe. Invisible, keyboard-hostile, and this list is 38 rows of small decisions where an accidental swipe costs a candidate.
One string, one lifecycle, three words that never overlap: the control says Set aside (a verb), the state it produces says not this one (a preference), and the way back says Show it again (a verb). Never Remove, Delete, Dismiss, Hide, or ×.

And list.declineAria — eng's Set {name} aside — is ratified verbatim. It was the right inverse of restoreAria, and the visible label is now the same verb, so the ENG-INVENTED marker comes off rather than the string.

Emptied by choosing is not empty

What a short form has to drop, and what it may not
The desktop sentence carries three clauses. A shorter one that is still two lines buys nothing, so the test is which clause survives alone.

The promise survives. Nothing is added until you say so is the entire reason the review step exists, and it is carried nowhere else on the surface — not in the caption, not on the door, not in the head.

The provenance does not need to. all from your collection is carried three more ways the moment the list opens: 31 filled --success dots, the head's own Tick the 31 I own, and every unticked row's buy tag. The count stays because it is the figure the sentence is about.

Three desktop strings collapse to two at phone, which is the part worth auditing. The two reopen variants differ only in an ownership figure, and at 390px the closed caption directly above already states the candidate count — so the phone reopen note drops the figure entirely and keeps the clause that matters, which is that the selection survived. One string, both cases.

Rejected: truncating with an ellipsis or a “more” disclosure. A safety promise behind a tap is not a promise.

Rejected: dropping the note at phone entirely. It is the only thing on the closed strip that says a tap is safe, and phone is where the door is most likely to be tapped by someone who has not read the desktop version.

Ratified — and the two things to change
The pattern is right and the diagnosis is right. game-history-row's inline confirm is this app's only confirm vocabulary, and its own header comment states the case it was built for: the row is the only correction path, so the question lives in the row rather than in a dialog. Keep all is that case exactly — a bulk act, in a toolbar, with no undo.

Amendment 1 — the confirming button must not take --danger. In the game log it is a delete. Keeping is a graduation, not a deletion — it is the ruling the ledger rests on, and it destroys no cards. Danger ink would say this loses cards, which is the one thing it does not do; what it loses is the record. So both answers take BULK_BUTTON_CLASS's own treatment, and the toolbar goes on recommending neither answer — which is that constant's stated reason for existing.

Amendment 2 — the question must name what is actually lost, in the words the surface uses. Not the ledger, which is an internal noun that appears nowhere on screen — the same trap eng caught in seedReversal, where the sweep pointed at a control called Clear all auto. The ledger's own header says Where these 99 cards came from, so the question says the record of where they came from.

And the asymmetry is the rule, not an inconsistency: Clear all auto gets no confirm, because it already ships SweepReceipt with an undo. Confirm where there is no undo; receipt where there is. Two destructive-looking neighbours with different treatments, for a reason a player can feel.

The better fix, not asked for and not blocking: make keep-all undoable — restore the auto flags and the pass membership — and the confirm goes away. That is a server-side inverse mutation and a bigger change than this addendum; it is in READINESS as the thing to want if the confirm proves annoying.

A11y and targets — a control the handoff never drew has to be reachable four ways
· The decline is a real <button> outside the row's <label>, exactly as shipped — inside it, every tap on the word would toggle the tick as well. Two tab stops per row, in reading order: tick, then decline.
· list.declineAria is ratified verbatim: “Set Shark Typhoon aside”. The visible label is the same verb, so the name is a superset of the label and never contradicts it.
· Targets: phone decline 44px tall on line 2 (the word's own box, not the row's); desktop the word in a 60px slot inside a 34px row, which is the shipped inline-target rule for a desktop text control. Bulk restore 44px phone / 30px desktop, matching its two neighbours. Both confirm answers 44px / 30px, matching game-history-row exactly.
· The decline is never adjacent to the commit at either width, and the confirm answers are never adjacent to the sweep — the question replaces both bulk controls, so nothing destructive sits beside Keep them.
· The bulk restore announces, because 38 rows changing shape at once is the largest visual change on the surface: “38 candidates back on the list. 0 of 31 ticked.” A single decline already announces and is unchanged.
· The ladder changes nothing spoken. announce.capped still fires on the settle and the inert box's aria-describedby still points at the cap line — which now lives in the head at desktop and the bar at phone, so the id moves with it and must stay rendered in both.
· The confirm takes focus, and returns it. Focus moves to Keep them when the question opens and back to Keep all 40 on cancel — the trigger unmounts, and a button that unmounts drops focus to the body.
· Colour is never the only carrier, unchanged: the decline is a word and an underline; a declined row is an eyebrow, a word and an announcement; the cap is a sentence and an inert glyph.
· Focus is the global :focus-visible ring from @layer base — 2px --accent at +2px, not overridden anywhere in this addendum.
