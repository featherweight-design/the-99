Top off the last 31 · #360 · one panel, folding #359 and #361

A control that places 31 cards should ask first.

The shipped strip is two buttons that each commit up to 31 cards on one tap, and the only way back is a sweep that removes 92 of them. #360 asks for the cards first and a selective accept; #361 says the two CTAs read as out of place; #359 asks for provenance per fill, persisted, so a top-off reverses independently of the seed. All three are the same defect — the fill is an act when it should be a proposal — and one change answers them: the strip stops adding cards and starts opening a list.

The two CTAs collapse into one, and the mixed fill falls out of it for free. Two buttons split by ownership were an ownership filter wearing two verbs — and this product has already ruled that ownership is a label, not a filter. So there is one ranked list of candidates, owned and unowned interleaved by synergy, and it arrives with everything you own already ticked. That preselection is the old owned-fill button. Ticking a card you do not own is the old top-up button. A mixed fill is just a list with both kinds ticked, and it needs no control of its own.

R3 — the ledger meets the shipped auto lifecycle
R2 ruled on one of three controls. clear-auto.tsx is the kept note, the bulk keep and the sweep, with a per-row keep beside them — and keeping a card clears its auto flag. R2's ledger arrived on top of that cluster without mentioning it. The audit's four consequences all turn on one question, so R3 answers that question once.
The call
Keeping is not a third reversal. It is a graduation out of provenance — so a kept card leaves its pass, exactly as a card removed by hand does.
The shipped act already says this in the schema: keeping clears the auto flag. The card stops being the app’s suggestion and becomes the player’s choice. A ledger of what the app placed should not still be claiming a card the player has adopted — and R2 already has the rule for a card that leaves a pass. Keeping is a second way to leave, not a new kind of event.
1 · the vanishing reversal
The sweep is present exactly when the ledger is, and that is now an invariant rather than a coincidence. Sweep absent ⇔ zero auto rows ⇔ every pass empty ⇔ no ledger to hold an orphaned pointer. Keep-all empties both at once.
2 · undo vs. a kept card
The question dissolves. A kept card is not in the pass, so undo cannot reach it — no contradiction with the keep control, and no pass that is un-undoable in part. The line recounts and its button counts down: Undo these 22.
3 · two reports of one fact
They are two facts, and R3 keeps them that way. The toolbar note is about the deck — how many cards are yours for good. The ledger is about a pass — how many of its cards are still the app’s. The ledger never prints a deck-wide kept total, so nothing is said twice and the shipped note is unmoved.
4 · backfill
No backfilled seed line. The ledger begins at migration and says so. R2’s rule inferred the seed from auto rows, which keeping has already eaten — while filled stays true forever. Design will not print a count it cannot prove: one honest line beats a fabricated 41-card seed.
Both R2 calls are taken and #361 is confirmed clean. Everything else stands unchanged: one door for two CTAs, one interleaved list with owned pre-ticked, the tick cap as the trade, the two mid-fill sentences, the parity row with its cost track, no inner scroller, the commit in the dock’s slot. R3 adds no control, no string to the toolbar, and no schema field beyond R2’s table.

Scope 1 · see the cards first
The strip opens a list in its own place, in the page. Not a modal, not a tray, not a second route — the review list renders where the buttons were, above the decklist it is about. The strip's control changes from a verb to a door: Choose the last 31.
Scope 2 · the mixed fill
One list, both pools, interleaved by synergy — and no pool control at all. Owned arrives ticked, unowned arrives unticked. The mix is whatever the player ticks, so “mixed” is not a mode the app has to offer or the player has to find.
Scope 3a · #361, the two CTAs
Closed by deletion, not restyling. They read as out of place because they were two doors to one room, differing only in which pool they drew from. One door, one weight: brass outline. The --warning count moves off a button face and onto the money, where it belongs.
Scope 3b · #359, undo and provenance
The provenance line becomes a ledger of passes, and each line is reversible on its own. Persisted, so it survives a reload; the seed is a line in it too, and its reversal is the sweep. Show these 27 is how the distinction is seen before deciding. No toast, no time window, no new row atom.
Scope 4 · mid-fill
Partly accepted is the ordinary state, not an edge case. The strip returns, recounted (The last 4), and the list remembers: what you declined stays in it, marked and restorable. Stopping short by choice and running out of cards are two different sentences and must never share one.
The constraint · 31 rows at 390px
No inner scroller, ever. Why 38 candidates for 31 slots — and why that is the whole mixed-fill design
A list of exactly 31 for 31 slots is not a choice, it is a receipt. So the list is every legal candidate EDHREC ranks — in colour, not in the deck, singleton-legal — and the arithmetic does the rest: you cannot tick more than the slots you have.

#359 in full — the schema, taken
R1 ruled no schema change and no new marker. #359 asks for both, in its own words, and R2 takes them. Design does not get to decline a schema change the issue exists to request — and the argument was wrong on its own terms: “no time window, ever” cannot be built on client memory. Row ids in a page’s state die on reload, which is a five-second window with extra steps and a worse failure mode, because nothing tells the player it closed.

So a fill is a persisted pass, and the provenance line is a ledger of them. One line per pass, newest last, each stating what it placed and what it cost. Each line reverses on its own, in any order, at any age — a top-off is undoable independently of the seed, which is exactly what the scope asks for and what a single batch pointer could never give.

The seed is a line in the ledger too. That is the ruling that makes the rest coherent: if a fill is a pass, the build is the first pass, and pretending otherwise would leave 68 of 95 cards with no provenance in a table built for provenance. Its reversal is the sweep, which is where it already lives — so the seed line names its reversal rather than growing a second button for it.

“Before deciding” is Show these 27. The scope asks the player to see which cards came from which pass before reversing one. Tapping it puts a brass hairline on that pass’s rows in the decklist below — marks them, dims nothing, filters nothing, moves nothing — and the ledger line stays put with its undo beside it. The decision and the evidence are one tap apart and never on separate screens.

The row keeps its floor. No pass ordinal on the tag, no new row atom: AutoTag stays exactly as it ships, and Clear all auto still sweeps every pass at once. The distinction lives where the decision lives.

Rejected: an ordinal on the auto tag (auto 2). A number no row explains, 95 times, to serve a decision taken in one place.

Rejected: a per-pass filter chip on the toolbar. The toolbar filters by ownership and it is not a history browser; a chip per pass grows without limit.

Rejected: a toast with an undo link. It expires, it covers the decklist it is talking about, and this product has no toast.
R3 — keeping, and the three controls R2 did not mention
The shipped cluster is three controls, not one: a kept note, a bulk keep, and the sweep — plus a per-row keep. R2 ruled on the sweep and inherited the rest by accident.

Keeping is a graduation, not a reversal. The shipped act says so in the schema: keeping clears the auto flag, and the card stops being the app’s suggestion and becomes the player’s choice. A ledger of what the app placed must not still claim a card the player has adopted. So a kept card leaves its pass — the same exit R2 already wrote for a card removed by hand, and not a new kind of event.

The line says which exit each card took, because they mean opposite things: 27 cards · 3 now yours, 2 removed since. Removed is a rejection, now yours is an endorsement, and a ledger that blurred them would be worse than one that only counted.

Which makes the undo honest. It never touches a kept card, never contradicts the keep control, and never leaves a pass un-undoable in part: Undo these 22. Keeping is how you protect a card from an undo — which is a sentence the player can act on, and a use the keep control did not have before.

And a pass at zero leaves the ledger, whether its cards were swept, removed or kept. Keep a whole pass and its line goes: nothing was reversed, but nothing is outstanding either.

Rejected: keeping as a third reversal-adjacent act the ledger models. It is the one act in the cluster that is not a reversal — modelling it as one would make the ledger a history of everything that ever happened to a card, which is the version-control feature every round of this handoff has refused.

Rejected: absorbing the cluster and leaving the toolbar only the sweep. Tempting, and wrong twice: it moves two shipped controls no issue complained about, and the kept note has to survive at zero auto, where there is no ledger to hold it.
Two facts, not two reports — and the invariant that saves the seed line
The audit is right that a count of fill cards would be printed twice. R3 keeps them as two different facts instead of moving either.

68 cards are yours now — 5 kept is about the deck: how much of it has graduated. It stays in the toolbar, unmoved, unedited, and it is the only place a deck-wide kept total appears.

27 cards · 3 now yours is about a pass: how much of that pass is still the app’s to take back. It is a per-line figure and the ledger never sums it.

The header states passes and money only — no kept total, no auto total — which is what keeps the two from colliding.
The seed line’s reversal cannot be orphaned, and now that is provable:
sweep absent ⇔ 0 auto rows ⇔ every pass empty ⇔ ledger absent
Each step is one of the shipped rules, and R2’s state (a) already ends there. Keep-all empties the ledger and the toolbar in the same act, so the line naming the sweep is gone at the moment the sweep is. What R2 had by luck, R3 states as an invariant — and it is the thing to re-check if any future pass can survive with zero auto rows.
Scope 4 — stopping short is not running out
The shipped §7.6 line says your collection ran out 7 short. That sentence is true when the pool is exhausted and a lie when the player simply did not tick everything — and after a selective accept, the second case is the common one.

So there are two lines and the app can always tell which it is: candidates remaining is the test. Above zero, the strip returns with a recounted heading and a caption that says how many are still on the list. At zero, the shipped shortfall line prints unchanged, keeping the provenance line's --accent rule — never --warning, because owning what you own is not an outage.

Nothing about a partly-filled deck is treated as unfinished business. No banner, no nudge, no badge on the tab, and the strip does not grow a second sentence urging the player on. Four open slots on a 95-card deck is a deck, and the count in the header is the only reminder it gets.

A11y — a 38-row selection has to be operable without sight or a mouse
· The door is aria-expanded / aria-controls on the list — CoverageDisclosure's shipped contract — and it does not steal focus. Opening moves nothing; the list is next in reading order.
· The list is a group of checkboxes, not a listbox and not a menu: role="group" with an accessible name carrying both counts, and each tick a real checkbox with aria-checked. Tab reaches every row; space toggles.
· The row's accessible name carries everything the marks say silently — the dot, the pip track, the availability chip and both figures are all aria-hidden: “Shark Typhoon. Five generic and one blue. Not in your collection, six dollars ten. Synergy plus 34 points. In 29% of decks.”
· The cost is spoken in full and never capped, from CostPips' sr-only alternative — the +N pip truncates the drawing, not the sentence. A reader who cannot see the track is the one reader for whom truncation is not a density trade.
· The committed state is a word, not a hue. --avail-hard-fg against --success is a colour difference alone at 7px, so the marker chip beside it carries none free as real text and the accessible name says “you own one, none free.”
· The cap is spoken, not just dimmed. Inert boxes take aria-disabled with aria-describedby pointing at the cap line, so a reader who tabs into one hears “31 of 31 slots ticked” rather than nothing happening.
· Ticking announces on a debounce, never per tap. One message per settle: “27 of 31 ticked, 3 to buy.” Thirty-one fast taps is one announcement — the shipped selection rule from surface A.
· The commit and the undo both announce once, with counts and denominators: “27 cards added. 95 of 99, 3 to buy.” / “27 cards put back. 68 of 99.”
· The ledger is a list, and each line's controls are named by their pass — Undo these 27 is ambiguous out of context, so the accessible name is “Undo the 27 cards from top-off 1”. Three passes means three unambiguous buttons.
· The mark is not colour alone. Show these 27 is a toggle (aria-pressed), it announces “27 cards from top-off 1 marked in the deck”, and each marked row's accessible name gains “from top-off 1” while the mark is up — so a reader gets the grouping the brass edge draws.
· Ownership is never colour alone — filled versus hollow is a shape, the word is in the accessible name, and the buy tag is real text. Nor is the ticked state: the box carries a ✓ as well as a fill and a border.
· Focus survives the commit. It stays on the primary as it becomes Sleeving 27 cards…, then moves to the ledger line that pass just created — which is where its undo is, and the reason the ledger is ordered oldest-first and never reorders.
· The bottom-slot swap is announced, not silent — “Quick-add hidden while the list is open” once on open, and focus never starts in the retired dock. Closing restores the dock with its query and focus intact.
· Closing the list returns focus to the door. Cancel and Close are the same act and the same string is not used for both.
· Targets: 18px box on a 40px row at desktop (the whole row is the label, so the target is the row); 26px box on a 52px row at phone; 44px on every phone button and both list-head controls.
Motion, and the list of things not added
No motion class is added. The pass mark appears and clears instantly — 27 left edges fading in across a 95-row list is a page that shivers. Ticking is an instant state change — a 38-row list where rows animate on toggle is a list that flinches, and this one gets toggled 27 times in a row. Opening the list is a height change with no transition, per the shipped disclosure. A decline collapses with no animation out.

Not added: no modal, no tray, no drawer, no sheet, no second route, no confirm step, no toast, no time-limited undo, no new row atom, no ordinal on the auto tag, no per-pass filter chip, no basket, no per-row subtotal, no pool switch, no segmented ownership filter, no inner scroller, no virtualisation, no pagination, no “show more”, no skeleton, no shimmer, no badge, no nudge to finish a 95-card deck, no second pinned bar, and no new empty-state construct.

Four shipped behaviours are deliberately changed rather than added to: the strip's two controls become one door; the fill provenance line becomes a ledger of passes with a reversal per line; the deck gains a persisted fill-pass table (#359's own request, and the only new persistence); and the quick-add dock stands down while the list is open. Everything else on this surface is a shipped atom moved, recounted, or reused verbatim — including every string that survives, which is most of them.
