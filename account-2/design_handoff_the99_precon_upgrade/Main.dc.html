Pick a direction, then take the swaps

Hugh’s five rulings are closed: the player picks the direction (nothing pre-selected), strategies come from stored data, one run is one checked call, 12 to 18 swaps with cuts from the other themes and no added card over $12, 2 credits a run. These are Design’s calls. Fixture: Prismari Artistry (Rootha, UR), illustrative, as earlier precon rounds; you own 41 of 85. Copy marked DRAFT goes through /copy-review. Dashed outlines mark what is new on a shipped page.

Design calls

  1. Entry: a section on the open precon page, under the Overview, above the list-note line. Phone and desktop alike: the Overview says what the deck does, and the next thing the page offers is where to take it. The head’s Start and Link a deck, the own box and the view switch do not move. Label Upgrade this precon in the Overview’s label style; one lead sentence; the strategy cards; one press. Not drawn on an early list (view-only, no Start), with the switch off, or for a precon with no Overview yet when it backs nothing (empty state below).
  2. The picker is the strategy cards themselves, nothing pre-selected. Each card: the shipped strategy glyph on a medallion, the name, N of its cards, one plain sentence of what this deck does with it, three of its own cards as art with names (each opens card details), and N more. Pressing a card selects it (radio ring, accent edge); pressing it again unselects. Three in a row on desktop, stacked on a phone. One backed strategy: one card, still a pick. None: a dashed line saying why, and no press.
  3. The Overview’s own plan is marked, as a source, not a recommendation: a foot line the Overview’s read in mono faint, the same weight as N of its cards. The player has just read that paragraph above; the line tells them this card is the same idea, so they don’t read it as a fourth one. It is never first by rule, never accented, and never selected.
  4. The credit confirm is the press, asked once. The press names the strategy (Upgrade toward Artifacts), and its line carries the range and the cost (12 to 18 swaps · uses 2 credits · …), so the chosen strategy, 2 credits and 12 to 18 are in front of the player as they press. No dialog for a first run. The one ask is v2’s same-inputs rule: running a strategy already run on this precon asks once, inline.
  5. The wait is v2’s clock, short: the press row becomes the clock box (0:04 · Finding swaps toward Artifacts · usually about 6 seconds), the other cards dim and hold, three skeleton rows below. No streaming: one call lands the set whole.
  6. Results, in place under the picker: a head with the glyph and 14 swaps toward Artifacts, then one mono line: bracket 2 → 3 · Upgraded (v2’s bar wording), 10 to buy · $28.90 · each under $12, 4 you own, the date. Pairs are grouped by the theme the cut came from (Cuts from Spells 12, Cuts from Tokens 2), which shows the rule at a glance. Each pair is the page’s own read-only DecklistRow twice, out then in (side by side with an arrow on desktop), owned dot and cost on both, the add’s price at the right or you own it, one reason under. Every name opens card details.
  7. No Pick toggle. Pairs come as a set: a cut’s slot is filled by its own add, so a picked half-set would leave the deck off 100 or keep a cut with nothing in its place. Taking the set or starting over is the honest choice, and the 12-to-18 floor is part of what was paid for.
  8. After results (the open call): Start the deck with the swaps applied. Start the deck with these 14 swaps opens S1 with its facts updated. Why: a read-only list leaves a new owner retyping 28 changes, which is the job they paid for; the started deck links to the precon, so v0 is still the box as it came and v1’s diff shows exactly the 14 swaps (R3 K10); and nothing is applied to anything that already exists. The head’s Start stays as it is, for the box unchanged.
  9. Another strategy: once a result exists, the cards fold to one row each (glyph, name, status: 14 swaps · Nov 10 or N of its cards · 2 credits). Selecting a run one shows its stored result at no cost; selecting an unrun one opens its card and the press, whose line says Another upgrade · … · uses 2 credits · Artifacts stays here. Every kept result stays one press away.

Gaps for engineering

  • G1 · Short runs are not stored as results. readUpgrades returns only done, unrefunded runs, and a short run is refunded (charge.ts). The board shows a short result as kept and startable; engineering either keeps refunded-short runs readable (recommended) or the short state says it isn’t kept.
  • G2 · The buy total. buyTotalCents sums every add. The head counts only adds the player doesn’t own (10 to buy · $28.90), as Shopping does; ownership is read at draw time, so the stored total isn’t the shown one.
  • G3 · Strategy glyphs. Detection keys on EDHREC slugs (detect.ts), the glyphs on strategy keys (strategy-icon.tsx). Slugs with no glyph draw the shipped dashed fallback; a slug → glyph map is owed.
  • G4 · Blurb copy. detect.ts’s fixed blurbs are generic (“Play many artifacts and reward each one.”). The brief asks for what this deck does with it, for someone who has never played it. Drawn sentences are deck-specific and DRAFT; either the blurbs gain a commander-aware form or the generic ones ship. /copy-review.
  • G5 · Start with swaps needs createDeckFromPrecon to take a run id and apply its pairs to the draft, linking the deck so v0 stays the box. The I own this precon box still adds the box’s 100 cards and tokens.
  • G6 · Bracket after. bracket_after may come back equal; the line then reads bracket stays 2 · Core (v2’s wording), drawn on the short state.
