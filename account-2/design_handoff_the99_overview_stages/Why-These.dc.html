The calls

1
When it shows
From N = 6 (12 or more changes; about 9 a side to read). Under that the per-card lines are short enough to read whole. One constant, so engineering can move it; your 20-change example is N = 10.
2
Where
One box, Why these cards, after the You asked box, the lead and the read line, above the lists (desktop) or above the Additions and Cuts switch (phone): the shipped You asked box’s look (--surface-rail, 10px radius, mono label). Then one line at the top of each list, under its count, saying what that side’s cards have in common.
3
What it says
Three lines, each naming at most two cards as card links (the shipped CardName): the lead (≤ 120 characters): what the set does for the request as a whole; the additions line and the cuts line (≤ 160 each): the groups inside that side, with a count each. No per-card reasons: the rows keep those.
4
While it streams
The box and both side lines hold their place from the first row, with a faint line saying when they fill (Written once both lists are in.), so the lists never move under a pick. They fill together when the cuts finish, with the run’s other end-of-run changes.
5
History
Stored with the run and shown the same way when a past run opens from History. A run written before this has none and draws no box.
6
Engineering
Written in the cuts call (it already sees the additions), as why: { lead, add, cut } on the stored report; each line runs the run’s own checks (names in the list or the offer, counts that match the rows). A line that fails is left out, not retried; if the lead fails, the box isn’t drawn.
7
Copy (drafts)
Why these cards · Written once both lists are in. Pick while you wait. · Said once both lists are in. For /copy-review.
