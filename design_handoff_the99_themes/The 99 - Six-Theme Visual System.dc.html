What the specimen surfaces
  • Syncopate is the outlier. It has no bold below 700, very wide default tracking and tall caps — “Ghired, Mirror of the Wilds” takes three lines at 29px where Cinzel takes two. Oil and Iron needs a 26px cap on that slot, or the modal header grows a line.
  • Big Shoulders is the opposite problem. Condensed and short — it fits everything, and at 22px reads a full step smaller than the others. Screen Glow wants +1px on headings under 24px to keep the hierarchy even.
  • Metamorphous has no weight axis at all — one weight, 400. Every font-bold and font-[700] on a heading is a no-op under Rune Frost. That is 30-odd call sites silently losing their weight contrast; it needs a synthetic-bold decision, not a CSS one.
  • Spectral and Cinzel are interchangeable at these sizes in width, so Moonlit is the safe theme and Cinzel-fitted layouts port to it unchanged.

Picker rules
  • Selected is a ring plus a tick, never colour alone — §10's no-colour-only rule applies to the picker itself.
  • Focus is the app's own 2px accent outline at 2px offset, inherited from globals.css @layer base. It reads against the popover surface in all six.
  • Roving-tabindex radiogroup, arrow keys move and select in one step. Selection applies instantly; there is no Save, so the live region announces “Neon Rain applied.”
  • Mobile chips are 44px minimum in a 2-up grid; the 3-up desktop grid is pointer-only.
  • Font cost is deferred to the interaction. The miniatures render all six display faces, which fetches all six — but the chips do not exist until the menu opens, so page load is unaffected and the spec's lazy behaviour holds. One user-initiated fetch of ~6 woff2 subsets, once per session. Called out because it is the one place the six-font budget is spent deliberately.

§8 conflicted with itself in two places — both resolved

1 · The fifth chrome column is --fg-faint, not --fg-muted. Read as muted it missed §8.9's 4.5:1 floor in four of six themes — Neon Rain 3.81, Screen Glow 3.36, Moonlit 4.00, Oil and Iron 4.04. Amonkhet settled it: §8.2's #8E8269 is shipped --fg-faint verbatim, while shipped --fg-muted is #A89B84. Confirmed in the R1 audit — §8.2's header is being amended and no hex changed.

2 · Oil and Iron's accent misses the button floor by 0.05. #518179 gives 4.45:1 against its own --bg, and a lighter label is worse, not better — a mid-lightness accent is squeezed from both sides. Lifted to #52827A, ΔE 0.003.

§9.5 — Moonlit needs a floor

Confirmed, with a number. At C 0.0189 the locked accent carries less chroma than the border budget allows (0.04) and sits ΔE 0.021 from its own --fg-muted. A focus ring that cannot be told from muted body text is not an accent.

Floor: C = 0.045 — the minimum that clears every chrome budget. #9AA9A3 → #8AAFA1, ΔE 0.026. Still the least saturated of the six by 2.5×, and still moonlight rather than mint.

One knock-on: the floored sage lands ΔE 0.09 from --success, so Moonlit's success deepens to #679F5F. It is the only theme where a semantic moves for this reason.

§8.5 — the accent area budget, counted

On the recommendations card above, accent paints the fit figure, the three-segment fit bar, one primary button, the availability figure and the focus ring — about 4% of the card's area. The budget is not at risk on data surfaces. It is at risk in exactly one place: the empty-state vignette, a 560×320 radial that covers roughly a third of the viewport at 7–8% alpha. Counted as painted area it is the largest accent region in the app by an order of magnitude.

Ruled: §8.5 is not a CI check. §8.4 makes chroma CI-enforcing; §8.5's “roughly 10%” is an eyes-on heuristic, and painted area is not statically computable anyway. Area × alpha is the model — the vignette costs ~2.5% of the viewport and the densest data surface ~4%. The vignette stays, and the ten empty states are unchanged.

What the mobile pass changes — three things, all small
  • CTAs stack full-width below 480px and grow to a 44px hit box. On desktop they sit in a centred row. This is the only structural difference in the ten, and it is the shipped flex-col md:flex-row pattern from not-found.tsx, not a new one.

§H · audit item B

Metamorphous — the ruling

Correction accepted: font-synthesis-weight: auto is the default, so all 32 call sites render synthetic bold rather than silently flattening. The question is whether that synthetic bold is good enough. Below, Metamorphous 400 against synthetic 700 at the app's four real display sizes, with Cinzel as the control — same sizes, a real weight axis.

Ruling

Metamorphous stays. Synthetic bold goes. Rune Frost pins every display weight to 400 and takes its hierarchy from size and colour, which the app's own scale already provides.

Three reasons, in order of weight:

  • It fails at the sizes the app actually uses most. Read the specimen from the bottom up, not the top. At 30px synthetic bold is merely crude — heavier, blunter, but legible. At 22px it starts losing the crispness that makes the face worth having. At 15px and below the decorative detail fills in and the word becomes a dark bar with a silhouette. That is the damaging range, and it is where most of the 32 sites live: the display scale runs 12 · 15 · 15.5 · 16 · 17 · 18 · 19 · 20 · 21 · 22 before it ever reaches a page title, so the majority of Rune Frost's headings would render in the worst part of this curve.
  • Synthetic bold is a dilation, not a weight. The browser thickens the existing outline uniformly, which is exactly wrong for a face built on high stroke contrast — thin strokes gain the same absolute weight as thick ones, so the contrast that gives Metamorphous its character is the first thing flattened. Cinzel opposite is the control: at 700 the letterforms change shape. Metamorphous at 700 is the same letterforms, thicker. On a face chosen for character, that reads as a rendering fault rather than a style.
  • The app does not need weight to carry hierarchy. Checked against the 32 sites: display headings are differentiated by size almost everywhere — 30/28/24/22/21/19/18/17/16/15 — and the handful of places where 600 and 700 appear at the same size are separated by colour (--fg vs --fg-body) or by container. Nothing in the app is only distinguishable by display weight.

The change is one rule, not 32 edits: [data-theme="kaldheim"] .font-display { font-synthesis-weight: none; }, in @layer base beside the focus ring so a component can still override. No call site changes, no new face, no per-theme type scale — §8.7 holds.

Why not swap the face? Considered and rejected. Metamorphous is the reason Rune Frost reads as Norse at all — it is the only one of the six display faces doing genuinely representational work, and the alternatives with real weight axes (Cinzel, Spectral) are already taken and would make two themes look alike. Losing a weight axis the app never used is cheaper than losing the theme's identity.

One consequence to accept: a Rune Frost heading sits slightly lighter than the same heading in the other five. That is a legibility gain, not a loss — and it is the honest version of what the face actually offers, rather than a browser's impression of a weight that does not exist.
