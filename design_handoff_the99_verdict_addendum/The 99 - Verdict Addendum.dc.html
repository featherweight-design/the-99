The offset is the whole answer to the phone objection. Two sheets in the same rectangle with the same chrome give a reader no way to predict a dismissal. The card sheet sits 44px lower, the receipt's header stays visible above it and steps down to --surface-sunken — you can see the thing you will land on before you dismiss.
Closing the receipt on a tile tap was the alternative, and it throws away the reader's place. Five game changers read one at a time becomes ten taps, and a set the reader had not finished is gone on the first check. A receipt is opened to read a set.
Depth stops at two, hard. Nothing in the card modal opens a third sheet — no related card, no jump to another combo. The Commander Spellbook ↗ link is not a third sheet; it leaves the app. On desktop the receipt stays behind at full size, one ground step down, no offset needed.
5b The mark is a graphical object — 3:1, and the dim rises to 0.65
/Split 0.45 — rank 2 reads 2.30
/Split 0.65 — rank 2 reads 3.36 ✓
Chasing 4.5 was whack-a-mole by construction — lightening rank 1 buys --surface and loses --surface-raised, and the next ground added restarts it. The mark is never the sole carrier of its meaning: Optimized or cEDH is written beside it every time, at full opacity, and that string is what must clear 4.5. So the mark is held to 3:1, and rank 1's 4.4768 passes untouched.
0.45 fails the new gate anyway, so it moves. 0.6 lands at ~3.07 — too thin to spend across six themes and thirteen grounds. 0.65, measured on all thirteen; 0.7 if any miss, and no further. The step still reads as unlit, which was the only thing 0.45 was for.
If 0.7 misses too, the dim is not the tool. The fallback is already in the drawing: the two marks differ in size as well as opacity, so the upper one goes full opacity, one size step down. No third differentiator, and no tint.
