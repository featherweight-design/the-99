The locked rule is exact, so staleness stops being a per-card guess.

R1 derived soldOut from a wall-clock window. schema.ts:87 says a row is live when its as_of equals the newest in the table — a comparison, not a duration. The failure mode inverts, and that is the whole gain: a missed run leaves every row live, so no card says sold out, and staleness becomes one fact about the table instead of eight cards asserting it badly.

So the per-card tags are unchanged. Four tails, same four words. What R1 got wrong was where the fact lives, not what it looks like — and the run-health state it said #291 didn’t expose is drawn below, as one clause on one line.

Read R2-README.md and R2-READINESS.md. Rulings 1–4, the four-tails structure, the no-link-out reasoning and the deck-level denominator are accepted and not reopened. R1’s canvas and documents are untouched; its four stale drawings are recorded in R2-README §7.

Item 1 · blocker 1 — the derivation, corrected
Withdrawn — R1
soldOut = now − row.as_of > cadence + grace
Fails exactly where it must not: a missed run ages every row past the window and eight cards claim to be sold out at once. The locked rule cannot do that.
Locked — schema.ts:87
live    = row.as_of === max(as_of)
soldOut = row exists && !live
absent  = no row
foilOnly= live && cents === null
         && (foilCents ?? etchedCents) !== null
priced  = live && cents !== null
No constant, no grace period, no clock. Every row from one run shares a timestamp, so the comparison is exact. sold out now means exactly what the table says it means: a later run went out and this card wasn’t in it.

The clause, and what it doesn’t do
• The figures stay, and so does the total. The prices are real; they are just old. Suppressing them would be R1’s over-reaction one level up.
• A date, not a duration. Past a week a weekday stops being unambiguous, so the line switches to 12 August at the same threshold that adds the clause.
• Not a warning colour. The line stays --fg-locked. Old prices are not an error and the player didn’t cause them; amber would put the surface’s loudest ink on our operational problem.
• No banner. Unchanged: nothing ranks, filters or sorts on price. This is quieter than a banner and truer than eight sold-out tags.
• Third clause and last. Two is the shipped shape, three the ceiling. A fourth pricing fact needs a different home, not a fourth middot.
