R1
Ordering the deck list · one control, one row, two widths

A tenth child for a row that already wraps.

One control on one surface: an order picker for the deck page's Your deck list, mounted into group-toggle.tsx's existing row. It is FilterPanel in selectionMode="single" with widthPx={260} and desktopRowMinPx={44} — the mount filter-bar.tsx:242-267 already ships, not a fifth menu.

So the menu is not the question. §1 reads the row and answers 4.1; §2 and §3 draw the four variants at 1300 and 390; §4 is the trigger and the panel at rest, open and chosen; §5 answers 4.2–4.5; §6 is the copy block and three defects the mount exposes in a shipped component.

Read at main, not recalled
The brief in full, then decks/group-toggle.tsx, decks/clear-auto.tsx, decks/price-strip.tsx, ui/filter-panel.tsx, ui/view-toggle.tsx, lib/collection/sort-options.ts and lib/copy/collection.ts. Every pixel in every frame below is one of those files' declared value — the pills' paddings, the bulk buttons' mono border, the well's 70×36 and 94×48, the panel's 44px rows and 16px marker, the sheet's 36×4 handle. Nothing here is drawn from the brief's prose where the source had a number.
§1

4.1 — the row is three clusters, and sort joins the third

The brief offers four candidate hosts and rules none. None of them is quite the answer, because all four treat the row as a line of eight peers with two ends. It is not. Read the children by what they say and they fall into three groups — and the third group is the one the reader is in when they want a different order.

The arrangement cluster finally reads as a sentence. By type says what the sections are, Name · A–Z says what happens inside them, the well says how each row is drawn. That is brief §2.5's ruling — sorting happens inside the grouping, which stays — expressed as adjacency rather than as a sentence in a doc nobody reads.

The leading edge is wrong because the pills own it. All 99 is the first thing read and it is the one control that changes which cards exist; a sort trigger in front of it puts an ordering question before the question of what is being ordered, and it separates the two lane pills from the count in the deck's own heading above them.

And trailing-most is wrong because that slot is allowed to vanish. The well is All-only. A trigger positioned against the row's right edge would sit 96px further right under Shopping than under All — the same objection the brief raises against the price strip, arriving from the other direction. Trailing-most stays with the child that disappears.

Its own line is the one candidate this actively buys back. §3 measures 345 of 358px at 390, so the trigger costs no line under either lane — on the condition in the box below, and on 4.3.

The one structural change the placement requires

The three grouping children become one flex-none wrapper, exactly as the auto cluster already is. clear-auto.tsx's own comment states the pattern and the reason: “the cluster is a single child of the toolbar's wrap, so without this the answers would be pushed off the edge rather than under the question.” By type, By role and the well are three loose children today and get away with it at seven. At eight they do not: a free wrap puts the well alone on line three under All, or strands By role — which is the outcome brief §4.1 names as the thing to avoid.

the rule
The row wraps between clusters, never inside one. flex-wrap stays on the row; each cluster is flex-none and nowrap inside.
the hairline
1px × 22px --border-strong, desktop only, in front of the arrangement cluster. A separator, not a child: with nothing to its left, and on any wrapped line, it renders nothing.
not the chassis' hairline
There it divides filters from not a filter. Here it divides acting on the cards from reading them — and sort is on the reading side, so it is inside the rule, not past it.

§4

The trigger and its panel — two or three rows, never six

One row per key, not one per direction. buildSortOptions maps over the keys and hangs the direction on the chosen row as its hint; picking another key adopts that key's own default, picking the chosen key reverses it. So the deck list's panel is two rows under All and three under Shopping — the shortest menu in the app, which is most of why it fits.

§5

§4's five, answered in order

4.1
Where the trigger sits → after By role, before the well, inside a new arrangement cluster
Drawn in full at §2 and §3, argued at §1. None of the four candidates as posed: not the trailing edge (that slot is All-only and would move the trigger 96px between lanes), not the leading edge (the pills answer which cards first), not the strip (absent under All), not its own line (unnecessary — 345 of 358 at 390px). The condition is the one structural change: the three grouping children become one flex-none wrapper, the pattern clear-auto.tsx already uses on the pair beside it.
4.2
The phone form → the labelled 44px pill, not iconOnly
The row gains 110px, not 44, and it has 123 to give. An icon trigger cannot say which order is on, and on this surface that is the control's entire output: the list is always sorted, so there is no off state a glyph could stand for. The chassis' iconOnly mounts sit on a persistent row beside a search field that must keep its width; nothing on this row shrinks, so nothing is bought by shortening this one. And the compact trigger is already a rounded-full pill at min-h-[44px] — it lands in this row's vocabulary with no prop at all.
4.3
Does it state the order → yes, and the Sort: prefix goes — a third answer
The brief offers Sort: Name · A–Z or a bare Sort. Take the order and drop the prefix: the prefix is 38px, and 38px is the difference between the view well staying on the arrangement line and landing alone on a fourth. 345 of 358 with it dropped; 383 of 358 with it kept.
It is also the right word to lose on this row. /collection's prefix earns its width because that trigger sits among Type, Set and Availability, where an unprefixed value would read as a filter value. Here the neighbours are By type and By role — already arrangement statements — and the refresh retired this row's Group caption for exactly this reason: a label naming the category beside controls that say it themselves. One new copy key, triggerOne: (order) => order. The accessible name keeps the word in full — “Sort the deck list, Name · A–Z, collapsed” — so nothing is lost to a reader who cannot see the neighbours.
4.4
What happens to the view toggle → nothing. Neither yields, because neither has to
Both sit on the arrangement line at 390px with 13px spare, and both sit on one desktop line with 55px spare in the widest state that exists. Had one needed to yield it would have been the well, and the reason is worth recording in case the row tightens later: the well is a persisted preference (the99:deck-view, one key for every deck — “I read decks as cards” is a fact about the reader), so it is set once and rarely revisited, while an order is a per-visit question. A control you touch once belongs behind something; a control you touch every visit does not.
4.5
Chassis control or toolbar member → the same component, this toolbar's vocabulary
Chassis decision 11 does not reach here, and the split it implies is the wrong axis. What travels between surfaces is the component and its behaviour — one row per key, direction as re-pick, the round marker, the hint on the chosen row, Escape-dismisses-never-undoes, the pick committing and closing. What is local is the frame, and the shipped component already gets it right without being told: rounded-lg at desktop, where it sits beside the rounded-lg view well; rounded-full at phone, where it sits among pills. The only thing this mount asks the component to stop doing is spend accent it cannot mean — §6. No hairline + trailing slot quotation: that hairline says not a filter, and on this row nothing is a filter but the two lane pills.
§6

The copy, and three defects single-select already has

All three are live on /collection today, not introduced here — each is a branch keyed on selected.length > 0, which under selectionMode="single" is always true and therefore says nothing. This mount is what makes them visible, because this bar has a stated rule about accent.

finding 1 · the permanent accent border
The trigger's selected.length > 0 branch takes border-[--accent]. Fix: condition it on selectionMode === "multi". This improves /collection too — an always-lit border there distinguishes Sort from nothing, and reads as a filter that is on.
finding 2 · the sheet's badge reading 1
The sheet title takes an accent count pill on the same condition, so Sort by wears a permanent 1. Same one-line fix, same collateral improvement. A badge counts a selection the reader made among many; one-of-many has nothing to count.
finding 3 · the trigger is 36 and the well is 38
Accepted, not fixed. The row already mixes 27px pills with a 38px well under items-center; a third height 2px off the well adds no inconsistency a reader can see, and forcing agreement means either a magic padding on this mount or a height prop on a shared component. Recorded so the next person does not read it as a mistake.

Price names its extremes as figures, not as shopping. Lowest / Highest rather than the straw man's Cheapest / Dearest: dearest is British, and this app writes US English (Color identity, en-US pinned through every formatter in the copy layer). It also keeps /collection's pattern of naming the extreme rather than the direction — Fewest / Most, Common first / Mythic first — and beside a strip reading $129.50, highest can only mean the figure.
Which changes why mana value takes Curve up / Curve down. R1's first argument was collision — the shipped Cheapest to cast sitting two rows above a Price · Cheapest. With the money words gone, that argument goes with them, and the curve pair now stands on one thing only: it abbreviates a sentence this same deck already shows the reader — deckVersioningCopy.changed ships the curve goes up and the curve comes down on its own diff. The shipped string is available again and would need a US reverse that is not a coinage; Dearest to cast is the word just retired. Still the one call flagged in READINESS.
what changes from collectionFilterCopy.sort
triggerOne — the one departure. (order) => order, not `Sort: ${order}`. 4.3.
panelAriaLabel — Sort the deck list. The page holds one list, and Sort cards would name the wrong noun on a surface where card also means a tile.
sheetTitle — Sort by, verbatim.

groupDefault, groupMatches, search*, empty*, clear, footer* — written out, unreachable, because FilterPanelCopy is a shared shape.
Persistence, recommended not ruled: the99:deck-sort, one key for every deck, holding both lanes' orders. The trigger now lives in the same cluster as the view well, and the well's own precedent is that how you read a list is a fact about the reader, not about the deck.
§6b

The reference menu, read against §2

A comparable control from another app, shared in review: trigger Price • Desc behind a sort glyph, and a menu of three keys, a divider, then Ascending / Descending as a second group, each group with its own check. It is a good control and it is worth being precise about which parts of it this round can take, because two of them are the two things §2 locks shut.

taken · the trigger's shape
Key, separator, direction — already what R1 draws (Price · Highest), and the reference is the second surface to arrive at it independently. It also confirms decision 7 from the other side: the reference prints no Sort: prefix either. The direction is abbreviated there because Descending is a long word; a named extreme is already short.
blocked · the second group is a direction affordance
§2.2 rules direction is re-pick, and names no new affordance — sort-options.ts:69-73's reasoning is that an asc/desc control “would sit in the filter row doing nothing four times out of five.” The reference's divider-plus-pair is precisely that control, moved inside the menu. Reopenable, but it is a locked decision and a change to /collection as well, since the two share the component.
blocked · and it costs the per-key labels
One direction group for every key means one vocabulary for every key, which is §2.3 reversed: “A–Z says more than ascending for a name and nothing at all for a rarity.” The trade is legible — six labels become two (Asc / Desc), the mana-value wording question disappears entirely, and A–Z goes with it. Hugh's call, not mine: it is two locked decisions and a shared component, so R1 draws the ruled shape and states the alternative rather than quietly taking it.
not taken · the per-row icons and the lit ink
The reference gives every row a glyph and paints the chosen row's label in its accent. filter-panel.tsx carries leadingGlyph and exactly one mount uses it (Availability, whose rows are distinguished by a mark rather than a word). Three rows reading Name, Price, Mana value are distinguished by the words. And the chosen row is already said twice — filled round marker, plus the direction hint no other row has; accent ink would be a third, on the bar that spends none.
