The push line and the upgrade path

Round 4 of 5 · revised against the R1 audit

The push line is a property of a candidate card — drawn on the candidate, wherever a candidate can be committed, never on the analysis tab, which is about the deck you have. Six things did not, and all six are corrected here rather than argued.

1a One atom, three hosts — unchanged by the audit §4.1

The push line fires at the moment of an add, and the moment of an add is a row, not a page. On the analysis tab it would make the player leave the list they are choosing from, and it would put a claim about a card the deck does not hold inside the block whose whole authority is that it describes the deck that it does. So the atom lives on the candidate, in the same slot, on all three surfaces that can commit one: the quick-add row and the top-off row at sentence density, the Consider tile at clause density. /commanders/[slug] is not a fourth: its synergy rows carry a selection into deck creation, so there is no deck and no profile for bracketDelta to evaluate against.

top-off candidate row — the sentence, on the rows that speak

✓ Thassa’s Oracle +58
If you add this
→ Completes Thassa’s Oracle + Demonic Consultation, and your tutors already find the other half.
+ Rhystic Study buy $34.10
The atom never goes on the commit bar. bracketDelta takes one candidate against a fixed profile; a bracket preview for a 27-card batch is a computation nobody has written and a sentence nobody could defend. See 2b for what the pool’s real size does to that.
the three rules that fall out of placement It does not persist after the add (§4.5). The card is in the deck now, and the verdict block is the record. A preview that outlives its subject becomes a claim about a deck that has already changed, and it would be the app’s only self-congratulating sentence. No toast, no afterglow, no badge on the tab. No link to the analysis tab. Tempting, and wrong: the tab still reads the deck without this card, so the link would promise an explanation of something that has not happened. One atom, one sentence. No shape ever prints two, which is what makes the precedence order in 1d a real decision rather than a formatting preference.
1b The shapes — and the two sentences that were false on their edges §3.1 · audit 4, 5

One rule orders the set: a bracket mark is the mark of movement. moves and a counts that moved draw marks on either side of an arrow; a fact draws none and is inked --fact under a label naming what kind of fact it is. Both fact shapes now have two forms, because both had one sentence doing the work of two and each was false on one side of the deck’s own reading. Which form prints is decidable from to and the profile the row already holds — no new data, one branch each.

Two brackets on both sides — the widest the grammar gets, and the reason the sentence names ranges and never a slot. Violation.bracket is not printed as a number: the rule is stated, the numeral stays in the mark.

A range collapsing to one of its own members — the case that breaks any sentence built on from N to M. Core is on both sides and the sentence stays true, because it is about what the deck permits, not where it sits.

1c The two-clause sentence, and the bracket that actually bit §4.4 · audit 6

The limit named must be the limit that bit. Quoting Upgraded’s 3 unconditionally told a deck that just lost Exhibition on its first Game Changer a true fact about the wrong bracket, and a player checking it against the rules would find the app had answered a question they did not ask. The sentence takes the binding bracket and its figure, which engineering can carry, and reads allows none rather than allows 0 when that figure is nought — the phrasing the upgrade path already uses for the same rule.

count, then consequence, then evidence
If you add this That would be your 4th game changer, and Upgraded allows 3.
→ It moves what your cards permit from Upgraded to Optimized or cEDH.
Combo changes Completes Underworld Breach + Brain Freeze.
Two lines, and the count is line one — a limit with a number is the only thing here a player can act on, and delta.ts routes a Game Changer through counts even when it moves precisely because the count is the sentence. The combo evidence is a third line, not a clause: it is what the count cannot explain, so it is marked as a different kind of statement.
corrected — the first game changer, binding bracket 1
If you add this That would be your 1st game changer, and Exhibition allows none.
→ It moves what your cards permit from Exhibition or Core to Core.
One string, two figures from eng. bindingBracket and its limit — the bracket the candidate costs you, which is the lowest bracket in from that the new count breaks. When nothing moves there is still a binding bracket: the lowest one in the deck’s current reading, whose limit the count is approaching. That is the figure a player can act on, and it is the only reading under which the non-moving sentence is worth printing at all.
rejected — one line, both clauses Your 4th game changer of 3 allowed moves you from Upgraded to Optimized or cEDH. Four figures and two claims in one breath, and the phrase 4 of 3 prints a fraction whose denominator a reader will try to satisfy by ticking something off. Two sentences cost one line and read at a glance.
1d The combo that moves nothing — three shapes, and it speaks first §6 · audit 2, 3

Silence is wrong here because the player is about to be surprised by their own deck. A pod asks what is in it; the answer includes a two-card infinite the app watched them assemble without a word. The shape speaks, as a fact and never as a consequence — and the audit found the design failing its own test twice: a third reachable case neither sentence covered, and a precedence order that muted the shape on the canonical add. Both corrected.

2b 262, not 38 — both arguments re-made premise

R1 costed top-off at up to 38 rows, almost all silent — the number of cards a player is adding, not the number the list draws. The list returns the whole ranked pool: 262 for the average commander, 423 at the widest, 480 for a pair. Neither argument that rested on 38 is refuted, and both were resting on the wrong thing.

legibility — the unit is the viewport, not the list
The claim was a push line is rare, so it reads as a flag, and rarity was measured across the list. A reader never meets a list; they meet a screenful. Ten to fourteen rows at a time, of which the marked ones are a handful at most — and that ratio is a property of the rules, not of the pool: roughly forty cards in a colour identity are Game Changers, mass land denial is a few dozen in the format, and a completed combo needs the other half already in the deck. A larger pool makes the ratio better, not worse — the pool grows with the commander’s colour identity, the rule-touching cards grow far more slowly, and the tail of a ranked list is where the unremarkable cards live. What would break the argument is a filter that concentrates them: a Game Changers only view would put the slot on every row, and then it is noise. None exists today; if one is added, the slot should collapse to the clause density on that view.
cost — the unit is the rendered row, not the candidate
The claim was 38 rows, so no fetch per row is fine. The real argument is that bracketDelta is pure and synchronous over data the page already holds — which is true at 480 as at 38, and it is why there is no fetch to begin with. What 480 does change is where it may be called. Eng constraint, and it is the only new one in this round: compute the delta inside the windowed row, not by mapping the pool. An eager map is 480 pure calls on every profile change and every keystroke that refilters; windowed it is the dozen rows on screen. Memoize by (oracleId, profileVersion) so scrolling back is free and an add invalidates everything exactly once. The row does not grow when the atom is absent, which is what keeps a windowed list’s measurement cheap: no reserved height, so the vast majority of rows keep the shipped height and only marked rows are taller.
and what it does to the batch preview
The pool’s real size strengthens the refusal to preview a batch on the commit bar: a running total would have to re-derive a moving profile across a selection drawn from hundreds of candidates, and be recomputed on every tick. It stays the thing to want next, and it stays a sub-project. It also makes the selection count the honest thing to show there, which the commit bar already shows.

Three placements tried and dropped
A preview block on the analysis tab. One sentence about whichever candidate is “current” — and nothing on that tab has a current candidate. It needed a selection model the tab does not have, and it put a claim about a card the deck lacks inside the block whose authority is that it describes the cards the deck has.
A confirm step on the Add. The honest version of a preview — and it taxes every add in the app to warn about the rare one that moves a bracket. The product has no confirm dialogs, and a player topping off does not want twenty-seven of them.
A running total on the top-off commit bar. Attractive — a batch is where a bracket really moves — and uncomputable: bracketDelta takes one candidate against a fixed profile, and a batch preview needs a profile that changes as the batch lands. 2b makes it harder, not easier.
