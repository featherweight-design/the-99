Amendment 5 · Shopping reads availability · #281

Owning one copy is not owning a spare.

Shopping is built from !c.owned and counted as built − ownedCount. Both ask do you own one when the question is have you got one to spare — so a card whose single copy is sleeved into a finished deck is hidden from the shopping list of the deck that also wants it. The availability map has answered the real question, per row, on this very list, since #43.

One state enters, one group holds it, one word marks it, three count strings follow it. No query, no prop threading, no new token, no new component, and no group-header arithmetic moves.

The constraint, as the headline it is owed
Never recompute free from owned − hard. That is the off-decklist case. On a deck's own list, free = (owned − ownClaim) − (hard − ownHardClaim) — Addendum 2 §6, corrected in R5 §1 and promoted to a warning in R5 §6. Read availability.free and hand it to availabilityState(a, { selfExcluded: true }), exactly as decklist-row.tsx:129 already does one element to the left of where this list would draw.

Why the group is last, and why its clause is not arithmetic
from = roleCounts[role] is computed over owned cards only — and a spoken-for card is an owned card in this deck, so it is already inside from. Buying a second copy closes nothing. Drop one into Ramp and from + n over-shoots by one, in a clause the player can check against the role strip on the same screen. The file's own comment names this failure: “the baseline would draw arithmetic the list cannot honour: 5 · closes 9→10”. So the group takes the clause slot the eye already reads for that answer and spends it saying already in the deck.
Sorted alphabetically, not by shortfall
A short 2 is a bigger purchase, not a more urgent one. Every other decklist group sorts by name; ranking this one would invent a priority the marker already states in words.
No second exit drawn, deliberately
Cutting the card is the other way out of the contest, and Addendum 2 §6 already ruled we never warn on a cut: “a cut releases a card — it is the one action that makes a copy freer.” So there is no cut it instead control, no advice line, no --danger text. The ✕ the row already carries is that exit, unlabelled and unhurried.
2 The row ledger what is the same, what is one word ↑ contents

Three things already distinguish this row, and #43 built all three. The ownership dot is solid green — it may not lie, you do own the card. The marker renders in --avail-over-fg behind the doubled glyph. And the row wears the shipped over tint, an inset ring rather than a border so it never adds 2px to one row's height. The loudest row treatment in the app already means exactly this, so the distinction costs no new visual language — and the one thing that does change is the tag word, because the plate is saying “a trip to the shop” and the trip is the same trip.

And the ruling that came out of drawing it: a Shopping row is a nameless host. The marker reads a bare short 1 here, not short 1 — also in Atraxa. The clause's fixed prefix is ~120px and never truncates by contract; a Shopping row also carries the EDHREC figure, the 2nd copy tag and a 44px ✕, so at 390px — and inside a 2-up desktop column — there is no width left for a name that could still read as a name. Rather than invent a truncation the marker's own source forbids (“the floor is what keeps truncates from meaning vanishes”), this surface takes the nameless contract availability-marker.tsx already ships for /collection and every chip. The name is not lost: the All view's row states it, the summary band above names the claiming lists, and markerAria's overAriaCountOnly keeps the accessible name honest — count-only in both halves, which is the rule Addendum 2 §3 set for exactly this trade.

The condition becomes “the Shopping list was showing this card”: the same predicate as the filter.

Why the caption cannot move — and what that costs
54 owned · 8 to buy splits built into two halves that must sum to it. A spoken-for card is owned, so counting it on the right would count it twice: 54 + 10 ≠ 62. So the caption keeps the deck’s arithmetic and the pill takes the list’s size. The honest cost: Shopping 10 now sits a few pixels from 8 to buy and the two do not match. Gap 1, taken knowingly, on the precedent the same file set for basics.
The fill’s intro retires whenever a card is contested
The shipped sentence — “cards we reached past your collection for … every one is closing a specific gap” — is untrue twice of a spoken-for row: nobody reached past the collection for a card that is in it, and the purchase closes no gap. So it is gated on spokenFor === 0 and superseded, never edited. At zero, every string on this surface renders exactly as it ships.
Copies, not rows — everywhere, as today
Every Shopping figure counts copies through cardCopies because they all describe one purchase. The new group and the new clauses follow it. Consequence worth naming: an “any number” card at ×3 makes the header read 3 where the summary band reads 1 card here is spoken for twice. Both follow their surface’s shipped convention. Gap 3.
4 The summary is the index one link, builder only ↑ contents

Not redundant, for three reasons, and the third is decisive. It is the only thing that names which card and why — the rows carry one marker each and no aggregate. The rows sit behind a filter that is not the default, and a summary that merely repeated them would be redundant while a summary that sends the player to them is the reason the tab gets opened. And its other home — the deck card on /decks — has no Shopping list to be redundant with, so it gains nothing and changes in no way.

So: every word it ships stays, and it gains one text link. It flips the decklist’s filter in client state — no ?filter= param, matching GroupToggle’s URL-free locality — and does not scroll: the list is directly below the band, and the pill going active is the receipt. Absent when Shopping is already the active filter, and absent wherever there is no callback, which is what leaves the deck card alone.

6 Rejected shapes, the ledger, the gaps ↑ contents
Four shapes rejected, and what each one costs
Mixed into the role groups. The cheapest-looking answer and the one that breaks the arithmetic: the card is already inside from, so closes 7→10 would over-shoot by one against a role strip on the same screen. The file’s own comment forbids exactly this shape.
A third filter pill — All · Shopping · Spoken for. Promotes a two-card state to a permanent tab, on a toolbar the deck-import handoff already closed at two filters, and splits one shopping trip across two screens. A group inside the list is the same information at a fifth of the cost.
Stay out of Shopping; mark it in the All view only. That is the status quo, and it is what the bug report is. The All view already draws the marker perfectly; the complaint is that the surface named Shopping omits the purchase.
A distinguished row — its own plate, tint depth or a second tag colour. Rejected: the row already carries the loudest treatment in the app for precisely this state. Adding a second severity signal beside the marker would say two different things about one row.

Gap report — eight, and the first two are the ones to argue with
1 · The pill and the spine caption disagree on screen — Shopping 10 above 8 to buy. Both right about different questions; neither can move.
2 · Nothing prices the second copy, so the list cannot say that the duplicate is usually the cheaper trip.
3 · The group counts copies; the band counts cards. An “any number” card makes them differ, and it will look like a bug once.
4 · A row can appear here because a different deck was finished. No announcement, by design. Undrawn.
5 · The 98th-card cliff is inherited — one add elsewhere can promote soft to over and grow this list by one.
6 · A degraded read hides the rows silently; the export tab under-reports until decision 9 lands. The two places a count can lie.
7 · On an over-99 deck this compounds amendment 3’s gap 3: second copies offered for slots that do not exist.
8 · No aggregate says “two of your ten are duplicates” except the footer clause, at 10.5px in the quietest colour on the surface.
