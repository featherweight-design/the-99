# The 99 — the Analysis tab, consolidated (design handoff · R1)

> **Start with `HANDOFF.md`.** This file is the R1 long form: why the tab is arranged this way.
> *Superseded in part* by the touch-ups recorded in `HANDOFF.md` — the trigger, the stale treatment and the sheets changed since; where the two disagree, `HANDOFF.md` wins.

## Why a consolidation

Five rounds each drew their own block, correctly, inside a frame none of them owned. Built together
on staging they stacked edge to edge: an orphaned control at the top, a summary line, a 1240px-wide
bracket box holding a 14px name, the climb, a path panel expanding under it that pushed the
findings off the first screen, a provenance disclosure doing the same, and a mix strip with its own
gutters inside none. Nothing was wrong with any block; the page had no composition.

## The composition

**The first screen answers the question.** At 1300 the top row carries the summary, the bracket
and the climb — what the deck is, what it permits, and what moves it — and the findings start
above the fold. At 390 the bracket is the second thing on the page.

**The bracket box owns its control.** Round 4 put the trigger at the right end of the box's head
row, same position in every state; the shipped panel put it above the tab because the box did not
exist until there was a reading. Now the box always exists. Before a reading it holds the
invitation; while reading, the disabled control; after a failure, the sentence and the retry; after
a reading, the reading. The eye learns one place.

**Disclosures became sheets.** An in-place panel is right when what it reveals is short. The upgrade
path is not — five candidates, target chips, a foot — and in place it read as a second, larger
climb under the first. Provenance is five paragraphs read once, mid-argument. Both belong to the
moment, not the page. The addendum's stack is what makes that safe: a card opened from either sheet
lands on top and dismisses back to it.

**Persistence makes stale real.** Round 4 drew the stale verdict against a model where the profile
lived for one visit. Now it survives a reload, so the common case for a returning player is a
reading from yesterday on a list edited this morning. The design keeps Round 4's answer exactly:
the reading stays, says how old it is, lists what changed, and invites the re-read at full weight.
It never guesses whether the edits mattered.

## What was tried and dropped

- **Summary and bracket as equal halves.** Ruled out by ruling 1, and rightly: the bracket box is a
  narrow instrument, and at half width its pill and chips float.
- **The climb full width under the top row** (the Round 2 canvas). Leaves the two-thirds column one
  sentence tall until sub-project 6. Kept as the stated fallback.
- **The trigger as a sticky footer at 390.** A second place for the one control; Round 4 rule 8 says
  one position.
- **Greying the whole stale reading.** Fails the text floor and says *disabled*, which it is not.
- **A fourth sheet for the ledger.** It is not a reading; see canvas §4.
