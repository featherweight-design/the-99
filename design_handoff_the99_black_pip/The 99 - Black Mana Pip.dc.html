R1
Amendment 1 · the black mana pip · app-wide atom

A black disc on a near-black canvas is a hole, not a swatch.

--b: #1E1B1A is declared identically in all six themes and every theme's chrome sits within a hair of it. Measured against the three grounds a pip actually renders on, black scores 1.01:1 to 1.27:1. There is nothing subtle about that number: the pip is not dim, it is absent, and it has been absent on all 22 reads of it, across 15 component files, since the theme system landed.

The call is a lifted, hued value, once, and no ring. --b becomes #98748F — a dark violet-grey, one hex, six declarations, the same value in every theme, exactly as the other four WUBRG swatches already are. R3 revision: R1 lifted to a neutral #837D78 and the review found it reads as a second white at pip sizes. R2 added violet at #857A93 — and the R2 audit found that a shipped test gates this: contract.test.ts:145 asserts ΔE ≥ 0.1 between every WUBRG pip and every theme's accent, and R2 measured 0.087 against Oil and Iron. R3 pushes the hue on to #98748F, which clears it at 0.115 on both binding themes. It clears 3:1 against the lightest ground a pip lands on and carries the theme's own --bg as its glyph ink at 4.5:1, which is what lets PIP_TEXT die instead of spreading.

Read README.md for the decisions log and the rejected ring, CONTRAST-black-pip.md for the measured ledger across all six themes and every ground, black-pip · copy strings.ts for the two accessible-name additions, READINESS.md for what blocks a green. Drawn on shipped amonkhet tokens unless a frame says otherwise.

The brief's constraint, drawn as written
One solution, every surface, all six themes, a single token change — not per-surface patches. So: no --pip-text, no --pip-ring, no sibling-aware primitive, no per-site override, and PIP_TEXT deleted rather than promoted. The 36-key contract keeps 36 keys and one of them changes value.
R2 → R3 — “the new pip reads almost identical to white”, and the test that gates the fix
The complaint is not about contrast — R1's grey sits 3.35:1 below --w and ΔE 0.34 away from it. It is about which axis carries the difference. Three of the five pips are separated from white by hue; R1 separated black from white by lightness alone, and at 9–12px with no glyph two hueless discs are two pale dots of different brightness. The fix keeps the lightness and adds the hue back — L 0.2115 against R1's 0.2085, so the contrast ledger moves by at most 0.03. How much hue is not a taste question: contract.test.ts:145 asserts ΔE ≥ 0.1 between every WUBRG pip and every theme's accent, iterated over all five pips and all six themes. R2's #857A93 measured 0.087 against Oil and Iron and 0.092 against Rune Frost — two failing themes, and R1's neutral failed the same gate harder at 0.064. R3 is the value that clears it with headroom.

Why violet rather than more darkness. Going darker is the direction the six grounds forbid — that is the whole defect. Going lighter makes it whiter. The only remaining axis is hue, and violet-magenta is the one the palette has spare: it is black mana's conventional second colour, and at hue 335 it is ΔE 0.115 from the nearest of the six accents, against the 0.100 floor contract.test.ts:145 enforces per theme. Two candidates were rejected on that one number and neither could be told apart from the called value at 9px — which is why the row above is the argument and the ledger is the referee. It also fixes the one pair R1 was weakest on: the generic {n} disc, which was 2.20:1 away in Screen Glow on lightness alone and now differs in hue too.

Why not a ring
the ring on a known ground
B Works. The disc is still 1.01:1 — what you are reading is the ring, so the swatch has become a hairline outline of a swatch.
the ring on an unknown ground
Fails, and this is the argument that ends it. A ground-following ring token cannot follow card art, and pips render over art in three places — the commander header, the card modal's blurred wash, the deck card thumb.
A lifted disc has no ground dependency at all. That is the whole reason it is one token change and a ring is two — --pip-ring plus a per-surface assignment of it, which is the per-surface patching the brief rules out. The availability pass's row ring stays exactly where it is: it is a selection ring on a filter control, not legibility plumbing.

Six value edits and one deletion is the whole change. No component file is touched, because not one of the 22 reads was ever wrong — they all read WUBRG_COLOR, which reads var(--b). lightbox.tsx is the one exception and amendment 4 deletes the file outright, so the diff above lands whichever order the two ship in.
E · contract · a11y · what does not move
Token contract
36 keys in, 36 keys out. --b keeps its name, its position in the WUBRG family and its --color-b alias in @theme inline. The chroma gate is unaffected — #98748F measures C 0.059 after 8-bit quantisation, inside a family that already runs to --r's 0.138. The chroma budgets in the theme brief are stated for surfaces (≤0.02), borders (≤0.04) and text (≤0.03) and do not apply to the WUBRG class at all — R1's "achromatic, so the chroma gate is unaffected" was answering a question that was never open. The gate that is open is contract.test.ts's pip/accent ΔE ≥ 0.1, per theme, and it is the constraint that set this value. globals.test.ts and contract.test.ts assert key presence, not value, so neither breaks; if either pins the hex, that is the pin to update.
Accessibility
Pips stay aria-hidden everywhere they are decorative — the lift is a legibility fix, not a semantics change, and the identity is already in text beside every one of them (colorLabel, the Identity meta field, ColorPip's own aria-label). Not colour alone, before or after. Two accessible-name strings are added and both are for amendment 4's modal, not for the pip — see the copy file.
Not moved
--w / --u / --r / --g · Phyrexia's #527030 exception · the generic {n} pip's --border-dashed disc and --fg glyph · every pip size at every call site · ColorPip's 0.40 opacity and its brass selection ring · the availability row ring · commanderGradient / identityGradient, which pick up the new value for free · the suggestion tile's two-colour split fallback.
