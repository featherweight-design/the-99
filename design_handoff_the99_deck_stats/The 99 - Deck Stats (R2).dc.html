DECK STATS R2 the reference idiom, on our four figures R1 →

R1 drew four figures in one strip-sized row. The reference spends real height on two of them and a compact summary bar on the rest — which is the better call, because two of our four are distributions and a distribution in a 148px row is a sparkline.

So R2 splits the block by shape rather than by figure count. A summary bar carries the two scalars — record and cost — in the all-decks strip's own register: ring, small-caps label, metric row. Below it, the two distributions each get the room they need: an axis'd curve chart and six colour cards. The all-decks altitude is that same summary bar with a ring counting decks instead of games.

Four things were borrowed and three were refused. Borrowed: the y-axis with dashed gridlines, the count printed inside the bar, the N spells caption under the chart, and the per-colour card with a percentage inside its own track. Refused: the cost-vs-production pair (we compute no pips and no sources — that is a different app's data model, and drawing the frame without the numbers is how a placeholder ships), the 0–15 axis (CURVE_MAX is 7, so ours ends at 7+ and a 16-column axis would be eight empty columns), and the blurred-art backdrop (our spine already carries the identity wash; a second one behind a chart costs contrast the figures need).

Every ruling in README.md still holds except the two amended in §0 below. Same tokens, same copy module, same loader. PROPERTY · STATES · copy strings.

Amended · percentages are now printed
R1 refused them because the six colour figures sum past the deck. The reference shows the refusal was too broad: each colour's share divides by copies, so each percentage is individually true — it is only their sum that misleads, and the sum is never drawn. So the share prints inside its own track, the count stays beside it as the auditable figure, and the caveat caption still prints unconditionally.
Amended · the block is taller than a strip
R1's 148px desktop row is replaced by ~470px in three bands. It still sits at the foot of the Deck panel, under the list, and it still unmounts with it — but it is no longer pretending to be a strip, which also retires R1's hardest problem: it can no longer be mistaken for the two strips above the tabs.
Held · every arithmetic and vocabulary ruling
Two denominators, never one. No hard 99. Bars scaled to the tallest bucket. 7+ as a ceiling. Lands reported, never a ninth column. No $0.00, no 0–0–0, no avg 0. Accent on To buy alone. Each average names its list. The roll-up carries record and colours.
Not borrowed · cost vs production
It is the reference's best idea and we cannot draw it honestly: it needs pips per colour and mana sources per colour, and stats.ts computes neither — colourSpread counts identity, once per colour, lands included. Flagged as a data request in READINESS, not drawn as an empty frame.

The percentage never sits on the fill, and it never chases it either. The reference puts it inside the coloured run, which works against its mid-tone palette but not against ours: --w is a near-white and --u a mid-blue, so one ink cannot clear 4.5:1 on both. Placing it on the --elevated track gives every percentage the same 13.1:1. It is pinned to the track's right edge at every width rather than trailing the fill's end: a trailing label has to go somewhere at 95%, and a 261px card would clip it. Pinning also puts six percentages on one vertical line, which is what makes the six cards comparable at a glance. 100% is the single exception — no track remains, so it sits inside the fill and the ink flips.

The count is still the auditable figure. 60 cards under the bar is what a player checks against the list; the percentage is what they compare between colours. Printing both is what lets the caveat caption be a footnote rather than a warning.

§3

All decks · the summary strip

the same register as band 1, one altitude up

The reference's all-decks strip is a ring, a small checklist, an identity zone and two metric rows — and the shape is right, because a roll-up is a row of scalars by nature. Ours keeps four zones: the ring counts decks, the record zone sums every deck's, the devotion zone names the colours you actually play, and the fourth zone carries the two figures the reference calls avg. Where we differ from the reference: no avg cost and no avg curve — there is no sumManaCurves, an average of averages is not a mana value, and cost already has the Commitments card. The zone carries the colour roll-up's top three instead, which is the figure a player cannot get anywhere else.

what the ring counts, and the one thing it must not

The ring is a count, not a gauge. The reference's ring is a dashed circle around a number with no arc, and that restraint is the reason it works: an arc would need a denominator, and how many decks you have has none. Ours stays dashed and arcless for the same reason, at both altitudes — 5 decks on Home, 5 games on the deck page.

Ready to play / still brewing is the deck page's own status vocabulary, summed — not a new axis. If the status list grows a third value, this zone grows a third row rather than a percentage.

Three colours, not six, and it says so. Six rows at this altitude is the deck page's cell pasted into a strip; the top three plus your three heaviest and an all six link is the roll-up's job — a shape, then a way in. The caveat travels with it, because the reason the figures overshoot does not change with altitude.

Placement is still the home handoff's call. This strip is specified to sit full-width above Home's two-column body — which is what the reference does — but Home is being reshaped by a brief that is not written, so the mount is theirs.

§4

The absences, redrawn

same rulings as R1, new geometry

A taller block makes absence more conspicuous, not less — which is why the em dash is now paired with the zone's own label rather than floating in a 152px column. The ring is the one new absence: at zero games it keeps its dashes and prints 0, because how many games you have logged genuinely is zero — it is the record that is unknown, not the count. That distinction is the whole reason the ring and the record sit in the same zone.
