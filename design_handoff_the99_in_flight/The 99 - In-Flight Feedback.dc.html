In-flight feedback after the two-lane split · re-placement, not a new surface

The crown is the whole treatment. The three participles retire with the subtitle they lived in.

R2 placed a participle in the clause of the header subtitle that each fetching control owned. #435 deleted that subtitle — page-header.tsx is now a fixed title and gloss, “the scope is now the route, the nav is the switch” — and reduced the controls the ruling governs from three to one. All three participles are homeless, and two of them have no owner left to be homeless for.

The answer is (a): nothing beyond the crown. Not because the wait does not deserve description, but because the thing that made R2's treatment free is gone. A subtitle clause was a line already on the page that was going to change anyway; the participle borrowed a slot the arriving answer would overwrite. Nothing on the rack has that shape. The result line is a count, not a description, and it is the lane's only live region — a participle there is a new element with a live-region collision attached, which is a region loader wearing a sentence.

Read RULING-in-flight-feedback.md for the ruling in full, README.md for the calls and gaps, in-flight · copy strings.ts — which now exports nothing. Drawn on the build in design_handoff_the99_commanders_discover/, which is what this must match.

What R3 changes, in five lines
1 · counting…, re-ranking… and re-scoring… are retired. Delete the strings; do not re-home them.
2 · The result line holds its outgoing sentence, unchanged, for the whole flip. No pending form, no dash, no blank, no aria-busy.
3 · The 500 ms gate is retired — it gated the participle, and the crown was never gated. This is the one place R3 disagrees with the gap report's keep-list.
4 · The ruling is discover-only. The owned lane has zero fetching controls, so nothing on it ever pends.
5 · Everything non-placement in R2 stands: list untouched, no mark on client controls, nothing announced mid-flight, no escalation tier, LoadingScreen is the region loader.

Why the 500 ms gate retires with the participle
The gate existed for one purpose: to stop the subtitle participle appearing and leaving inside a 270 ms flip, because an element that flashes reads as a fault. With no participle there is nothing to gate.
The crown was never gated, in R2 or in the build — select.tsx renders it the moment pending is true. That is correct and stays: a press must be acknowledged on the press, and a 500 ms silence after a tap is the exact complaint the crown was built to answer.
A crown that flashes for 200 ms is not the same failure as a sentence that flashes. It is a label swapping to a mark and back inside its own box — no reflow, nothing to read, and it says the one thing it exists to say: the press registered.
Flagged: the gap report's keep-list carries “one gate, 500 ms, never tuned per control” forward. R3 disagrees on that one line only. Keeping PENDING_GATE_MS would leave a constant no code path reads, which is how a retired rule gets re-implemented a year later.

E · rejected, drawn once — the three places the participle could have gone
(b) the result line gains a pending form
the option the brief asked to be ruled on
re-ranking… 3,301 commanders · re-ranking… — of 3,301 commanders
Three ways to lose. Replace the sentence and you delete a true count to report a wait. Append and you get the fourth-clause reflow R2 spent a page refusing, on a line whose height the rack promises not to change. Dash it and you break the locked rule that missing data is omitted, never dashed — and the data is not missing.
And the collision the brief names is real. That line is the rack's only live region, debounced 300 ms and aria-atomic so a reader hears one whole sentence. A participle in it either announces mid-flight — against ruling 5, and 300 ms after a press that already spoke — or has to be surgically hidden from the one node whose entire job is to be heard. Muting a live region to say something that carries no information is the argument against saying it.
(c) a fourth rack row carries it
controls · chips · result · …and a status line
3,301 commanders re-ranking…
The rack is three rows, always all three, and it does not change height when the results change — a locked invariant. A row that exists only while waiting violates it in the most visible way available: by pushing the list down at the exact moment the reader is watching it change.
Reserving the height instead spends a permanent empty row on a state that is usually skipped entirely by the cache. At that point it is a region loader with a smaller font, and ruling 7 already answered it: the region loader exists, it is LoadingScreen, and it is for when there are no outgoing rows.

Something new▾ 0–500 ms: pressed, and the control says nothing at all
This is the shipped behaviour inverted. A gated crown means half a second of a control that looks untouched after a deliberate press — which is the complaint #338 and PRs #411/#416 were written to answer.
A gate is right for a sentence, which has to be read to be worth anything and costs a reflow to appear. It is wrong for a mark inside a fixed box, which costs nothing and answers a question the reader asked with their finger.
F · the contract
One control, one flag, sometimes. Discover's scope, on a cache miss. Everything else on both lanes is client-side and gets no mark — unchanged from R2's ruling 4.
The crown, ungated. Three diamonds 8 / 11 / 8 px at rotate(45deg), 5 px gap, 28 px box, currentColor. It takes the chevron's slot on the trigger and the selected option's label in the sheet, so neither resizes. Control at 0.55, aria-busy, never disabled.
The list is never touched. No skeleton, dim, overlay, aria-busy or pointer trap on the region. The outgoing rows are the previous answer and stay pressable. Unchanged.
The result line holds. Its outgoing sentence, verbatim, for the whole flip — and the sheet footer's copy of it too. It rewrites once, on arrival. No pending form, no aria-busy, no second node.
Nothing announced mid-flight. The crown is aria-hidden and the control's own name carries the state. On settle the lane announcer speaks the re-rank once; the result line speaks only for client narrowings, on its 300 ms debounce.
No escalation, at any duration. No spinner, no progress bar, no "taking a moment". Unchanged from R2 — and now stronger, since the slow case is a first visit to a scope, which is also the case with the most to arrive.
Failure is the route's. The error path owns the page; the rack returns to the state it was in, count included. The result line is not a status bar.
The boundary is unchanged. Outgoing rows → in place. No outgoing rows (first paint, reload, cold shared URL) → LoadingScreen, still the only skeleton. No third component.
Motion: one thing moves. The crown's 1,200 ms opacity pulse, 0.34 → 1. prefers-reduced-motion holds it static at 0.7 and changes nothing else, because nothing else was ever motion.
Focus survives. Never disabled, so the pressed control keeps focus and its ring throughout, and the trigger still opens its menu while the request is out. No focus move on arrival.

Two corrections, folded in
1 · “two participles under I own”
Was already one before the split — commanders-view.tsx never passed an availability pending flag, which R2's own ruling 4 said in prose two pages earlier. The sentence contradicted the ruling above it. Post-#435 it is zero, and frame D draws that.
2 · ordering was never optional
R2's reference assembler typed it optional and ran .filter(Boolean), which taken literally deleted ranked by fit from the owned subtitle rather than holding it. The true statement is narrower and survives the subtitle it was written about: the owned lane has no sort control, so that clause could never go pending — which is not the same as not existing.
Both are statements about ownership, not about the subtitle, which is why they outlive it. Their shared shape is the R3 ruling in one line: a control that does not reach the server has no state to report, and drawing one for it invents a wait.
