CARD DETAIL R2
price · zoom · flip — an amendment to four ratified documents

The art column is the one region that exists for every card. So that is where the price lives.

Three moves, one placement rule between them. The price is a readout pinned under the sharp art — not beside the printing region, which an unowned card does not have, and not in the pinned bottom row, which belongs to the link-out. Zoom is an overlay inside the same dialog, scoped to the modal's own rectangle at desktop and to the sheet at phone — a layer, never a mode, never a second dialog. And the flip turns only inside the zoom: outside it the modal's art keeps the swap card surfaces ratified, so this handoff spends its one unit of motion where the card is big enough to be worth turning.

Everything below is drawn on the shipped geometry: 820×620 / max-h 85vh at desktop, the full-bleed sheet with its 180px art header at phone. No token, no component and no geometry is added. The modal does not resize, on any of the three moves — which is the constraint the printing picker's conflict table already fought for, and the reason zoom overlays rather than pushes.

Read README.md for the four rulings and the four amendments, STATES-card-detail.md for the matrix (four price states × zoom open/closed × both faces, both widths), MOTION-no-token-set.md for the literal durations and every reduced-motion static state, card-detail · copy strings. Governing and amended: printing picker R3, full card R1, card surfaces R2, link-out R3/R4. Untouched: availability R5.

Trap 1 · the unowned card has no printing region
A card you do not own renders no picker, no You own figure and, at one printing, no disclosure — so any placement anchored to the printing region has no anchor in the state where a price matters most. The art column is the only region every card has. That is the whole argument for the placement, and it is why the two price states solve as one.
Trap 2 · the availability block stays
A price is not availability, and neither absorbs the other. The block says what your shelf can spare (none free — in Gonti Theft); the tag says what the store wants. They sit in different columns and never trade places. Shipped AvailabilityDetail is untouched by this pass — R5 is not reopened.
The constraint · there is no motion token set
Durations are literals in globals.css:524-536, so this handoff quotes existing literals rather than inventing values — and every animation ships its own static state, not a global disable. Two animations, two statics, both drawn in §5.
§1

Where the price sits

Directly under the sharp art, at both widths, on both the owned and the unowned card. It is the deck-side buy tag verbatim — mono 10px --accent on 1px solid --accent-dim, radius 4 — so no new vocabulary enters the modal. At desktop it sits on flat --bg and needs no plate; at phone it sits over the wash and takes the printing picker's badge plate (rgba(10,8,6,.72)), which is the only difference between the two.

The ruling, and why it splits
The tag survives; the mark does not; the zoom does. The flip control is withheld at 72px because it is a control that must be seen to be pressed — a glyph plate is the only way in, and at that size the plate eats the art. Zoom has never needed its glyph. The art is a button on the builder spine and the desktop commander art with no mark at all; the ⤢ is a courtesy at full size, not the affordance. So the courtesy is dropped and the gesture is kept — and it is precisely here, at 72px, that a player most wants the card big.
Three consequences to build
  • The thumb needs a visible focus ring — the shipped --accent ring at 2px. With no glyph it is the only standing evidence the thumb is pressable.
  • The zoom that opens is the full one, 318×444 with its caption and the shipped flip plate at a 44px target. Collapsing the header does not collapse the zoom — that is the point of keeping it.
  • The tag still re-reads on every pick, one row up from the tile it belongs to. Both figures are then on screen at once, which is the picker's whole job.
Rejected
Zoom unavailable while the picker is open. It is the tidy answer — symmetric with the withheld flip control, one less state — and it is wrong: it takes the gesture away at the one moment the art is too small to read, and it would make zoom the only thing in the modal whose availability depends on another region being closed. Withholding a mark is a visual decision; withholding a gesture is a functional one.
§2

The four price states — and the line between a store's no and our silence

Copy is ratified and this pass adds no string: silence is the state. Never an em dash, never $0.00, never a greyed box holding the slot. The slot is not held — nothing above the tag moves when it is absent, because the tag is the last thing in the column. But silence is narrower than “no figure”: it is the state where nothing reached us, and a store that answered no is not silent. See the ruling below the four.

The ruling — the line is who was silent, not what the answer was
The tag renders no stock when the store answered and the answer was no. It renders nothing when nothing reached us. That is the whole rule, and it makes both ancestors right rather than picking one: set-cell.tsx always has a store answer for a card in a set it is showing, so it always has a word to print; the buy tag's ratified silence is the no-answer case, which is ours to own and not the store's to be blamed for. So sold-out and never-stocked collapse into one word — the player's next action is identical, the store is one tap away, and a second word would need a field the feed does not carry — while never-stocked is emphatically not silence. R1 had them on the wrong sides of the line in the drawing; the prose was right. no stock keeps distinguishing the two facts in its accessible name only, exactly as set-cell does.
§3

Zoom is a layer inside the dialog, at both widths

Overlay, not replace — on phone and desktop, for the same reason: the printing picker's decision 4 forbids modal modes, and replacing the panel is a mode by another name (it needs a back control, a title that changes, and a second definition of what ✕ closes). The layer is scoped to the modal's own rectangle, never the viewport: darkening the page would make this a third dialog and would orphan the backdrop's close gesture, which the full-card handoff's decision 4 kept deliberately.

What the zoom holds
  • The whole card, at the largest 63/88 the rect allows.
  • One mono caption: set · code · collector number — so you know which printing you are holding.
  • The close ✕, in the modal's own slot, at its shipped size.
  • The flip control — the shipped corner plate, unchanged, at a 44px target. Only on a two-faced card.
And what it refuses
  • The price. A card you are holding has no price printed on it. The tag is behind the layer, one dismissal away.
  • Prev/Next. Paging inside a zoom makes it a browser; the zoom is a look at one card.
  • Oracle text, meta, the picker. All of it is on the card or one dismissal behind it.
  • Pinch-zoom past 1×. Not a viewer; the printing picker is where art is compared.
Dismissal · three doors, one stage
Tap the card, tap the layer, or Esc. The zoom takes the first Esc and the dialog keeps the second — the same two-stage shape the printing region already ships, so the count of stages does not grow. Focus returns to the art.
§4

The flip — Y axis, 320ms, and only in here

Rotation about the vertical axis, because that is how a hand turns a card over; X would tumble it end over end, which no player does. 320ms on cubic-bezier(.2,.7,.2,1), face swapped at the midpoint, perspective: 1400px. 320 sits above the disclosure's 160 (this is an object moving, not a box opening) and below half a second, so a second press never queues behind the first.

The amendment, stated narrowly
Card surfaces ratified zero net motion and a modal flip that swaps. R1 amends that in exactly one place: inside the zoomed view the flip turns; everywhere else it still swaps. The 180px phone art and the 264px desktop art keep the ratified instant swap, the hover preview keeps its 240/80ms appear, and the row keeps its // mark. One animation is added to the app by this handoff, and it is here.
§5

There is no motion token set, so there is no global switch to inherit. Each animation names its own reduced-motion end state, and neither is a disabled feature — the zoom still opens and the card still turns over. Only the transit is removed.
