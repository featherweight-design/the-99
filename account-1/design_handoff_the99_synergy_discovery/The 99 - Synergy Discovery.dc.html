R1
Synergy discovery, and the last 31 · track 2 · stage 1

Two figures, and only one of them is the ranking.

The EDHREC Synergy panel stops being an ownership audit and becomes a synergy ranking of ~260 real cards — one column of 44px rows at both widths, creatures led, every row a control, every row selectable into the build. On /decks/[id] the same ranked list becomes a Consider shelf and the path from 68 to 99: an owned-only fill, an explicit buying top-up, and the shipped sweep, held apart on purpose.

Synergy is signed points in brass. Inclusion is an unsigned percentage in neutral. Different units, different colours, different columns, and only the brass one sorts the list. That is the whole §5 constraint, and it is the one thing in here that must not be drawn away.

Read README.md for the decisions log, DENSITY-synergy-panel.md for the §7.1 density call, STATES-fill-controls.md for the §7.5 matrix, synergy-discovery · copy strings.ts for production copy. Not gated — §0's ingest has landed; what is missing is a read path.

§7.1 Density at ~260
One column of 44px rows, both widths. Not tiles — the data contract has no image. Not two columns — a rank order read in columns is not a rank order. Plain overflow.
§7.2 The ladder
One facet strip, one disclosure. Types are a strip defaulting to Creatures; depth is Show all N. Two mechanisms, not a ladder of reveals.
§7.3 Ownership
Filled dot vs. hollow ring — both already on this panel today. The dashed border does not survive 258 rows, and no ownership filter: that is the audit ruling 1 deleted.
§7.4 Select into build
A 34px + → ✓ toggle at the row's end. The locked promise strings stay as the zero-selection copy; a third string branches when selection is non-empty.
§7.5 The three controls
They never sit side by side. The two adds live in a last-31 strip under the provenance line; the sweep stays where it ships, in the toolbar. Brass outline > neutral ghost > a whole row away.
§7.6 Short of 99
The owned fill never promises 99 — its label states what it can place. Stopping short reuses the shipped provenanceShort clause verbatim, and the top-up is the way forward.

Ruling 3 — the exclusion, drawn as a rule not a filter
excluded Owned cards Role Coverage already draws for ramp, draw, removal or wipes. Sol Ring, Arcane Signet, Swords to Plowshares. They are on the page, forty rows up.
stays Unowned role staples — Mana Crypt above is ramp, unowned, and drawn. Nothing else on this page shows it, and ruling 3 rejects excluding the four roles outright for exactly that reason.
stays Everything that is not one of those four roles, owned or not — creatures, enchantments, lands, the whole point of the panel.
The exclusion is silent. No “12 hidden” note, no toggle to show them. A player cannot act on the fact that a card is drawn elsewhere on the same page, and a count of hidden things invites a control to reveal them — which is the ownership audit coming back through the side door. The facet counts are post-exclusion, so 66 is the number of creature rows that exist, and the headline of top 258 is the number EDHREC associates — those two disagree by design, and the headline is the one ruling 1 locked.

Creatures are never excluded in practice: classifyRoles can class a creature as ramp or draw, so a few do drop out of the default facet. That is the rule working, not an edge case — and it is the one place the default facet count moves with the player's collection.
Absent & degraded — the panel's two null states
⚠ No synergy data — EDHREC doesn’t track this commander, so there is nothing to rank. The panel is not drawn.
⚠ Synergy data unavailable — we couldn’t reach EDHREC. Try again
Both are shipped strings and shipped shapes, unchanged. What changes is the consequence: today an outage hides two chip lists; now it hides the panel's ordering principle, so there is no degraded rendering to fall back to. Absent gets no retry, degraded gets one — FitHero's distinction, held.

A per-row null is different and does not trigger either. One card with no synergy figure among 257 that have one is an em dash, which is legible because its neighbours give it meaning — the recommendations handoff's rule, and it applies here to each figure independently.

How ruling 6 and the copy tension are both satisfied
§7.4 names the tension: the button promises “We’ll fill 68 of the 99”, which stops being true the moment something is selected. Ruling 6 locks stickyPromise and seedNote.

Both hold, because the two locked strings are the zero-selection copy and the new one is a branch. Nothing is edited, nothing is deleted, and the default state of the page is unchanged — which is what ruling 6 is protecting. Two new keys, one per width.

The button label never changes and never takes a count. The promise line under it holds the arithmetic; a badge on the face would print the same number twice, and the label is the one thing on this page a player has already learned.

Selecting an unowned card is allowed, and it says so. The row already told you (hollow ring); the promise line names the count in --warning. Ruling 5 requires that buying be explicit, not that it be impossible — and a selection the player made by hand, card by card, with the ownership mark on every row, is the most explicit buying in the product.
Selection lifetime — no tray, so: page state
Ruling 4 forbids a tray that outlives the page. So selection is client state on /commanders/[slug] and nothing else:

· Cleared by navigating to another commander, by Clear selection, and by the build itself.
· Not restored by back-navigation. A restored selection the player does not remember making is worse than an empty one they can redo in four taps.
· Survives facet changes and expansion — a card selected under Creatures is still selected when the facet is All, and the toggle shows it. This is the one thing the facet strip must not reset.
· Not capped. A player who selects 40 cards gets a deck at 99 and no room for the fill — which is a legitimate way to use this panel, and the promise line reports it honestly (plus your 40 — 99 of the 99). Past 99 the count clamps and the line says so; that is the only refusal.

§7.5 — why the three controls are not side by side
Two of them add cards and one removes 68 of them. Put in one toolbar they are three ghost buttons of related shape, and one of them is the undo of the other two.

So they live in two different rows, separated by a rule. The two adds sit in a last-31 strip directly under the provenance line — because they are about the open slots, which is exactly what that line just told you about. The sweep stays exactly where it ships, right-aligned in the decklist toolbar beside the All / Shopping filter. Nothing moves that already shipped.

Three weights, in order: brass outline (add, owned, the main action) → neutral ghost with a --warning count (add, buying) → neutral ghost, a rule and a whole row away (remove).

Rejected: filling the owned control brass-solid. It would out-shout Build this deck, the only solid brass button on the previous screen, and make reaching 99 look like the app’s goal rather than the player’s. Ruling 6 says the opposite: 99 is opt-in.

Rejected: a segmented control with owned / buy as two halves. It reads as a filter, and the second half spends money.

A11y — two targets per row, and both figures spoken
· The row's name is a CardDetailTrigger; its accessible name carries both figures and the ownership, because all three are aria-hidden visual marks: “Murmuring Mystic. Owned. Synergy plus 47 points. In 31% of decks.”
· The toggle is a separate button, aria-pressed, named “Add Murmuring Mystic to the build” / “Remove … from the build”. 34px at desktop, 44px at phone.
· Ownership is not colour alone: filled vs. hollow is a shape, the count is in the headline, and the word is in the accessible name.
· The two figures are not colour alone either — one is signed and in points, the other unsigned and a percentage, in fixed columns under a header row.
· The facet strip is a radiogroup, not tabs: it filters one list rather than swapping panels, and arrow keys must not change the facet while a selection exists without announcing the new count.
· The disclosure keeps aria-expanded / aria-controls and does not steal focus — CoverageDisclosure's shipped contract.
· Selection count changes announce on the existing live region, debounced — one message per settle, never one per toggle. Twelve fast taps is one announcement.
· Focus ring is the global :focus-visible, unmodified, on both the row and the toggle.
Motion, and the list of things not added
No motion class is added. Selecting a row is an instant state change — a 258-row list where rows animate on toggle is a list that flinches. Expansion is a height change with no transition, per the shipped disclosure. The crab and the kaiju do not appear on either surface: these are working surfaces, and the richness pass says working surfaces stay quiet.

Not added: no new panel, no new modal, no new empty state, no tray, no confirm step, no toast, no new tag, no ownership filter, no price, no derived score, no virtualisation, no per-row bar on surface A, no second figure column on the phone, no count of hidden cards.

One shipped construct is deliberately reused against its current condition — the tile's lift line, ungated from ownership. That is the only behavioural change to an existing component, and it closes an already-open flag.
