1 · the marker's place on the tile

Anatomy. An 18px circle, centred in the chips row (22 / 24), 8px after the chip. 1px hairline of its ink at 70%, a 14% tint mixed into --surface-inset (the H5 treatment the live tag uses), and the "!" in IBM Plex Mono 600 12px in the ink. The glyph is a graphic, so its bar is 3:1: measured at 3.5:1. A circle, where every chip and pill is radius 6, so it reads as a mark rather than a fourth chip.

One glyph, two inks. Red for a card not legal (Hugh's ruling). --fact for unread data notices: red on news that a list changed would say the deck is broken when it is not. Both kinds on one deck draw one red mark, never two: the more serious face wins, as it did with the tag. The tooltip and the spoken name carry both counts.

The shipped FactTooltip, with sentence: fine pointers only, 400ms open, above by default and flipped when there is no room, portalled, --elevated bubble at 11.5px, aria-hidden. The existing MarkKey, one new mount. The first full-width item of the tile list (col-span-full in the three-up grid, as CollectionMarkKey does), above the first tile. The real mark at its real size beside a word, mono 10 / 10.5 --fg-faint, group label Deck marks. Drawn at 1300 too: the key is the gloss and the tooltip is extra, which is the rule the card marks shipped under.

Conditional, like buy and proxy. Each entry is drawn only when a tile in the current shelf and filter draws that mark, and the line is absent when none does. A key for a mark nobody can find is clutter, and "not legal" printed over a shelf of legal decks would be false. Why not tap-for-tooltip: the tile is one link, and a tap that opens a bubble instead of the deck is a control nobody asked for. The deck header, one tap away, spells the notice out in full.
