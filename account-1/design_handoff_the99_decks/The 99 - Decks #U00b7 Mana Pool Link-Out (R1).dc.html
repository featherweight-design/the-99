Decks · #330 · the Mana Pool link-out

The app’s first outbound link is a primitive, not two placements.

grep -r 'target="_blank"' src/ returns zero hits, so whatever this pass draws becomes the convention every future link-out inherits. It is therefore drawn once — ink, glyph, states, label grammar, a11y contract — and then placed twice under one structural rule: the outbound link is the last thing in its container, below a hairline.

That rule answers both open questions at once. In the export dialog it makes “quieter than the two local actions” structural rather than a colour choice; in the card modal — which has no actions row today — it says where the row goes without inventing one.

Read LINKOUT-R1-README.md and LINKOUT-R1-READINESS.md. The price addendum’s rulings are inherited, not reopened: the buy tag is never a target, the price strip is the Shopping filter’s, exports copy names only. Colours resolve against amonkhet except in item 5; no hex here is normative.

Before the audit — what this pass does not cite

docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-05-manapool-checkout-design.md is not in this project. docs/superpowers/ contains one file, design-tables/appshell.md. Neither is src/components/cards/card-modal.tsx — src/ here holds components/shell/nav.tsx alone.

So no claim below is sourced to §4 or §7, and no endpoint-probed fact is repeated as if I had read it. That is deliberate: R1 and R2 of the price addendum both went amber for citing a string I had not verified, and inventing a URL contract would be the same failure in a worse place. The modal’s anatomy is taken from the drawn record — PARITY-lightbox-to-modal.md and the Full Card canvas — and everything the missing spec owns is carried, listed, and marked. Three carried items, all in §6. Nothing in the visual system depends on them.

1a · The underline is not decoration — it is the whole signal
In this app brass tint already means five other things: the price tail, buy, 6 printings you own ▾, Rare, ×1. Tinted text is the app’s ambient state, so tint cannot also mean “this navigates.” The underline is the only mark not already spent — nothing else in the shipped surfaces carries one.
So the convention reads: tint alone is a fact; tint plus an underline leaves the app. That is one sentence, it is enforceable in review, and it generalises to every link-out after this one.
1b · Glyph: yes, and named destination: yes
• The glyph stays. A new tab that opens without warning is the one navigation players cannot undo with Back. ↗ costs six pixels and is the standing convention for it — and it is aria-hidden with real hidden text behind it, because a glyph is not an announcement.
• “Mana Pool” is named, always. This is the only control that hands the player to another company; a label that hid that (Buy these cards) would be the app spending trust it has not asked for. Naming it also makes the price strip’s prices from ManaPool provenance line and this link visibly the same relationship.
• Label grammar: Buy … on Mana Pool ↗, where the middle names the scope and nothing else. Deck: these. Card: nothing.
1c · :visited is not styled — ruled, not skipped
A visited state on a store link makes the browser’s purchase history visible on the player’s own screen — on a shared screen, on a stream, in a screenshot of a decklist. The app does not own that fact and should not render it.
It would also be wrong on its face: the state is per-URL, so one visited card in a list of eight would read as done when the player only looked. The link states a fact about the card, not about the player.

The hairline is where the app ends. Above it, two actions that keep the player here and one note about them. Below it, one link that does not. The hierarchy is then read off the structure rather than off a colour, which is the only version of “quieter” that survives six themes and a reviewer who disagrees about brass.
Why a second row and not a third slot
The footer already carries two buttons and export.pricingNote at 560px. A fourth item in that row either wraps or forces the note off the line — and the note is a live string the price addendum just ratified across two call sites. Adding a row costs 45px; editing a ratified layout costs an argument.
It is also the placement that lets the phone sheet be identical rather than analogous: there the local action is one full-width button, so a trailing slot does not exist at all, and a second row does.
What the link does not change
The dialog’s tabs, the list block, both local buttons, the note, the est. total row and the info callout are byte-identical to shipped. export.pricingNote keeps its ratified wording: the exports still copy names only, and the link does not make that false — it does not export anything.
Decklist tab — rejected, not deferred
No link on the Decklist tab, and no slot held for one. A 62-card basket including the 54 the player already owns is not a thing anyone wants bought, and a link that silently means the 8 while the tab says 62 is a mislabel. The Shopping tab is the only tab where a basket is the unit — the same reason the tab exists.

One state, as briefed. The link is below the printing region, not inside it: the picker is a disclosure that stays here, the link is the one thing that does not, and the hairline is the boundary between those two facts.
Why the last position, and not the meta grid
The grid’s four cells are facts about the card, each a label over a value. An action in a fact grid has no label that fits and no cell that means do this. Putting it last, under a hairline, gives the modal the actions row it did not have — with exactly one action in it, which is why no row had to be invented.
And it is the same position as the export dialog’s. Two surfaces, one rule, and the next link-out inherits a place to go rather than a precedent to argue about.
Three routes, one modal — nothing route-dependent
deck builder  → same link, same place
/collection    → same link, same place
commander pg → same link, same place
The link takes the printing on screen and nothing about the caller. No deck context, no owned/unowned branch, no from= in the label. If a route ever needed a different link, that would be a different component — the parity table’s whole point is that this modal is the one card surface.
Owned cards get it too — and that is the interesting half
A player who owns one copy is the player most likely to want a second, a foil, or a different printing. Branching on ownership would hide the link from exactly them, and would make the modal’s content depend on the collection — which the availability block already reports and does not gate.
Desktop: identical, in the right column
The desktop modal’s content column ends with the Set / Rarity / Identity / You own field row; the hairline and the link follow it, left-aligned, 14px, unchanged. No size step at desktop — the primitive has one size, because a link that grows with its container starts competing with buttons that do not.

The buy tag — still never a link
buy·$45.00
9px · 92% scrim

The shipped price addendum’s ruling stands, and this pass is the reason it was right: the primitive above requires a 2px focus ring at 2px offset, and on a 9px tag inset 6px into card art there is nowhere for one to sit — over the 92% scrim it would ring the scrim, not the text.
Which gives the convention its first negative rule, and it is a useful one: a link-out goes only where its focus ring fits. That is a measurable test the next ticket can apply without relitigating the tag.

Ratios computed from the theme table’s own hexes against each theme’s --surface. Recompute in CI rather than trusting these; the ranking is the load-bearing part, not the second decimal.
One rule, not two exceptions
--accent is the obvious ink and it is the wrong one: phyrexia lands at 4.3 and kaldheim at 4.5 for 14px text. The tempting fix is a per-theme override for those two, which is the shape this handoff has rejected twice — a primitive that looks different in some themes is six primitives.
So the link’s rest ink is the lighter step that already exists in every theme. One token, six themes, AA clear in all six, and no new value added to the table.
Then hover goes to --fg, which is better anyway
Spending --accent-hover on rest leaves hover nowhere to go inside the accent ramp — so hover leaves it: --fg, the palette’s brightest ink, with the underline at full opacity. It brightens in all six, it is the strongest contrast available, and it makes hover the one moment the link stops being brass — which is exactly the moment the player is about to leave.
The ring keeps --accent
A 2px ring is a graphic, not text: 3:1 is the bar and all six clear it comfortably. Keeping the ring on --accent also matches every other focus ring in the app, so the link’s focus state is the app’s, not its own.
