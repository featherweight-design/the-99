Addendum
Decks · #291 · price on the buy tag

One tag, four tails, no new marks.

The buy tag keeps its word, its amber and its place at both densities. What changes is what follows the middot — a figure, a figure and a word, a word, or nothing — and the fourth of those is the tag we already ship.

The ingest’s four states are four tails on one atom, in availability-marker’s logic shape rather than a second vocabulary for “you can’t have this”. There the axis was severity and silence meant free; here the axis is how much we can say about buying it and silence means we have never had it.

Read ADDENDUM-README.md for the four rulings and ADDENDUM-READINESS.md for the verdict. The shipped handoff and its reviewer notes are unedited; the add-panel addendum’s §7 ruling — the tile borrows this row’s tag, same hex, same word — is inherited, not revisited.

1 · Where the number lives
Inside the tag, both densities. Price is the consequence of unownedness; the tag is where unownedness is declared.
2 · Does it link out
No. 9px, over card art, pointer-events: none. A first link-out belongs to the export dialog, not to a chip.
3 · Deck-level total
Yes, on the Shopping header only — and it carries its own denominator, because it cannot sum all four states.
4 · Staleness
Three levels, one date. Never in the tag; once on the header; always in the accessible name.
Absence is absence
Never stocked renders nothing after the word. No dash, no zero, no skeleton.
Sold out keeps buy
buy is the ownership fact and stays true when the store is out. The tail says what you can do today.
Locked upstream — the whole design problem, not a detail
Absent from the price table = never stocked at ManaPool, not free and not unknown · null non-foil with a set foil price = stocked in foil only · the ingest never deletes, so a stale timestamp = sold out, present in the table but not buyable now · whole cents, one store, refreshed weekly, never rounded. Four distinct states; the shipped tag draws one.

Four marks or fewer — the answer
Zero new marks. No glyph, no hue, no token. Four presentations of one tag, told apart by a figure, a word, or the absence of one — so the file greyscales and all four survive.
One size rule, borrowed
Row figures take --fg-body; tile figures take --fg. Same reason --avail-hard-fg exists — 9px mono on a scrim over card art needs the brighter ink. A size decision, not a hue one.
Magnitude is never a colour
Inherited verbatim from the discovery pass: a price in --warning is the product having an opinion about the player’s money. The amber belongs to buy, never to the number. Past three figures the figure brightens one step and nothing else.

Why the slot is off here
This list is mostly owned rows with no tag at all, so there is no column to form — and a padded tag with nothing above or below it is a phantom column. Under Shopping every row carries a tag, so the figures make a column whether we plan one or not; the slot just makes it deliberate.
And no total
A figure over a list of 62 mostly-owned cards invites the reading this deck costs $84.25, which is false. It costs that much to finish. The total goes where the denominator is unambiguous, and that is the Shopping filter.
One prop, not a media query
dense={filter === ‘shopping’} off the filter state. The tag is one component with one optional segment and one optional slot; nothing branches per width, and row height never moves.

Item 5 · does the tag become a link — no, and here is the target ledger
What a 44px line does to a 9px chip
buy·$45.00
the tag is 18px tall
the line is 44px
Getting either density to a legitimate target means a 44px box around an 18px chip, inside a row that is 30px tall — or on the tile, a target nested inside the Add target, at 9px, over card art. The tag stops being a tag. #184 exists because something shipped under that line.
• The tile tag is pointer-events: none over the art, and the tile’s own action is Add. The availability pass ruled the same way for the same reason — a 6px glyph that responds to taps in a grid of 44px buttons is a mis-tap generator.
• This would be the app’s first link-out anywhere. Leaving the product needs a visible affordance, a named destination, a new-tab decision and whatever disclosure the store relationship requires. None of that fits in a chip.
• Eight of them down one list is eight exits from a page whose job is to finish a deck.

Item 7 · how honest staleness has to be — three levels, one date
Level
What it says
Why there
The tag
buy·sold out no date, ever
Eight dates at 9px is noise, and the player’s question is can I buy this, not when did you look.
The header
prices from ManaPool · updated Monday
Provenance named once per surface, quietly. A weekly cadence makes age material, so the age rides with the source. A weekday, not 3 days ago — a relative phrase goes wrong at exactly the boundary where staleness starts to matter.
The accessible name
“$45.00 at ManaPool,
priced Monday”
Said in words, once per card, to the reader who cannot see the header. So yes — “priced Monday” gets said. Once on the page, once per price, never in the tag.
The road not taken
buy·$0.34·sold out
The last known price on a sold-out card is genuinely useful for budgeting, and it is still two facts fighting: a figure we will not stand behind, in a slot the header then has to decide whether to sum. Dropped, and carried as gap §1 — if it comes back it is two decisions, not one.
The honest caveat
The ingest never deletes, so a stale row means sold out or the ingest missed a run. The copy asserts the first, because that is the one the player can act on. A run-health signal would let the header say prices are behind instead of eight cards claiming to be sold out — and #291 does not expose one. Gap §2.

Contract
• State 4’s accessible name is today’s, byte for byte. The price clause is a suffix appended only when there is a price fact — the same rule rowAria took.
• Colour is never the sole signal. Every state is a word, a figure, or the absence of one; the amber carries buy, which is a word in all four.
• The tag is never a target at either density — no focus style, no hover, no 44px question.
• Contrast on --surface-sunken: 7.1:1 · 9.4:1 · 7.0:1 — all AAA at normal size, and the tag’s tint lifts all three.
• The total is static text in the existing header, not a live region. The decklist’s own announcement already covers a change.
• Exports untouched. Names only, both tabs, and the dialog’s prices for reference caption unchanged.
