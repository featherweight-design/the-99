HOW IT WORKS R1 an addendum to About R6 · placement · the method key · rows · entry links · phone

A closing appendix on /about: eight rows, one per thing the app decides, each built in the shape the Analysis tab’s sheet already uses. The four methods are words, not new shapes, and an entry that makes one of these decisions links down to its row.

Brief: 2026-09-28-how-it-works.md and its inventory (branch docs/about-how-it-works-brief). §3 is taken as locked. Page chrome, rail, sheet, index and jump mark are R6’s, unchanged. Frames are 1300×820 and 390×844. One thing needs a ruling before build: two answers fit none of the four methods (open 1, below).

§4.1 placement · appendix
The chapters run in the order you would use the app, and this runs by decision, so a fifth chapter would break the ordering it sits in. A page of its own would split the search and the contents, and it is a new route, which the brief rules out. So it goes after 04 with no numeral, below a hairline in the contents. The intro gains one clause.
§4.2 the marks · words
Four mono tags: rules text, other decks, model-written and your input. All four are drawn alike. Every shape in the app already has a meaning: the dot is ownership, the diamond is availability, and the ⓘ disc is “the app wrote this”. A fourth shape would be a fourth thing to learn, and a tag reads the same in a 560px sheet.
§4.3 entry links · yes, one each
Every entry that shows one of these decisions gets one quiet line after its keys: How this is decided ↓. It links to one row only, the row behind the entry’s main figure. Nineteen entries have one. The row lists its entries back, under explained in. The landing reuses R6’s jump mark.
§4.4 phone · same rows, stacked
Nothing is cut. A closed row is the title, then its tags and its verdict on a second line, at 52px or more. An open row stacks each label over its sentence. The key is a single column. The phone contents gains a fifth row, which is a link and a disclosure like the chapter rows.

Why a card is filed under Ramp

/decksopens a deck

The strip counts cards by the job they do, because that is usually how a deck comes up short: one board wipe is a problem in a way eleven creatures rarely is. The recommended mix comes from what other decks with your commander actually run, where we have enough of them, and from a common starting point where we do not. The strip says which. Either way it is not a rule: a role over its number is fine and says so by saying nothing.
