Where the retry goes, and why not per panel
One retry for one fetch. The four panels read one payload, so four retries would be four calls to the same endpoint and three of them lies about scope.

Desktop places it inline, right of the sentence, on the baseline of the clause it answers — the shipped degraded-banner idiom from the add panel, unchanged. Phone gives it its own 44px line under the sentence: inline at 390px it would either wrap mid-clause or steal width from the sentence, and it is the only control in the block.

Left border is --warning, not --fg-faint. That is the whole visual difference from the absent note, and it is the right one: absent is a permanent fact about this commander and gets the neutral rule; degraded is a thing that went wrong and can be fixed. Same shape, different rule, different string, different presence of a control — three signals, none of them colour alone.

Retry is not a spinner. In flight it takes the shipped disabled treatment (opacity, aria-busy) and the note text does not change. On success the note unmounts and figures appear; on a second failure the note stays exactly as it is — no escalating copy, no attempt count.

The bar is indeterminate, and says so structurally
An empty track is pixel-identical to a 0% fill, which is exactly the reading decision 9 forbids. A dashed 5px pill does not escape that objection — it is R1’s track geometry with a different border, so it is still a vessel, and a vessel invites the question of how full it is. R2 caught the prose and the drawing disagreeing on exactly this; the drawing moved.

Mid-fill the cell carries no track at all: a 1px dashed --border-strong rule on a zero-height element, holding the 5px the track occupies as margin so the cell does not change height between frames. A rule is not a container, so there is nothing for a fill to be measured against. Dashed is this codebase’s established “nothing here yet” (the dropzone border, the spent slot, the unowned dot), so it needs no new vocabulary.

The denominator stays. This is the one place the desktop cell keeps / 10 while the numerator is a dash: the target is known before the fill starts, and printing it is what makes the dash read as pending rather than broken. Every colour in the cell drops a step — label to --fg-locked, figure to --border-strong — so the whole strip reads as not-yet rather than as five failed roles.

The status pill goes dashed too, reading Draft: the deck genuinely is a draft until the rows land, and Building would be a computed claim about counts that do not exist.

No motion. No shimmer, no pulse, no indeterminate sweep — nothing new joins the reduced-motion block, per R1’s contract. Eight plate rows and one mono line, and if Design gets its way (one write, redirect held) none of it renders.

Three signals at phone, where desktop has four
The phone cell has no bar and no denominator — both are hidden md: in the shipped cell. What carries short at 390px, all three already shipped and none of them new:

1 · the brass figure — 2 in --brass against four in --success, straight from roleCellState.
2 · the arithmetic — 2 above rec 3. The rec N line is the phone’s denominator; it is why the shipped cell drops the fraction rather than shrinking it.
3 · the caption becomes a control — see shelf in brass where met cells draw an inert dash, and the whole 62px cell is the 44px-clearing target.

So brass is never the only signal, and none of it is new drawing. The one addendum call: a short cell in a just-filled deck takes 1px --border-strong instead of --border — a hairline, not a tint, not a colour. At 390px five cells in a row is a lot of small type, and the one the provenance line is talking about should be findable without counting. It is the only phone-only addition in the whole handoff, and it is one token swap.

Desktop keeps its bar at 67% of a --brass-mid fill, per roleCellState. 2 of 3 is a real quantity, so a real bar is correct there — unlike item 3, where there is no quantity to draw.
