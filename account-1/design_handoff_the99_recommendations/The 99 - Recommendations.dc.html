Recommendations you can act on · #130 · #131

The chip carries a number. The button carries a deck.

Two surfaces, one sentence. On /commanders/[slug] every Role Coverage chip becomes a control carrying one figure — inclusionPct — and opens the shipped card-detail modal. On /decks/[id] the deck arrives with 68 real rows in it, each marked auto, and one sweep to undo the lot.

Nothing new is invented. No new panel, no new modal, no new token, no new empty state, no blended score. The one drawn addition is a colourless auto tag; the one geometric change is that a wrap of names becomes a wrap of two-part pills, and on the phone a wrap becomes a capped list.

Read README.md for the decisions log, DENSITY-long-role.md for the max-render call, recommendations · copy strings.ts for production copy. Gated on the recommendation foundation (brief §0) — ready to draw, not yet ready to build.

§9.1 Chip density
Inline, two-part pill on desktop; the wrap becomes a 44px capped list on the phone. Never a bar per chip — 40 bars is a texture, not a figure.
§9.2 The auto marker
The buy tag’s geometry with no colour at all — a word on a plate at --fg-locked. It is a receipt, not a warning.
§9.3 Clear all auto
In the decklist toolbar, count on its face. No dialog — PR #204’s receipt extends, but as one strip, not 61 rows.
§9.4 The arrival
One provenance line under the strip, no toast and no moment. It leaves when the last auto card does.
§9.5 To-buy cards
The shipped buy tag carries it, unchanged. Shopping gets role groups and a count — no prices, because we hold none.
§9.6 The role strip
Unchanged. The fill can still miss a baseline (state 13), so the gap language still has a job. Arriving met is a state it already draws.

A kept row and a hand-added row are identical, and that is the point: once you have kept it, it is your card. Nothing records how it arrived.
The marker is the keep control
Ruling 5 needs a deliberate keep, which needs a control — and a second button on 68 rows is exactly the clutter the ruling is trying to avoid. So the tag is the button: it says auto at rest and keep ✓ under the pointer or focus. Activating it removes the tag and the row is an ordinary row forever after. Nothing else on the row changes.

Colourless on purpose. The buy tag’s geometry (mono 9.5px, radius 4px, 1px×5px) with --fg-locked on --elevated. Orange means “you need to buy this”; auto needs to mean nothing at all until you go looking for it. It is a word, not a colour, so §10 is satisfied by construction.

On a phone the tag keeps its 9.5px face inside a 44px hit box, the same trick the row’s ✕ already uses.

Inspection is not endorsement. Opening the modal from the name touches nothing. Two adjacent targets, two different meanings, and the destructive-looking one is the one that does nothing.
Phone row — 44px, three targets
Swords to Plowshares auto ✕
Smothering Tithe buy auto ✕
Both marks on one row is the busiest this list gets: buy is about the card, auto is about how it got here. Reading order puts the card’s own fact first. The marker yields before the name does — same shrink ordering the availability marker uses.

Where the sweep lives
In the decklist toolbar, right-aligned beside the All / Shopping filter — the row that already holds controls that act on the whole list. Not in the spine (that is identity, not action), not floating (nothing floats in this builder), not per group (four sweeps is not one act).

It carries its count: Clear all auto · 58. The number is the honesty — you can see the size of the act before you commit it, which is what a confirmation dialog is usually for.

It is a GhostButton per the §404 secondary contract, not a danger button: this is not destroying your work, it is declining ours. Absent entirely at 0 auto — no disabled control advertising a thing you cannot do.

Accessibility
section[aria-labelledby=role-ramp]
  ul > li > button.chip[aria-label]
  button.disclosure[aria-expanded][aria-controls]

li.deck-row > (button.name + button.auto[aria-label] + button.remove)
• Chip name: View details for Sol Ring. In 79% of decks. — the figure is inside the label, not an aria-label on a nested span, which BuyTag already learned gets discarded. Absent: … No EDHREC figure. The em dash itself is aria-hidden.
• Chips are a list of buttons, so a screen reader gets a count before the names and can skip the role wholesale.
• Tap targets: phone chip rows 44px; the desktop chip is 28px (pointer only, and its phone equivalent is the row). Auto tag and ✕ are 44×44 hit boxes at 9.5px and 13px faces.
• auto is a word, never a colour or a tint. Its control is aria-labeled Keep Sol Ring in this deck — the verb, not the marker’s name, because the marker is not what the player wants.
• The sweep announces once: 58 auto cards removed. 8 of 99. on the builder’s existing live region. A per-row announcement would read 58 messages over one gesture.
• Modal: shipped trap, Escape, first-focusable, focus returned to the originating chip. Nothing overridden.
• Focus: global :focus-visible, 2px --brass at +2px. The chip’s radius is 999px and the ring follows it.
