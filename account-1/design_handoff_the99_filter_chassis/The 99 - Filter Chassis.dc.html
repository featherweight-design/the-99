R2
One filter chassis · four routes, three implementations, one frame

One bar above the content, at five dimensions.

A new chassis for /commanders, /discover, /decks and /collection — three rows, one control vocabulary, per-surface dimensions. Drawn against /decks first, at its five (§2), because it carries the most; the other three are proved against it in §5 rather than drawn independently.

The one thing that is not var(--token) anywhere in this file is the mana pip, and §4 is about why that is correct and what it costs the surround. Everything else inside every frame is a token, as set collections established.

§2.3 is not a settled dimension. Set filtering on /commanders is locked, but §6 leaves its matching rule open and it is the one call here with a data dependency behind it. Ruled in §6.
Changed at R2, from review
1. The wrapping sort was a defect, and it is fixed by a rule rather than a nudge. The control row now never wraps: two forms, wide and compact, switching at 900px, with search as the only shrinking child. §2 carries the arithmetic, and it is set by /collection’s seven controls rather than /decks’ five.

§1 — the chassis

Three rows, always all three, in this order.

Lifted wholesale from filter-rack.tsx, which already carries the invariants and the tests: the chassis never collapses, never reorders, and never changes height when the results change. What is new is that the same three rows now hold five dimensions, that the frame sits above the content on every route, and that one panel shape opens every dimension.

Row 1 — controls
Every desktop control is 34px. The rack ships search at 34 and Order at 32; one rhythm, and the 2px mismatch goes. Dimensions run leading-edge in reading order; sort trails past a hairline. The width it returns
DeckFilterColumn is a 262px sibling inside a sm:flex-row. Mounted above the content, the chassis costs ~101px of height and returns all 262px to deck art.
§1b — every shipped filter, and what it becomes

Four control types. Nothing is dropped.

The chassis does not have a dimension list of its own — it has slots, and a route fills them. Every filter live today appears below with the control type it takes, and the type is decided by the shape of the values, not by the route, which is what makes the same dimension look identical on two surfaces and at two widths.

One rule does most of the work: segmented is only for a dimension with no off state. Colour match is always Any or Exactly; score-against is always one or the other; view is always rows or grid. Last-played has two values and an off, so it is a panel — which is also why the shipped deck column draws it as two independently-clearable rows rather than a pair of segments.

§2 — /decks, five dimensions, desktop

The densest surface, in five states.

Search · status · colour · played-window · sort. The control row never wraps. There are two forms and no in-between: the wide form above, and the compact form of §3, switching at 900px. Search is the only child that shrinks (min-width 132); every control is flex:none with white-space:nowrap; the trailing sort group is flex:none too, so it can never be pushed to a second line.

The arithmetic that sets the 900px switch is /collection, not /decks — seven controls is the widest list in the app: search 132 + pips 148 + Type 150 + Set 78 + Availability 120 + hairline 1 + Sort 160 + view toggle 70 + seven 12px gaps + 44px padding = 887px. Below that the wide form cannot be honest, so it does not try. A wrapped filter bar was the defect R1 shipped: the sort landed on its own line and read as a layout bug rather than a trailing slot.

Why the empty state names one fix
Because commandersCopy.empty already does, and it is the best copy in the app on this problem — bodyOneFix names the control and promises the row count that dropping it returns. /decks and /collection today show a generic nothing-found. The empty state lives below the chassis and never replaces it — the filters that caused the emptiness must stay on screen and stay removable.

§3b — the compact form on the other routes

/collection is the hard one. Seven dimensions on 390px.

It is the route that conforms least today — a horizontally-scrolling row of unlike pills, with colour behind a panel on the phone and inline on desktop. Under the chassis it is the same three rows as every other route. The view toggle is what makes it hard: four things want the persistent row and only three fit.

Ruling: the view toggle goes on the result line, at its trailing edge. It is not a filter, the result line is the one row with dead space to the right of it, and “how many results” sitting beside “how they are drawn” is a sentence that reads. The arithmetic it avoids: search 132 + sort 44 + Filters 44 + view 76 + three 8px gaps + 32px padding = 352px of a 390px screen, which fits but leaves search at its minimum on the app’s most search-driven surface.

Sheet order is fixed, not per route
Pips first, always. R1 put them in the middle of the deck sheet, between two chip groups, and the block’s weight made the sheet read as unbalanced — five 44px circles of full-strength artwork outweigh everything around them. At the top they are the anchor instead of an interruption, and they are also the dimension every route shares. Then segmented controls, then chip groups, then anything carrying a hint line. A hint is the last thing in its group and the group is near the bottom, because a sentence between two rows of controls is the same interruption in a different form.
A long vocabulary stays searchable in the sheet
Set and Printed in cannot be chip groups — 41 owned sets is not a chip row, and the full set list is thousands. In the sheet they are a 44px row that opens the panel’s own searchable list as a second layer. That is the one place the compact form nests, and it nests because filter-panel.tsx’s searchable branch already exists and already works at this width.
Every control keeps its shape across widths
A segmented control is a segmented control at 34px and at 44px — not a segmented control on desktop and a pair of pills on the phone, which is what the deck sheet does with sort today and the commander sheet does with match. Only two things change between widths: target size, and whether a panel is anchored or a block in the sheet.
§4 — the pip, and the surround

Stop dimming it. That is the whole treatment.

§4 of the brief says the pip cannot adapt, so the surround must. Having measured it, the surround’s job turns out to be getting out of the way: all three shipped colour controls carry their unlit state as an opacity of 0.34–0.40, and a faded cream circle with a black glyph in it is illegible on a near-black ground. The pip is never dimmed and never tinted. Lit is an --accent ring; unlit is no ring.

§5 of the brief is right that no prior figure exists. Here are the figures, computed from the six theme blocks to two decimals — and the headline is that the state this round replaces sits at 1.38:1, well under the 3:1 non-text floor, on three shipped surfaces today.

The ring is the only state carrier
--accent against --surface-sunken floors at 4.42:1 (Phyrexia) — clears 3:1 in all six. --border-strong does not: it floors at 1.24:1, so a hairline rim can never be the carrier and the unlit pip carries no rim at all.
Never colour alone
The ring is one carrier; the chip in row 2 is the second, and it names the colour in words (White, or 2 colours past one). Past one pip the chip shingles at −5px, borrowing cost-pips.tsx’s own overlap.
Circles, not rounded squares
The asset is a circle. The rack draws a 6px-radius square and the deck column a 7px one; both would crop the artwork or float it in a box. 22px circle desktop, 44px circle phone — and the phone pip is a real 44px target, not a 26px pip in a 44px box.
One figure to confirm at build
The brief quotes W = #F8F6D8 and the WU half = #C1D7E9 verbatim. B, R and G are the canonical pip colours, not quoted anywhere I could read — the figures above assume #CBC2BF / #F9AA8F / #9BD3AE. Confirm on first fetch. The floor has 6.5 points of headroom, so no plausible value threatens it.

§6 — the calls

§3.1 – §3.5, and §6’s open question.

§3.1Two forms, four control types, and a route fills slots.
The chassis has no dimension list of its own. It has a field slot, a pip slot, an ordered run of dimension slots, and a trailing slot for sort — and a route fills them from §1b’s four control types, chosen by the shape of the values rather than by the surface. That is what makes status on /decks and type on /collection the same object, and what lets a fifth route mount the chassis without a design round.
And there are exactly two forms. Wide above 900px, compact below, nothing in between and no wrapping at any width. R1 let row 1 wrap and the sort dropped to its own line, which read as a bug rather than as a trailing slot — correctly, because a trailing slot that is not trailing is not a slot. Search is the only child that shrinks; everything else is flex:none.
A control keeps its shape across both forms. Only the target size changes, and whether a panel is anchored or a block in the sheet. A segmented control does not become a pair of pills, and a pip row does not become a dropdown — which is the specific inconsistency /collection ships today, with colour inline on desktop and behind a panel on the phone.
§3.3The result line is the shared answer. Clear is two controls with two scopes.
result-line.tsx moves up to the shared chassis unchanged: mono 11px, --fg-muted, tabular, one live region per surface, aria-atomic, drawn instantly and announced at 300ms, and stating the lane rather than a ratio at rest. It already carries the reasoning and the tests, and /collection’s bar already debounces its own count the same way.
On clear: the chip’s × removes one value, the panel’s footer clears that dimension, and Clear all filters at the chip line’s trailing edge clears every dimension and never the sort. Three scopes, each worded to say which. This deliberately keeps filter-panel.tsx’s per-dimension Clear rather than retiring it — FilterChangeIntent exists precisely because “Clear” and “toggled the last one off” land on the same state and must announce differently, and collapsing to one global Clear would throw that distinction away. What is retired: the deck column’s footer Clear, the deck sheet’s footer Clear, and the column’s all statuses on / someOn(n) line, whose job the chip line and the panel footer now do between them.
§3.4Sort stays in the chassis, in a separated trailing slot, on every route and at both widths.
Not because sort is a filter — it is not, and the hairline is what says so — but because every alternative is worse on this app’s actual surfaces. /decks’ desktop sort lives in a toolbar that disappears with the sidebar, so “leave it where it is” is not available; /collection and both commander lanes already trail it inside the bar. Three of four routes need no change.
At phone it is the 44px icon trigger on the persistent row, which is what filter-rack.tsx ships today via iconOnly={!wide} — already built, already tested, on two routes. The deck sheet’s Sort group is retired, which is the one place the app answers this question twice. Consequence to accept: sort is then never inside the sheet on any route, so the sheet is filters only and its badge stays honest.
§3.5The two lanes converge on the frame and keep exactly two differences — both data, not layout.
Score against stays owned-lane only (the tests assert its absence on discover, and a greyed stand-in was already rejected), and the sort options stay per-lane — two of discover’s five decide what the lane is ranked on, and the owned lane has no equivalent. Both survive because they describe what the data can answer. Everything else merges: one frame, one control order, one panel shape, one result line.
The brief asks whether the difference is “still meaningful”. It is, and the test is a question a player could ask: what would this control do here? Score-against on discover has no answer — you own none of it.
§6Any printing, on both lanes — and the control is called Printed in, not Set.
Option (1). Option (2) — owned printing — is the better long-term answer and engineering is right that it is the direction, but it cannot ship this round on the surface that needs it: by construction candidates.ts partitions /discover to commanders the player does not own, so an owned-printing filter there matches nothing, ever. That forces the dimension to be owned-lane-only — a third per-lane difference in the same round that §3.5 argues for convergence. (1) also needs no printing-aware row rendering, so it does not drag the printed-name work onto a lane whose art is oracle-level by construction.
The wording is the fix for the visible contradiction the brief names. A row matching on a set while drawing another set’s art is only a contradiction if the control claimed to be about the art. Printed in Duskmourn is true of the oracle card whichever printing the row happens to draw; Set: DSK implies the thing on screen. One word, and the row needs no change.
Named collision: /collection’s Set ▾ means something genuinely different — sets you hold cards from, counted from setCounts. It keeps its name. Two differently-named controls because they are two different questions; this is the same trap set collections caught as its Set ▾ / By set collision, decision 20. When the commander lane becomes printing-aware, (2) is a filter swap behind an unchanged control — and at that point the honest label is still Printed in.
§2.1One flag on the locked decision: the new chassis retires a component that landed three weeks ago.
Drawn as locked — new chassis, not an adoption. But the brief’s rejection names “the collection bar” and “the commanders rack”, and the collection bar is five mounts of ui/filter-panel.tsx — 42.5KB of component with 34.6KB of tests, ratified in collection-filter-panel.md, named “the reference implementation” in rack/filter-sheet.tsx’s own header, and listed by set collections’ readiness as “mounted, not forked”.
So the drawing above deliberately specifies the panel as what FilterPanel already does — anchored not in flow, searchable past a length threshold, counts per row, single-select mode for sort, one live region written by the bar, a per-dimension Clear that announces its intent. Read §2.1 as a new chassis around the existing panel and nothing needs rewriting; read it as a new panel too and this round throws away a tested component and set collections’ Task 9 lands on a control that is about to be replaced. That is the one decision I want confirmed before build, and it is the difference between a moderate refactor and a large one.
§7 — the contract

What a build must not change.

Invariants
1. Three rows, always all three, in order. Row 2 prints no filters on at rest rather than collapsing.
2. The chassis never changes height when results change.
3. One live region per surface, and it is the result line.
4. One treatment mounted per width — never a desktop copy and a phone copy hidden past each other.
5. The pip carries no opacity, no tint, no token colour. Ever.
6. No filter state is carried by colour alone.
7. The badge counts active dimensions, never search and never sort.
8. Every phone target is 44px, pips included.
9. The empty state sits below the chassis and never replaces it.
Retired by this round
deck-filter-column.tsx — the whole 262px column, its FilterRow keyline vocabulary, its footer Clear and its allOn/someOn line.
ColourSwatch, rack/colour-control.tsx’s pip half, and collection/color-pip.tsx — three controls, one replacement. The match modifier survives.
The deck sheet’s Sort group.
The collection bar’s COLOR caption.
Every opacity on a colour control: 0.34, 0.40 and color-pip’s opacity-40.
The 32px/34px control-height split inside one row.
Dependencies & sequencing
Set collections Task 9 goes first and produces the extracted colour control this treatment lands on. Its test asserts getByRole("button") with aria-pressed; the real API is role="switch" with aria-checked — the brief flags it and it must be rewritten before the task runs.
The set pages inherit this pip. Brief §5 takes that branch and it needs ratifying against a Green handoff. The contrast is measured here: 9.51:1 floor, so the inheritance is safe on the number, not merely assumed.
Asset delivery is unspecified. Five remote SVGs per surface, one per pip, theme-independent and cacheable forever. Whether they ship as static assets, sprite or hotlink is engineering’s, but hotlinking a third party in the filter chrome of four routes is the one choice Design would push back on.
cost-pips.tsx is out of scope and drawn as if convergence will be yes — same circles, same assets, same −5px shingle.
