R1
Commander row · how a deck plays · PR #664

Two sentences and three figures. The second sentence always says something.

One row component on two routes. The first sentence is the commander's and it is already stored and already shipped on Commander Details; the second is the reader's, templated, and drawn on every row so the height never moves between two sorts. Nothing in the composition can be read off ownership — which is what cut not in your collection, and what every state on this canvas is checked against.

The one open item resolves into the slot that already exists. Nothing new is a copy swap in the gap line's own slot, asserting the overlap rather than a gap — no glyph, no chip, no colour, no reordering. And it came with a finding: that condition has two causes and only one of them is this state, so No tags is redefined post-classifier and takes the other.

Read INDEX.md for the call and what engineering is buying, STATES-commander-row.md for the matrix and the ten rules, commander-row · copy strings.ts for the one new string and the retirement list, READINESS.md for the three figures R1 derived rather than measured. §3 the gap line — four occupants, one slot, one ink

Three name counts and the two empty cases, drawn at the desktop measure. The names take --fg at 600 and never brass — the same treatment why-section.tsx gives a figure inside a sentence on Commander Details, which is the surface this row is now agreeing with.

Three is the cap the spec sets and the dictionary indices the payload carries. No and 32 more: the count was the old sentence's disclosure, and the reader cannot act on it.

No tag data — EDHREC hasn't filed this commander under any archetype yet.
The claim is about EDHREC, not the reader, so it takes the plated note rather than prose in the reader's voice. This is the branch the Glarb case lands in once the classifier has run.
what the classifier changes about the drawing
Nothing, except which occupant the slot gets — and that is the point of keeping it to one slot. The two failure modes the spec records are both invisible here: Equipment, Treasure, Auras, Sagas, Food, Clues, Vehicles, Shrines, Books, Adventures, Mounts, Toys, Eggs are mechanics that must reach the line (one of them is drawn in state 5), and Dwarves, Faeries, Horses, Fungi, Heroes are creature types that must not. Design has no drawing for either; it has a dependency on both, and it is listed in §3.
why the line is prose and not chips
Three labels in three chips is the composition the role chips just left, and it reads as a filter control the row does not have. The sentence also carries the subject — none of your decks — which a chip row cannot, and which is the only thing making these three words about the reader rather than about the commander.

§5 the three figures — and why Fit is not in the foot line
Fit draws in the band's chip, at both widths
The spec's sketch prints FIT 60/100 first in the foot line. Three arguments move it up into the chip that /commanders already draws:
1 · A headline third in a row of three is a peer. The locked decision is not just Fit is the figure, it is that Fit is the headline figure. 27px in a plated chip says that; 12.5px mono beside a deck count does not.
2 · The band needs a trailing figure and the phone needs it more. Both shipped rows put one there; take it away and the phone band is a name on 208px of art.
3 · It is the parity ruling, drawn. Commander Details prints FIT FOR YOUR COLLECTION 60/100 as a headline, and /commanders prints the chip. Three surfaces, one number, and now one prominence.
The sketch's reading order survives intact: Fit is still read before popularity and price, because the band is read before the foot line.
popularity
The one thing on the row the sentence cannot tell you. Kept as shipped, with the figure at --fg and the words at body ink — Emphasised's pattern, unchanged. An absent count prints no figure, never a zero.
price
PriceSlot, folded into the foot line rather than given a hairline of its own: one line of three figures needs one rule above it, not two. Its dot, its Mana Pool label, its magnitude step at three figures and its absent rule are all unchanged.
the height arithmetic
Desktop: band 208 + 24 + 2×21.6 + 11 + 21.6 + 11 + 1 + 10 + 20 + 24 ≈ 375px at two sentence lines. Phone at four: band 208 + 12 + 4×20 + 9 + 20 + 9 + 1 + 9 + 18 + 12 ≈ 378px. The old owned row was 208 + ~190; this one trades a data zone of figures for one of sentences at roughly the same cost.

one entry, not two
The row is the same component on two routes and that is the feature. Two entries — one per lane — would be the same sentence twice, and would teach the reader a distinction the row was built to remove.
the caption's second sentence is the feature
A reader whose decks already cover a commander sees Nothing new and has no way to know it is a state rather than a shrug. The About page is the only place that can say so without drawing a badge on the row.
what the entry must not say
Nothing about EDHREC tags, archetypes, the classifier or creature types. The reader's model is how it plays and what I don't play yet; the vocabulary underneath is the reason the old row read as a data table.
