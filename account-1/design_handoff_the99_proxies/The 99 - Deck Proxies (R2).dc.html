Deck proxies · /decks/[id] · card detail · /collection/proxies · /decks · /changes

Covered is not owned.

A proxy is one deck's answer about one slot: this deck needs nothing from the collection for this card. The slot is filled, the claim is released to whichever deck holds the real copy, and the player owns nothing new. Every drawing in this package is built to keep those two facts apart, because this is the one feature where the app could quietly lie about what is in a box.

The feature spends no colour, and that is the central call, not a saving. Every hue in this app is already booked — --accent is in use / to buy, --warning is money, --success is complete, --danger is over-committed. A proxy is none of those, and the one thing it must never say is owned. So the tag is neutral ink on a dashed hairline, which borrows no meaning and inherits the app's existing not-cardboard vocabulary — the unowned ownership dot, the unresolved row, the missing set cell, all dashed already. §9 is the dividend: no per-theme variant exists anywhere in the feature.

Source of truth: docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-14-deck-proxies-design.md, read in full at main. The five rulings are drawn as written and not reopened. Structural sibling: design/design_handoff_the99_set_collections/ — same folder shape, same copy-export shape, and the same problem of a new route under the collection reached from the filter bar's rack.

§1 · the tag, and the slot it shares with buy

One glyph, one slot, and a key that decodes it.

BuyTag is the one place the word buy and its colour are declared: mono, radius 4, px 5 / py 1, --warning ink on a 13% warning plate. The proxy mark is that box at its collapsed density — the density the All view already draws money at — carrying P where money carries $. Same box means the row does not move when the state flips, which is the whole reason the two share a slot rather than sitting beside each other.

anatomy · the two marks, row density, 1:1
P buy·$32.90 $
Top to bottom: the proxy mark, the Shopping-density buy tag with its FIGURE_SLOT tail, and the All-view collapsed buy tag — the third of those is the pattern the first one joins, not a new one. The proxy mark has one density and the buy tag has two, and the asymmetry is the model's rather than a shortcut: a proxied card is never a Shopping row, so there is no surface on which the word would ever be drawn beside a card name. Money needs a full form because it carries a figure, and the proxy mark never does.
why dashed, and why that token
Dashed is already this app's word for not settled cardboard: the unowned ownership dot is a dashed hollow, the unresolved row is dashed, the spent-slot receipt is dashed, the missing set cell is dashed. A printed card is exactly a card-shaped thing that is not cardboard, so the edge says it before the word does.

The edge is --fg-locked and never --border-dashed, which is set_collections' general rule restated: a dashed line that carries state takes a foreground token, not a border token. Measured against --surface-rail, the row's own ground, --fg-locked clears 3.14–3.35:1 in all six themes and --border-dashed clears none of them. The glyph takes --fg at weight 600 — 11.3:1 (First Frost) to 14.4:1 (Low Sun) — because it is text, it owes 4.5:1, and it is the one character carrying the whole mark. It is the brightest ink in the row, a step above the --fg-body the word used to take, and that lift is where the eye-catching quality comes from without spending a hue.
the glyph, and why a letter rather than a shape
P for proxy — the initial of the word players already use, and the word in the route. It sits in the same box as $ and therefore reads as the same kind of thing before it is decoded.

A letter, not a shape. Every small geometric mark in this app is already taken: the rotated diamond is the availability glyph in four states, a filled diamond is printing_picker's Your pick, an outlined one is BY NAME and the rack's link, and a disc is the ownership dot. A fifth diamond would be unreadable, and any new shape needs teaching anyway — at which point a letter teaches faster, because a key can spell it out.

And a letter scales where a hue does not. Marks are about to become a family — game changers, bracket movers — and there are 26 letters against four hue families in the token contract, all four of which are already booked. §1b is that argument in full.

The word is still authored, in three places: the key, the title, and the accessible name. Rejected as the word: printed (one letter from printing, on surfaces that ship both), paper (slang, and it names the object rather than the deck's answer), covered (states the effect and hides the cause).

the exclusion is structural, not a pair of conditionals
A proxy tag and a buy tag can never co-occur, and the model already guarantees it: isShoppingCard tests proxied first, so a proxied card is never a Shopping row and never carries a price at all.

Draw it as one slot, not two siblings. The row today writes {(!card.owned || spokenFor) && <BuyTag />}; adding {card.proxied && <ProxyTag />} beside it makes the exclusivity a property of two booleans that happen to disagree today. One function — deckRowTag(card): "proxy" | "buy" | null — makes both tags impossible to draw at once by construction, which is the same move the spec makes when it scopes the proxy read to membership rather than enumerating six cleanup paths. Two independent conditionals is how they co-occur eighteen months from now, in a state nobody drew.

Corollary, and it is the reason this is in a red box rather than the component table: the slot must also be unreserved. Row 2 above shows an owned card with an empty slot and no gap — the tag is flex:none and simply absent, exactly as the buy tag is today.
§1b · the mark's skin — ratified: A

If marks are a family, colour cannot be the identifier.

ratified · option A, neutral bright
Product ratified A — --fg at weight 600 on a 1px dashed var(--fg-locked) edge, no fill — which is what every other frame in this canvas draws. B, C and D stay in the package rather than being deleted, because the argument they carry is the one a future mark will reopen: the day someone asks for a game changer in green, this frame is the record of why the family shares one neutral skin and why no fixed hue survives six accents. C is the one to revisit if that answer ever changes, and it is a deliberate change to what --accent means rather than a free win.

The moment there are four marks — money, proxy, game changer, bracket mover — a reader cannot learn four hues, and the token contract does not have four hues to give. So the glyph identifies, the key decodes, and colour only has one job left: say there is a mark here. That reframing is what makes the question below answerable at all, because it turns what colour is proxy into what colour are marks, which is a decision made once.

Money keeps --warning and is the exception, not the precedent. It ships, and a price is the one mark that is a genuinely different kind of fact — it is the only one you can act on with a credit card. Everything else takes one shared skin, and the four candidates below are candidates for that skin rather than for the proxy tag alone.

the honest colour audit — there is no unclaimed hue
The 37-key contract has exactly four hue families and all four are spoken for: --accent is in use / to buy, --warning is money, --success is complete — which this feature is forbidden from touching by definition — and --danger is over-committed. The five --avail-* keys are aliases of accent, danger and --fg-muted, and the five WUBRG swatches mean colour identity, so a blue glyph would read as a blue card.

And minting one does not escape it. Three of the four semantics are near-identical in all six themes, so a new one would have to be too — one hex, six palettes. But --accent is the per-theme hue, and across the six it lands on orange, pink, yellow-green, teal, violet and blue. That is six of the eight hue regions. Any fixed hue collides with at least one theme's accent, in the one theme whose reader will never see the others to compare. Option D below is drawn precisely so that collision is visible rather than argued.

§2 · the commander slot

Expensive legends are the most proxied cards there are.

The commander has no row — it is the spine's art band, and its name lives in the mono caption beside the identity pips and the status pill. The tag joins that caption row, after the name group and before the status pill, at 9.5px. The route to the control is the one that already exists: the band's own FullCardTrigger opens the card sheet, and the sheet now carries the deck id.

The tag takes a scrim on the band and only on the band. Everywhere else it sits on a flat surface where the dashed edge measures 3.14–3.35:1. Here it sits on a photograph, where nothing is guaranteed — so it takes color-mix(in oklab, var(--bg) 92%, transparent) behind it, unconditionally. That is BuyTag's own tile scrim, at its own opacity, for its own stated reason — a theme with a lighter canvas wants a lighter scrim, or the tag reads as a hole punched in the art. Unconditional, not gated on how bright the crop is: a primitive that decided how to look from what is behind it is the defect that comment warns about.
The line under the bar is this handoff's one addition beyond the spec's surfaces, and it is severable. A 99-card deck reading Complete with seven printed cards in it is correct and is also the exact moment a player could be misled. 7 of these are proxies is one mono --fg-muted line, no colour, no control, absent at zero. Not a roll-up band — those are alarm chrome for over-commitment, and a proxy is not a problem to be solved. Cut it and nothing else in the package moves.
The status pill still says Complete, and that is not a lie. This app's vocabulary is already explicit that finished lists hold — the pill has always been a claim about the list, never about a box. What must not happen is a second, ownership-shaped reading arriving beside it; which is why the line above says proxies and not 7 you don't own, a sentence that would be true of the buy list too and means something else there.

§3 · the card sheet · mark and unmark

A square tick, because every deck really can have one.

filter-panel.tsx ships the ruling this feature needs and card_assignments spent a round confirming it: round for single-select — a square tick on a one-of-many list promises you can have two. The assignment chooser went round because one copy cannot honour that promise. A proxy claims nothing, so here the promise is true: every deck in the list can be proxying the same card at the same time, independently, and a square tick is the shape that says so. Same rule, opposite answer, because the supply constraint is gone.

Opened from /collection, from a set cell, or from commander detail, there is no deck to proxy into — so there is no block, not a disabled one. The grey in place, never absent rule protects a control's state; here there is no state, because there is no deck the control could be about.
Geometry is filter-panel.tsx's, taken unchanged: 16px desktop / 18px phone, gap 11 / 12, 1px ring, row floor 44px at both widths. The only departure is the corner — radius 2, the panel's own square-tick radius, against the chooser's 999px.
The fill is --fg-body, not --accent, and the check is --bg. Accent is reserved for in use / to buy on every surface this sheet can be opened from; spending it here would make a printed card the loudest thing in a receipt about real cardboard. Neutral is also what §9 costs nothing to theme.
No Apply, no confirm, no undo receipt. Ruling 2 says the toggle lands immediately, so a button would be a second act for a decision already made. Unmarking can put the deck back to short — the spec is explicit that this is the same arithmetic a pull already performs and needs no warning of its own. Drawn as written: state (b) to state (a) is one tap with nothing in between.
Accessibility is native and nothing is bolted on. role="checkbox", aria-checked, the label naming the deck. The state line under it is aria-describedby rather than part of the name, because it is the consequence rather than the control.
Where the block sits: under the availability receipt, never above it. The receipt is what the card is across every deck; this is what this deck does about it. Reading the general before the particular is also what makes the marked state's sentence land — nothing is claimed only means something once you have just read what the claims are.
One heading, not the deck's name. IN THIS DECK in the sheet's shipped mono caption skin; the deck's name rides the label where it is a fact the reader must check, not a heading they must parse. The sheet is opened from the deck they are standing in, but a sheet paged through with Prev/Next is easy to lose your place in — so the name is in the sentence you are about to act on.
§4 · the receipt · real holders and proxying decks together

The split still adds up. The proxies sit outside it.

AvailabilityDetail is a receipt and it has to add up: a total, one row per claiming deck with that deck's own ask, then in use + free === owned. A proxying deck was never in that arithmetic and must not enter it. So it rides below the split, behind a hairline, in a list of its own — and that list has no right-hand column at all, because holds N / wants N are quantities of cardboard and there is no cardboard here. The missing figure is the statement.

§5 · the entry point · filter-bar's rack

Beside By set, and it differs on exactly one condition.

Both pills change which surface you are on, so both live in the rack's trailing cluster, before the view well and never inside it. The one difference is the one the spec names: By set is gated on hasCollection and Proxies is not. A set browser with no collection has nothing to say; a proxy list is most useful to exactly the player whose cards are all printed.

Same 46px pill, same 7px diamond — outlined instead of filled. That is printing_picker's unset half again, the same move set_collections made for its BY NAME mark: not a new mark, the other half of one that exists. Hairline is --border-strong, not --accent, because two accent hairlines side by side is two permanent off-default signals in 180px, and the second one stops meaning anything.
Ranking is deliberate and it is not disabled-ness. The label is --fg-body, identical to its neighbour's; only the edge and the diamond's fill are quieter. A plain link, no query, no memory. Same rule By set took after the Commanders/Discover warning: nothing carries across the route boundary in either direction. /collection's search, type, colour and availability filters are not applied to the proxy list, and the list has no filters to apply back.

The row price is a bare figure, not a buy tag. The figure answers what would it cost; the tag would claim you have to buy this, which is exactly what a proxy says you do not.

And no Send N to ManaPool. The cart builder assumes a shopping list; this is a list of things the player has decided not to buy. The figure is a reference, not a basket.
read-only, on purpose — and this one is worth ruling on
There is no unmark control on this page. The row opens the card sheet with no deck id — a card here belongs to several decks, and the sheet's control is per deck — so the block in §3 correctly does not render. Unmarking happens where the proxy lives, and the deck names on each row are the links that get you there.

The alternative is expressible: the row knows every deck id, so it could carry one small control per named deck. It is not drawn, because that puts N state-changing controls inside one row of a list whose job is to be read — and because the most destructive version of this page is one where a mis-tap silently puts a deck back on the shopping list. Flagged for Product rather than settled: if the print-run workflow turns out to involve pruning the list in place, this is the decision to reopen first.

“takes no colour” is structural here, not a rule to remember
The slot is coloured by DETAIL_COLOR[status], so a naive addition would inherit --danger on an Over deck and --warning on an Unresolved one — a proxy count in alarm ink, which R1 forbids.

It cannot happen, and no new rule is needed to stop it. The line is a fallback into the null branch, exactly as the inUseElsewhere clause is — it never displaces a figure. And every status that would lend it a warning colour already has a figure in that slot: Over has 4 over, Unresolved has 3 lines unresolved, To buy has its roll-up. The statuses that reach the fallback — Complete, Building, Draft — are --fg-faint in the map, all three.

So the constraint is enforced by the precedence, not by a colour override, and it holds for any status added later: a status with something to say keeps saying it, and one with nothing to say has nothing to lend.

§12 · R2 · the review row's bucket

A chip, not a heading — and silence here would be the lie.

Gap report item 5 asked for a word on a bucket. The bucket headings do not exist. Deck-versioning's R2 ruled availability a per-row treatment rather than a grouping; review.bucketFree and its siblings were deleted, a test pins their absence ("bucketFree" in copy.review === false), and the buckets survive as counts and per-row chips. So the word is a fourth branch of bucketWord, in chipBuy's skin.

And that surface's own precedent argues for the opposite of what it looks like it argues for. bucketWord returns null at free and at a null bucket — so silence is a shipped, legitimate state, and a proxied add could plausibly borrow it. It must not. free means your collection has a spare copy for this. Drawing a proxied add the same way makes the app say a copy exists in a box, on the one surface that exists to tell a player what a commit will cost them.

Three figures must exclude the proxied add, and each has a shipped precedent for how. One thing I am flagging rather than deciding: groupInBreakdown(free, sleeved, buy) is a shipped three-term string, and a proxied add is in none of the three — so the terms stop summing to the group's count. My recommendation is to leave it under-summing rather than add a fourth term: each term stays true, and it is the same posture as footerUnknown dropping a term rather than printing a figure nobody can stand behind. A fourth term is a change to a shipped surface R2 was not given — the reason stamp.columnHeader was dropped from that same file.
