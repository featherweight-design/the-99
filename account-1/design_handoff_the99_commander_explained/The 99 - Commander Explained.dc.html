D · §4.3 — the hero at a denominator that is not 100
R3 correction — the two-string claim was wrong twice
R1 and R2 both said the bar was already telling the truth and only the caption lied — that deleting the rescale was entirely two strings and one rounding. That was true only while the rescale existed. The fill is width: fit.pct% and format.ts:27 computes pct = Math.min(s.score, 100). Delete the rescale and score becomes a raw 32, so the same bar renders at 32% of track, not 80%. Every frame below draws 80%, and 80% is correct — but only once fitSegments takes the denominator and computes pct = 100 × score / measurable. That is a change in format.ts, and it is the reason the denominator has to travel with the score rather than be derived at the hero.
The bar's appearance is unchanged and must-not 1 still holds — same card, same 12px track, same two segments, same two ramp tokens, same 80%. What is corrected is the claim about cost: two strings, one deletion, one rounding rule and one change in format.ts.
Not ours to answer — raising it
The score is also a sort key, and this handoff has been treating it as a display value. recommend.ts:214 sorts on it via byScoreThenOracleId, and store.ts:182 orders the persisted list by desc(commanderScores.score). Once a no-synergy commander stores 32 instead of a rescaled 80, it does not just print differently — it falls down the ranking, and with it the owned list's order and the home hero's first item.
There is a real argument on each side. A commander EDHREC cannot see has genuinely less evidence behind it, so ranking it below one that earned 80 of 100 may be the honest outcome — or it may bury good commanders for a data gap that is not the player's fault. Both are defensible and neither is a design call: it decides what the app recommends, not how it looks. Hugh's to answer. If the ranking must not move, the fix is a separate rankScore that keeps the rescale for ordering while score and measurableTotal carry the honest pair for display — which is more code than §4.3 implies and worth knowing before the estimate, not after.
R3 correction — “auditable by addition” needs a rounding rule
The whole case for points-over-weight is that 12 + 20 + 42 = 74 can be checked against the number above it. Under today's arithmetic it cannot. score.ts:179-181 rounds each segment to one decimal and rounds the total separately, so a commander at 12.4 + 20.4 + 42.4 stores 75.2 and the hero prints 75 above a legend reading 12/15 · 20/25 · 42/60 and a key line reading 12 + 20 + 42 = 75. The key would be printing a false equation, in the one element whose entire job is to be checkable.
The rule: round each segment to an integer, and define the score as the sum of those integers. Not the reverse — rounding the total and apportioning the remainder would put an arbitrary point on one segment. This makes the equation true by construction at every commander, which is stronger than making it true on average, and it removes the decimal from a figure nothing ever displayed to one place anyway.
It moves scores by up to ±1.5 against today's stored values, so it is a backfill of commander_scores, and combined with the item above it can change ranking order at ties. R2's “only the string changes” was wrong: making the hero auditable is a numeric change, and it is the price of the claim. The alternative is to print one decimal everywhere — 12.4 + 20.4 + 42.4 = 75.2 — which is true, checkable, and four decimals of noise in a 52px figure. Rejected, but it is the honest fallback if the backfill is unwelcome.
