The receipt is the 1+2 handoff's toast, and the row's slot holds still until the list is read again.

Call 5 — the receipt lives in the app's toast slot, not on the row. It's the same component as the 1+2 removal toast (R2 §D). It says what was added, and to which printing and finish, with History and Undo. Since it doesn't live on the row, it can't be lost when the row leaves. It's announced politely, and it lasts the toast's 8 seconds, paused while hovered or focused, or until the next write. When the copy went to an older deck, the toast says so in a full sentence, and the row says it too, so the toast going away loses nothing. Call 8 — a row that leaves keeps its slot. It becomes the spent-slot receipt shape: same height, a dashed sunken box, and the name in muted mono. It holds until the list is read again (a tab switch, or coming back to the deck). A row that stays redraws in place and doesn't move to Spoken for until that same read.

Call 6 — Undo is pressed on the toast, and it's a revert. The button reads Checking decks… from the first frame. If a committed deck would read short, the §E warning opens with the verb Undo: Undoing leaves a deck short · Undo anyway. The deck the copy was bought for is left out of that check. The player is on its page, undoing its own purchase, and they watch its row come back. Naming it in a warning would say the same thing a second time, on every clean undo. Call 7 — the History entry names its deck, as a link that reads the deck's name today. When the deck is gone, the entry says so in words and keeps Revert, because the copy is still in the collection.

One crossfade, 150ms, opacity only. The live row and the held slot share the row's own shell, so the height is the same by construction (H29, the spent-slot rule). Nothing above or below moves. Under reduced motion it's a swap in one frame. The slot isn't a control. It has no Undo of its own, since the toast and History hold that. Undo while the slot is held swaps the slot back to the live row in place, using the same 150ms fade.
