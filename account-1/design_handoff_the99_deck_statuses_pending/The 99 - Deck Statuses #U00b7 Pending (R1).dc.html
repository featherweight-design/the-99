The phase goes first. Draft and New.

R6 · 5a’s value could never reach the deck it was for
Why R5 is wrong — and it is worse than the name
To build sat below To buy, so it only ever fired on a deck where every card was already owned. Your deck — 99 cards, several not yet bought — read To buy, exactly as before. R5 could not reach the deck it was written for.
And that is structural, not a slip. One pill is one ordered cascade. A value placed below To buy can only occupy the gap the other six leave — the full, owned, resolved deck. The one deck that needed no help. Placing the phase last guarantees it describes the wrong decks.
Your transition case is the proof. Commit that deck and it becomes To buy, because committing does not buy anything. A phase that sorts below ownership produces that nonsense; a phase that sorts above it cannot.
The resistance to New / Draft, stated honestly
I wrote that the new value “does not want the word”. The real reason was the rename cascade — StatusPill, refreshCopy.status, the URL value, the filter row, the About key line. That is an implementation cost, and I presented it as vocabulary logic. It should have been a line item you priced, not an argument you had to see through.
On the merits your words win outright. A draft is a draft at any length — every other tool means it that way, and none of them requires a document to be empty first. New is then the honest word for the thing that has a commander and nothing else.

A seventh word: To build

R5 · retires both marks from 4a · superseded by 6a — placed below To buy, this value can never reach a deck with cards left to buy
What R4 got wrong — plainly
Both marks work as drawn, and both are wordless. They were the only signals in the deck list carrying meaning with no label anywhere on the surface. A vocabulary of six words gained two silent members, and a reader without the key cannot join them.
And I argued a contrast I had not measured. R4 said “filled against hollow separates them at a glance”. Built and measured: a plan’s plate is rgb(20,17,14) against a Building deck’s rgb(44,40,35) — ~39 units, near-neutral. Weakest at Draft and Building, whose plates are a text colour at 14–16% where the other four carry a hue at 18%. So the commonest WIP deck was the least legible case — the exact deck the feature was for.
The second failure is the instructive one: R3 refused a 168px figure it had only derived, then R4 shipped a contrast claim on no measurement at all. The rule this round earns: a signal that can be measured is not ruled until it has been.
The ask, restated — and why a word answers it
A 99-card deck with no committed version must not read as finished.
That is a claim about the deck, not an annotation on one. Every other such claim in this app is one of six words in one pill, and the pill was already saying something false: To buy, or Complete, on a deck nobody has sleeved.
R1’s ruling 1 is now overturned, and it should be. It refused a seventh value because the six “partition one fact”. They partition what stands between this deck and a table — and for a full, owned, uncommitted list, what stands between it and a table is that you have not built it. Same axis. Same question. It belongs in the pill.

“When would a Draft ever be pending?”
By cutting a committed deck to nothing — and that is the only way. Rare, and it is also the state where the pill lies loudest: Draft on a deck holding a committed 99-card v2.
R1’s reason for excluding it was false. It said a deck at 0 has never committed a version. It can have: built is the draft’s count, not the version’s. The exclusion is withdrawn — the mark applies to all six values.
“When would Building ever come into play?”
Constantly — it is the commonest draft there is. Cut three, add three: between the cuts and the adds the deck is at 96, which is Building. Add first and it is at 102, which is Over.
Which is the finding. One deck flips Complete → Building → Over → Complete in the course of one edit. The count pill goes transient while a draft is open — it reports the editing, not the deck. Nothing is blocked either way: state 6 already rules that a committed 101 commits fine.

Ruling 1 · the pill keeps its value
The six values partition one fact — how the matched count stands against the body size. Pending is a different axis: whether the list on screen is the list that was committed. A Complete deck with one swap is still ninety-nine resolved, owned cards.
So the pill is untouched and the fact rides beside it. Two marks, two axes, no precedence question to answer — and the status facet on /decks keeps summing to the grid.
Refused · the seventh value
pending outranking Complete
It deletes a true fact to state another one, it makes 6 of 6 a lie the moment a seventh row appears in the facet, and on a finished deck it reads as a regression from Complete when nothing regressed. One slot cannot carry two axes.

Ruling 5 · where it sits in the facet list
Not a seventh Status row. A row in that list is a value of STATUS_ORDER: it serialises to ?status=, it is what filterDecks compares statusPill against, and the heading's 6 of 6 is an assertion about the six. Ticking a seventh would OR it against the others and return a deck whose pill says something else.
Its own heading, immediately below Status — one row, Has pending changes. Adjacency says the two are read together; the boundary says they are different questions. Within a heading values OR, across headings they AND, exactly as every other facet, so Complete + pending is one tick each and an AND between them.
One boolean, not a pair. No Clean row: it is the unticked state of this one, and a two-row heading where the second is the negation of the first is a control that can say nothing. ?pending=1, absent when off, and activeFilterCount gains one — a value, which is what the phone badge counts.
Brass on the heading, and only while it is on. The other three headings are --fg-faint; this one is the single-row facet that would otherwise read as a stray checkbox.
