R1
Amendment 1 · the black mana pip · app-wide atom

A black disc on a near-black canvas is a hole, not a swatch.

--b: #1E1B1A is declared identically in all six themes and every theme's chrome sits within a hair of it. Measured against the three grounds a pip actually renders on, black scores 1.01:1 to 1.27:1. There is nothing subtle about that number: the pip is not dim, it is absent, and it has been absent on all 22 reads of it, across 15 component files, since the theme system landed.

The call is a lifted, hued value, once, and no ring. --b becomes #98748F — a dark violet-grey, one hex, six declarations, the same value in every theme, exactly as the other four WUBRG swatches already are. R3 revision: R1 lifted to a neutral #837D78 and the review found it reads as a second white at pip sizes. R2 added violet at #857A93 — and the R2 audit found that a shipped test gates this: contract.test.ts:145 asserts ΔE ≥ 0.1 between every WUBRG pip and every theme's accent, and R2 measured 0.087 against Oil and Iron. R3 pushes the hue on to #98748F, which clears it at 0.115 on both binding themes. It clears 3:1 against the lightest ground a pip lands on and carries the theme's own --bg as its glyph ink at 4.5:1, which is what lets PIP_TEXT die instead of spreading.

The brief's constraint, drawn as written
One solution, every surface, all six themes, a single token change — not per-surface patches. So: no --pip-text, no --pip-ring, no sibling-aware primitive, no per-site override, and PIP_TEXT deleted rather than promoted. The 36-key contract keeps 36 keys and one of them changes value.
R2 → R3 — “the new pip reads almost identical to white”, and the test that gates the fix
The complaint is not about contrast — R1's grey sits 3.35:1 below --w and ΔE 0.34 away from it. It is about which axis carries the difference. Three of the five pips are separated from white by hue; R1 separated black from white by lightness alone, and at 9–12px with no glyph two hueless discs are two pale dots of different brightness. The fix keeps the lightness and adds the hue back — L 0.2115 against R1's 0.2085, so the contrast ledger moves by at most 0.03. How much hue is not a taste question: contract.test.ts:145 asserts ΔE ≥ 0.1 between every WUBRG pip and every theme's accent, iterated over all five pips and all six themes. R2's #857A93 measured 0.087 against Oil and Iron and 0.092 against Rune Frost — two failing themes, and R1's neutral failed the same gate harder at 0.064. R1
Amendment 2 · update a deck by re-import · new flow

Import creates. Update replaces. The difference is that update can delete.

The shipped importer says so in a source comment: “there is deliberately no dedupe — updating in place is sync, a different and much larger feature, and quietly becoming it is the failure mode this comment guards against.” This amendment is that feature, built deliberately rather than by accident. Everything about the shape follows from one fact: a first import risks nothing, and an update can remove cards the player placed by hand.

So: the diff is always shown and nothing is written until it is confirmed — the one confirmation step in a product that has refused every other one, and it is earned by the deletions. Removals are the centre of the screen, not a footnote: they are counted, listed, individually keepable, and the ones the player added by hand are called out as such. The list is the source of truth — default is remove, because a player pasting a 106-line list is telling you what the deck is.

Read README.md for the decisions log, STATES-reimport.md for the state matrix (18 states, both widths), deck-reimport · copy strings.ts for production copy, READINESS.md for what blocks a green. Mock deck is continuous with the siblings: Kykar Spellslinger at 91 built, and the pasted list resolves to 19 in · 7 out · 84 unchanged · 3 we cannot name, one of which the player keeps — landing the deck at 104, which is amendment 3's Over state on purpose.

entry point
Both — the deck card's ⋯ menu and a ghost control beside Export in the builder spine. Same string, one route.
a diff first?
Always. Nothing is written until Apply. The only confirm step in the product, earned by the fact that this one deletes.
how removals read
Listed, not counted. Every removal is a row with a Keep toggle; hand-added ones are named as hand-added. No strikethrough, no red.
arrival
The shipped strip's geometry, delta copy instead of total copy: 19 in, 6 out. Its own param, its own dismissal, so it fires on the fifth update as well as the first.
celebration
Suppressed, on exactly the rule that suppresses it for a first import and for a fill: the player did not place the last card, a list did.
What update deliberately cannot do
It cannot change the commander. A list naming a different commander is a conflict state with two ways out, and neither is a swap: a commander change re-decides the colour identity and the legality of all ninety-nine, which is a new deck wearing an old name. It cannot merge (there is no “add these and keep everything” mode — that is the quick-add bar, one card at a time, and it already exists). It cannot rename the deck. And it never runs unattended: no auto-sync, no polling, no remembered source.

Why this order
Out first. It is the only group with a decision in it and the only one that can lose the player something. A diff that opens with its additions is a diff that buries its deletions.
In second, and it is a list, not a decision. No per-row toggle: refusing individual additions turns update into a curated merge, which is the quick-add bar's job. Its header carries the owned · to buy split, because nineteen new cards can cost money and that is the shipped vocabulary for saying so.
Unnamed third. Not a failure — the shipped importer already treats an unmatched line as a resolvable row on the deck, and this reuses it whole, including the #1A160F plate and dashed border.
Unchanged last, collapsed. Eighty-five rows carrying no decision, above the one control that matters, would be the screen. One disclosure, and it is the only group that has to be asked for.
The counts are chips, not a table. Same construct as the facet strip, and the 91 → 104 / 99 figure on the right is the only place the whole screen is summarised as one number.

H · what Apply actually writes — the five calls that are not visual
1 · the added cards are not auto
auto means the app put this here — it is what Clear all auto sweeps and what the fill controls write. A card the player's own list names is the player's choice, so it lands with auto = false, exactly as a first import's cards do.
2 · and auto is cleared on what the list claims
A card the app placed that the player's list now names is no longer the app's suggestion — it is in the deck on purpose. Apply clears the flag on every unchanged card the list names, which means Clear all auto afterwards will not delete cards the player just declared. Drawn in the diff: Ponder still wears auto in the removals because the list did not name it.
3 · unresolved rows are replaced, not merged
The new list's unnamed lines replace the deck's existing ones inside the same transaction. Merging them accumulates stale rows the player already dealt with, and a line number from an old paste points into a file nobody has any more. The diff says so in the group's own sentence: they replace the 2 unresolved lines this deck is carrying now.
4 · kept cards are exempt and stay exactly as they were
A kept card is not re-added and not re-flagged: its row is left alone, auto included. If the app placed it and the player keeps it against their own list, it is still the app's suggestion — and the sweep can still take it.
5 · one transaction, and no dedupe of updates
Adds, removes and unresolved rows all land together or none do — the same rule and the same reason the shipped importDeck writes a deck and its unresolved rows in one. And the double-submit guard is the shipped one: a synchronous ref, because pending only turns true on the next render and two clicks in one tick would apply the same update twice.
What is deliberately not written
No import history and no stored source. Nothing records that this deck came from a list, or which one — provenance is a fact about one navigation, which is the shipped arrival.ts ruling and it holds here. No undo of an applied update, and that is the gap this handoff would most like a ruling on — the diff is the confirmation, and it is the only one.

Amendment 3 · decks over 99 · /decks + builder

Over is a stage of building, so it is drawn in the building colour.

Lifting the cap means a list can arrive at 104 and be trimmed back. Nothing about that is an error — overfilling then cutting is how a deck actually gets built — so the over state takes no --danger, no role="alert", no banner and no blocked control. It takes --warning, which is the colour Building already wears, and it says how many to cut.

Three changes carry it. The count keeps its denominator and prints 104 / 99 with a +5. The bar re-scales to the total, so the 99 stays green and the overflow is drawn to scale in amber — the amber segment is the number of cards to cut. The pill gains a fifth value, Over, and Complete narrows to exactly 99.

Read README.md for the decisions log, STATES-over-99.md for the state matrix across both surfaces, over-99 · copy strings.ts for production copy, READINESS.md for what blocks a green. Mock deck is continuous with the sibling handoffs: Kykar, Wind's Fury, Jeskai, 104 built · 96 owned · 8 to buy.

The one thing not to get wrong
A deck at 104 is not broken, and the app must not act as though it is. It is exportable, it is playable-adjacent, its claims on your collection are as hard as any finished deck's, and the player did it on purpose. Every treatment below was picked by asking whether it would read as "you have work to do" or as "something has gone wrong". Red, alerts, disabled buttons and warning triangles all read as the second one.

Trimming back — 99 is a moment, and it survives
Ninety-nine.
That is a deck. Every slot spoken for, and you cut it down to size yourself.

D · what the lifted cap touches everywhere else
The hard stop, and the soft ones that stay
add-validation.ts:42's if (deck.built >= 99) return "deck-full" is the cap, and it goes. Every other stop stays. The two fill controls, the 68-card fill and every suggestion path stop at 99 as they do now — the app never overfills a deck on the player's behalf, because it cannot know which five they meant to cut. planLastSlots needs no edit: openSlots <= 0 already returns an empty plan, so at 104 the last-31 strip unmounts on its own. Only a human add and an import can exceed 99.
Dead copy
mutation-result.ts:18 — “This deck is already at 99 cards, so there's no room for another.” — becomes unreachable, along with its test. Deleted, not reworded. A rejection message for a rejection that no longer happens is the kind of string that comes back as a bug report two years later. deck-full leaves the DeckAddRejection union with it.
Availability — unchanged, and that is a decision
MATURE_DECK_SIZE = 99 gates whether a deck's claims on your collection are hard or soft, and it reads built >= 99. An over deck therefore claims hard, which is right: 104 sleeved cards are 104 cards you cannot put in another deck. Do not narrow that comparison to === 99 to match the pill. The pill is about legality; this is about physical cards.
Export — enabled, and it says so
Export is disabled at built === 0 and nowhere else, and that does not change. A 104-card list exports as a 104-card list — the player is likely pasting it somewhere to do the cutting. One line is added inside the export dialog when the deck is over: “This list is 104 cards. Five over what a Commander deck can hold.” Stating a fact in the one place the list leaves the app is not the same as refusing to let it leave.
No numeric ceiling at all
There is no 120, no 200, no “sane maximum”. A ceiling would need a number nobody can justify, and the failure it guards against — someone pasting a 400-line collection export into a deck — is already handled by the import's own byte caps and, past that, reads correctly: a bar that is 25% green and 75% amber is an accurate picture of what just happened. The bar's re-scaling is what makes an unbounded cap safe to draw, because it never overflows its track no matter the total.
Accessibility
The progress bar's aria-valuemax becomes Math.max(99, built) — a valuenow of 104 against a max of 99 is an invalid range and some readers clamp it to 100%. aria-label becomes “104 cards, 5 over the 99”. The two amber segments are never the only signal: the pill is a word, the trim line is a sentence, and 5 to cut is text. No live region — the player performed the add that took it over.

The commander stops being the one card in the app with its own modal.
