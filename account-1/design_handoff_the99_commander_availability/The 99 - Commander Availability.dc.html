R1
Commander availability · owned is not available · feat/commander-availability-brief

The dot says owned. The marker says what that is worth.

Five readings, three geometries, and no new vocabulary anywhere. The chip and the coverage row take the count-only marker /collection already ships; the synergy row takes one AvailabilitySlot before its toggle, with a priced tag on the rows you do not own. Nothing is drawn for a card that is free — which is why the most important artboard on this canvas is the one where nothing happens.

The four open items resolve inward, not outward. The aria coupling needs no third marker option, because on this page the marker is not the speaker — the chip's own aria-label is, and it always was. The legend does not need a replacement key set for five readings, because four of the five now print their own words; three keys become one. Both answers are subtractions, and both leave a free player's page byte-identical.

Read README.md for the decisions log and the gap report, STATES-commander-availability.md for the readings as a table, commander-availability · copy strings.ts for the strings and the reuse manifest, READINESS.md for what R1 could not verify. Read at feat/commander-availability-brief and main — precedence
The brief was read in full on its branch. The eight rulings in §3 are drawn as written and not reopened — including that this page draws the app's existing five readings and invents no sixth, that the dot drops to ownership-only with the marker carrying severity, that Role Coverage takes a count-only marker and never a deck name, that the committed border tint retires, and that the synergy panel's unowned rows take a priced tag. availability-marker.tsx, coverage-chip.tsx, coverage-section.tsx, synergy-row.tsx, buy-tag.tsx, copy/availability.ts and copy/commander-explained.ts were read at main, and every geometry figure below is quoted from the component that ships it. Where a component and this handoff disagree, the component wins.
Nothing here changes what a reading means. The marker is AvailabilityMarker density="row" nameless, the tag is BuyTag variant="row", the dot is OwnershipDot, the slot is update-diff-row.tsx:225's pattern. No new marker option is asked for and no new component: the four open items are answered with one copy change, one deletion, and two type rulings — see the ledger in the README. All card names, deck names, counts and prices below are fabricated mock data; reproduce the shape of the string, never the value.
4.1 · the aria coupling
No divergence to ratify, and no third option. The chip is a button with a computed aria-label; the marker inside it draws count-only and stays silent. The deck name rides the label it already rides — now through markerAria rather than a committedTo suffix.
4.2 · the legend
Three keys become one. Four of the five readings print glyph + number + word, so they legend themselves; the dot is the only mark on this page that is a colour without a word. One key, two forms, and the form a free player sees is the string that ships today.
4.3 · the sort sub-line
Four buckets in the four words the app already owns — the /collection filter's. One sentence, no counts, severity order left to right, and it stays true for a player with nothing claimed.
4.4 · marker type on the chip
The marker is a figure, so it takes the figure's size — mono 10.5px on the chip, 11.5px on the phone row, glued to the name at 6px and left of the plate. The brief's ~40px estimate does not survive measurement: see the measured table and the gap report.

why the key line survives verbatim
The honest word for an ownership-only dot is Owned. — but shipping that unconditionally repaints a free player's page for no gain. So the key keeps both forms and picks by what is on screen, which is the mechanism DotKey already uses for its second entry. No marker anywhere → today's string, byte for byte. Any marker anywhere → Owned., because "and available" is now false of a neighbouring chip.
the four things that must not appear
No tick, no free word, no empty span, no reserved width on the chip or the coverage row. The synergy slot is the one exception and it is a column, not a mark — 88px of nothing, reserved on every row so the toggle does not shuffle. A column that is empty on every row of a free player's list is invisible; a column that appears on row nine is not.
and the sort still sorts
A free player can still pick Availability. Every card lands in one bucket, the order is the tie-break's, and the sub-line stays true. An option that does nothing is better than an option that vanishes — the same call /collection made for its zero-count filter rows.
§3 §4.1 the aria coupling — ruled: there is nothing to diverge from

The item asks for a visible count-only label with a spoken deck name, and reads that as a departure from the coupled contract in card-availability.md §2/§3. It is not one, because the marker is not what speaks on this surface. markerAria is only consulted when a caller passes spoken, and the marker's own documentation names the two callers that don't: surfaces whose enclosing control already carries a full accessible name. The chip is a third instance of that case, and the coverage row and the synergy row are the fourth and fifth.

The chip is a CardDetailTrigger — a button whose aria-label replaces everything beneath it, computed today by chipAria and already ending in Already in ${deck.name}. So the deck name does not need to travel through the marker: it is already on the label, and it stays there. What changes is the sentence that produces it — one clause about committedTo becomes four clauses about availability, and the copy for them is markerAria's, called name-bearing, off to the side of a marker drawn nameless. Two calls, one source of words, no new option.

what the coupling actually forbids
That one component draw a count and speak a name from the same call — because then an edit to the drawn form silently reshapes the spoken one, which is the bug R5 §8 fixed by giving the count-only forms their own keys. Two calls with two explicit argument sets are not that. The marker here is a drawn figure with no voice; the sentence is the button's, where a screen-reader user was already getting it.
refused — the chip loses the spoken name
A regression against what ships, for a rule that does not bind here. It would also make the card modal the only place a screen-reader user can learn which deck holds the copy — two taps behind a fact the sighted reader gets in a sentence.
refused — a third marker option
A speakName flag would let a future caller draw a count and announce a deck on a surface with nothing behind it that names decks — the exact case nameless exists to prevent, on eight surfaces, forever. Paying that to solve a problem this page does not have is the worst of the three.
the one real cost, named
explainedCopy.committed.ariaSuffix retires with committedTo, and chipAria / rowAria import markerAria instead. Three call sites, one deleted string, no signature change on the marker. The suffix still lands last, after the figure clause, and a free card still produces the shipped name byte for byte.
§4 §4.2 the legend — three keys become one, and the one has two forms

Three keys cannot cover five readings without counting, and the no-counts rule holds. The way out is that four of the five readings stop needing a key at all. Every marker is glyph + number + word by contract, and the priced tag leads with the word buy. 1 in a brew legends itself; so does none free, short 2, and silence. No other surface in the app legends the marker — not the decklist, not /collection, not the roll-up — and this page does not get to be the exception.

What is left is the dot: the one mark on this page that is a colour with no word beside it. It gets the key, and only where it has two states to tell apart — which is the synergy panel. Role Coverage is drawn from the owned pool, so every dot in all four panels is the same dot; a key above them would be explaining a constant, which is the no-counts rule's sibling.

§5 §4.4 marker type and spacing — the marker is a figure, so it takes the figure's size

Card-availability §3.4 rules that density rides the meta line, and the chip has no meta line. It does have a figure, and the marker is the same class of thing — a count with a glyph on it. So it takes the figure plate's size, not the name's: mono 10.5px / 600 / 0.02em against an 11.5px name, and mono 11.5px on the phone row against a 13.5px name. Weight 600 where the plate is 400, because the plate has a filled ground to give it presence and the marker has none. update-diff-row.tsx:259 answered the same question the same way — an explicit size, matched to its neighbour.

Spacing: the marker is glued to the name, not to the plate. Name and marker sit in one inline-flex gap-6 group, so the chip's shipped gap: 8 falls between that group and the figure — one 6px join inside a clause, the 8px column gap outside it. Padding, height and radius are untouched: 0 4px 0 10px, 28px, 999px. The phone row keeps gap: 9, its 44px floor and its single line; the marker is flex:none and the name is what gives way.

⚠ the brief's §3 estimate is low
Ruling 3 says the chip grows “roughly 40px” in non-free states. Measured, it is 69–89px, because 40px buys about four mono characters and the shortest count-only form is seven. The decision is unaffected and stands — the name-bearing alternative is 120px and truncates — but the cost figure it was argued on should be corrected before anyone re-derives a chip-per-line budget from it. A contended 12-chip panel falls from about five chips per line to three, not four. Recorded as gap 1.

Dot, name, marker, plate. Severity reads inside the clause; the figure closes the pill. Same order on the phone row, where the figure is already right-aligned.
§6 §4.3 the Availability sort sub-line — four buckets, one sentence, no counts

The shipped sub-line describes two buckets and there are four. The rewrite borrows the four words the app already owns — Free, Wanted by a brew, In use, Over-committed, the /collection filter's own labels — rather than inventing a fifth vocabulary for one menu row. Severity order, left to right, which is also the comparator's order, so the sentence is a description of the sort and not a promise about it.

why the filter's words and not the marker's
The marker's words are per card and carry a number — none free, short 2. A sub-line naming buckets needs the class names, and the app has exactly one set of those: the four the Availability filter prints. Reusing them also means a reader who has met the filter has met this sentence.
one sentence, and no subNoFigures twin
Most played needs a second form because a panel with no EDHREC figures is in pool order and the option would be lying. Availability has no such case: every owned card is in exactly one of the four buckets, always. When the availability read is down the option is not drawn at all, which is the shipped rule and needs no line.
refused — “free first, then by how spoken for.”
Shorter, and it hides the thing the sort is for. A reader picking this order wants to know where the over-committed cards end up, and a sentence that says “by how spoken for” makes them find out by scrolling. Four buckets named is fifteen words; it fits the row at both widths.
