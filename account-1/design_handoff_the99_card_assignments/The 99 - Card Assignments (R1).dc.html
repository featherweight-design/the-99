R1
Assigning a contested copy · post-import summary + the contested row

Giving a copy moves money. Pulling changes a deck.

Two actions sit on one surface and a player must never read one as the other. Pull it reaches into another deck's sleeves, takes the card out and cuts that deck a new version — its list is different afterwards and there is no undo. Giving a newly-arrived copy to a deck changes no list and cuts no version. It decides which committed deck sleeves a piece of cardboard that has just turned up, and it is one tap to change back.

So R1 refuses to give the new action a verb-button. Anything shaped like Pull it and sitting beside it will be read as its sibling. Instead the deck names are the control — a row of committed decks, each stating what it gets, under a heading that says what the tap does. A chooser cannot be mistaken for a button that takes something away. See 1.

And the word claim never appears in the interface. It is engineering's word (revalidateClaims) and in a player's ear it is adversarial — it sounds like taking a card off somebody, which is exactly what Pull it does and this does not. A word that sounds like a pull is the one failure this feature cannot afford.

The second thing R1 fixes is a silence. Today a collection replace bounces back to /collection and every consequence for every deck happens off-screen. The summary is the receipt for the app's most destructive action, and it shows even when there is nothing to report — a redirect after a typed confirmation leaves a player unsure it happened at all. See 2.

Spec docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-14-card-assignments-design.md is not in the project — R1 is built from the brief plus the shipped vocabulary in deck-versioning.ts, undo-removal and the collection replace flow. One clause of the brief is contradicted on purpose — see 6. README · matrix · copy · READINESS.

The whole vocabulary, on one line each
Heading, not a verb-button: Give the new copy to — plural Give the 2 new copies to.
What each deck gets: gets it · gets 1 of 2 · buys it · $32.90.
The fixed holder: commander · keeps it. No control, because no tap could move it.
Your choice, marked: a brass your call tag and a back to first committed link. Nothing is marked while the default stands.
The default, named: first committed wins, with each deck's commit date on its own line so the order is legible rather than asserted.
Untouched: Pull it, its border, its pronoun, its guards and Pulling…. R1 changes no shipped control.
Rejected words, each for one reason: Claim it — sounds like a pull · Take it — sounds like a pull, harder · Reserve · Assign — clerical, and neither says a deck sleeves cardboard · Mine — every card is yours; that is the premise of the app.

The sentence a player has to be able to say back
“Pulling takes the card off another deck.” True, and it is the only one of the two that can make somebody else's deck illegal, short, or newly expensive.
“Giving decides who sleeves the new one. Somebody pays either way.” True, and the row shows which somebody, in money, before the tap.
Neither sentence contains the other's verb, and neither control carries the other's shape. That is the whole mitigation — no tooltip, no explainer modal, no first-run coach mark.

Every refusal sentence does four things, in this order: names the deck and card, gives the cause, says nothing moved, and states what is true now. The third clause is the one the brief demanded and the one a player will actually look for — “nothing moved” is not the same news as “that failed”.
The row re-reads from the server before it draws the refusal, so the options above the message are current rather than the stale set that was tapped. In (a) the deck that dropped its claim is gone from the list and the count corrects to 1 deck wants it; in (b) the arrival count corrects to 1 copy arrived. A refusal that leaves wrong numbers on screen is a worse silence than the one we started with.
Only (b) invites a retry, because only (b) can succeed later. In (a) the deck has stopped wanting the card and the row is about to disappear, so a retry would be an invitation to fail; the message says the row is leaving instead. Neither offers a button — the options are still there, and tapping one is the retry.
The message persists until the row changes or the page reloads. No toast, no timeout: a refusal that vanishes on its own is the silence the brief names, wearing a costume. It clears when the player taps any option in that row, and one live-region announcement is made per refusal, word-for-word the sentence on screen.

6 · Where R1 contradicts the brief

one clause, and it is load-bearing
“Claiming a copy changes nothing about the other deck — its list, its version and its own buy state are untouched; only the allocation of a physical copy moves.”
List and version: true, and R1 says so everywhere. Buy state: it cannot be. The feature's own premise is that a deck bills a card it lists and does not own. Move the copy away and that deck lists a card it does not own — so it bills it. Buy state is derived from allocation; it is the same derivation this whole feature is about.
Drawing the clause as written would make the row lie about money. A player would give a copy to one deck, see nothing change elsewhere, and find a purchase on another deck's page later — the exact silent-consequence bug in the ticket, reintroduced by the fix.
So R1 draws the trade, and turns it into the strongest thing on the surface: gets it and buys it side by side, moving together on one tap. It is also the cleanest way to separate the two verbs — giving moves money between decks, pulling changes what a deck is.
A ratification question, not a Design call, if the clause was literal. If allocation really is meant to be invisible to buy state, then a deck's buy count stops describing what the player must purchase, every money figure on this page is wrong, and the row should carry no money at all — which would leave the chooser with no stated consequence and no reason for a player to care which deck wins. R1 assumes the charitable reading: the clause means no list is edited and no version is cut.

8 · The contract

one route, one component, one new chip
The whole inventory
ContestedCardRow
Card name + mono supply line, a brass uppercase heading, and one option per committed claimant. Radio semantics — role=radiogroup labelled by the heading, options 44px desktop / 52px phone, with the selected option also being the clear control. Used unchanged on both surfaces.
Outcome words
Mono 11px. gets it / gets n of m · buys $x / buys it · $x / keeps it. Always a word before a hue, never a hue alone.
your call · Back to first committed
Brass plate on mono 10px, and a borderless brass link at 34px / 44px target. Both exist only while a pick is live.
Refusal note
In-row, --danger left rule on #1A1310, full-opacity ink, no icon, no dismiss. Two causes, two strings, one shape.
Summary route + still short chip
One new page, and one chip added to the shipped review strip. That is the only edit to a shipped surface in R1.
Not in R1
No new hex, no new font size, no modal, no toast, no confirm, no tooltip, no spinner, no drag, no bulk allocation, no motion, no first-run explainer, no onboarding of the two verbs, no change to Pull it or either of its guards, and no use of the word claim in any string.
Notes for engineering
A pick is a reservation row, not a deck edit. It must not touch deck_cards, must not create a version, and must not appear in a deck's history. If it can ever produce a version, the copy on this canvas is false.
Picking the held deck clears the reservation and the default reasserts itself — it is not a write of “first committed” as an explicit choice. A cleared row must be indistinguishable from one never touched.
Refusals are two cases and the response must say which: the claimant no longer asks for the card, or supply is exhausted. A generic failure is not enough to draw either sentence — the row would have to fall back to the transport treatment and the brief's requirement would go unmet.
A refusal response carries the row's fresh state (arrivals, claimants, allocation) so the row can redraw correct numbers with the message. One round trip, not two.
Optimistic on success, authoritative on refusal. Outcome words move on tap; a refusal replaces them with server truth. The displaced deck's outcome must be computed client-side on the optimistic hop, or the trade will lag the tap that caused it.
Seniority is the existing since ordering (versions.ts:175), and the commit date shown per option is the fact that ordering is derived from. If they can disagree, the row is misleading and the date must be dropped rather than approximated.
Commander claims come from the availability model and are the same fixed holders Pull it's third guard reads. No second source of truth.
The summary reads the upload, not live state. Its two money sections are the delta that import produced and must not drift as the player picks; only the contested section is live. A receipt that rewrites itself is not a receipt.
The route survives reload and stays keyed by uploadId. It replaces the bounce to /collection and renders for an empty result.
Two live-region messages, never more: one per settled pick (Lotleth Aggro gets Fauna Shaman. Sidisi Graveyard buys it, $32.90.) and one per refusal, verbatim. No motion anywhere.
Focus after a pick stays on the option tapped — it is a radio group and the tapped option is now both the selection and the way back. Focus after a refusal goes to the message.
Card-row parity · gaps
Name + one availability slot: held. The chooser is a panel beneath the row, not an occupant of it, and no decklist row grows or changes template.
Words, never colour alone: every outcome is a word first. Signed money in both summary sections; the hue only repeats the sign.
Gap — no bulk allocation. Ten contested cards is ten choices. Correct first shipment, and the obvious second want.
Gap — the summary has no windowing. The money sections truncate to four; the contested section does not, and a pathological import could make it long.
Gap — two players, one collection is out of scope and refusal (b) is the seam where it would show.
Gap — review.lede is wrong by one again on /changes: “nothing here is a decision except the pulls” now omits two. Flagged, not edited — a ratification question, as in #390.
