R2
Assigning a contested copy · read against the spec

Giving a copy moves money. Pulling changes a deck.

R1 was drawn without the spec, which was in the repo and not in this project's tree. The central call survives: Pull it cuts another deck a version; giving a newly-arrived copy touches no list and cuts no version, so the new action gets no verb-button — the deck names are the control, under a heading that says what the tap does. The spec asks for exactly this and says so: “Distinguishing them is copy on a new surface, and therefore a Design call.”

What the spec changed is everything around it. The summary carries no prices and no totals at all, which collapsed its money sections to two; contention and demand answer different questions about the same card and the copy is forbidden from implying otherwise; there are four refusal causes, one of which R1 had drawn as impossible; a commander can be a fixed holder and a pickable claimant on the same row; brews claim but cannot be listed; and the second door is two surfaces, not one.

And R1's frame 6 is deleted outright. It argued that the brief's “the other deck's buy state is untouched” could not be true. The spec agrees with the drawing and never made the claim: “The losing deck does not change. Its committed version still lists the card, it still bills the purchase, and no new version is cut.” R1 spent a frame arguing with a paraphrase. The trade stays drawn; the argument for it goes.

Source: docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-14-card-assignments-design.md at main, plus lib/copy/availability.ts and ui/availability-marker.tsx — which is where the vocabulary rulings in 1 come from. Diff against R1 · README · matrix · copy · READINESS.

What reading the spec changed — nine items
1 · Frame 6 deleted. The spec says the losing deck still bills the purchase. R1 argued for that against a clause nobody wrote.
2 · The diff's third class is a reason on a row, not a third section. It classifies each pair gone, reduced, or raised — and with no prices, reduced and raised tell a player the same thing. Two sections, and the class survives as 1 of 2 arrived. See 2.
3 · Two demands, one card. Money reads draft demand; contention reads committed claims. They can disagree, and the spec forbids copy that implies they don't. New frame 6.
4 · Four refusal causes, three drawable — including the card is that deck's own commander, which R1 had ruled unreachable. A stale payload reaches it.
5 · Commander and list copy on one row. A folded claim is part commander, part list card; only the commander half is protected. R1's “the whole row goes inert” was too broad.
6 · Brews claim and cannot be listed. Assignments are refused for decks with no committed version, so the supply line must not count them. One new line does it honestly.
7 · The second door is two surfaces — /decks/[id]/changes and the deck page marker. R1 drew one. See 7.
8 · in the box and spoken for both retired. The availability block bans asserting where a card physically is, and spoken for already means over-committed. See 1.
9 · holds / wants considered and rejected for the outcome words — they are the app's finished vs unfinished pair and would collide. 1.
Carried from R1 unchanged: no verb-button · the deck names are the control · the word claim appears in no string · nothing is marked while the default stands · two ways back · optimistic on success, authoritative on refusal · the summary renders for an empty result · commit order, never re-sorted, own deck never promoted · the contested section is never truncated · no motion.

Every deck named, no button, and a round mark per deck. The mark is the app's own single-select affordance, adopted verbatim from filter-panel.tsx, whose ruling is the reason it is here: “the shape is the affordance, and a square tick on a one-of-many list promises you can have two.” Without it the row read as a table — removing the button solved the confusion with Pull it and left nothing saying tap me. See 1b.
Four vocabulary rulings, three of them read out of the shipped copy
The word claim appears in no string. It is engineering's word (revalidateClaims, senior_hard) and in a player's ear it is adversarial — it sounds like taking a card off somebody, which is what Pull it does and this does not. A word that sounds like a pull is the one failure this feature cannot afford.
No sentence says where a card physically is. availability.ts: “we describe LISTS, never boxes, binders or sleeves.” So R1's cards in the box heading is out — it is in your collection — and no copy here is sleeved, cardboard, or in the shop. Flagged: the shipped review.bucketFreeNote says “in the box already”, so the rule is already broken once on a neighbouring surface.
spoken for is retired from this feature. spokenForTwice ships on two surfaces and means over-committed. R1's The copy is spoken for heading would have made one phrase mean two things one route apart. It reads Nothing to choose here.
holds / wants is the right-shaped pair and the wrong one. detailClaimHolds / detailClaimWants carry the app's whole model in two verbs — finished lists hold, unfinished lists want. Borrowing them for allocation would make wants it mean committed but lost the copy on this row and not committed at all on the next. So allocation gets its own two words: gets it and buys it. Third axis, third pair.
One word is borrowed, and deliberately: still short, from marker.over's short N. A contested arrival is the over state, so the section heading and the marker say the same thing about the same card.
And one sentence carries the default rule, once. The deck that committed the card first gets it, unless you say otherwise. It sits on the contested section, which is the only place the rule is explained — and unless you say otherwise is the whole licence to change it. It says committed the card first rather than the oldest deck on purpose: the ordering is per card, so a newer deck can outrank an older one on a card it sleeved earlier.

The mark states allocation; the accent fill and the footer sentence state authorship. That is what lets the group have a current value without making seniority look like a preference the player expressed — the ruling R1 got right and nearly drew itself out of.
More than one mark can be filled, and only one can be accent. Two copies and three decks means two allocated; a pick reserves one copy for one deck. So aria-checked is true for the accent option only, the neutral fills are aria-hidden, and each option's outcome carries the allocation into its accessible name.
16px desktop / 18px phone, gap 11 / 12, 1px ring, inner dot 6 / 7px — filter-panel.tsx's own geometry, unchanged. No new size and no new shape.
The shape follows the cap, and the cap is the number of copies that arrived. One copy is a one-of-many, so the mark is round and ticking a second deck moves the first. Two copies means a player really can give two decks a copy, so the mark is square and the heading carries the limit: 2 of 2 given. That is filter-panel.tsx's own logic rather than a departure from it — the component carries both modes and chooses by selectionMode. Its ruling is that a square tick promises you can have two; above one copy, you can.
R2 ruled this round-everywhere and wrote down the condition that would void it. That condition was “if a player is ever allowed to allocate several copies across decks in one gesture” — and two copies arriving at once is that gesture. The model always supported it: card_assignments is one row per deck, so N copies is N reservations of one copy each. The cap is what keeps the square tick honest — unlimited, it would promise three.
And no stepper, because no deck is ever given two. A deck wanting three lands takes one tick like every other claimant; the chooser allocates copies across decks, never within one. That is what keeps the row free of quantities — and it is why gets 1 of 2 is gone.
The your call plate is withdrawn, and that is a subtraction worth stating. A picked row carried five signals for one bit of state: the accent border, the accent mark fill, the plate, the footer sentence, and Back to first committed. The plate was the weakest of the five. Its stated job was words, never colour alone — but the footer sentence already is that word carrier, and a better one: it names the deck. The plate said something here was chosen; the sentence says you chose Kinnan Ramp.
And it cost width where there was least. A flex:none plate ate the deck name's share in the 345px deck-page panel, competing with this deck — the narrowest place the row appears.
The trade: the footer is now required wherever the chooser appears, including both doors in 7. Dropping the plate without that would have left authorship on hue alone on the two panels that did not draw a footer — the rule this feature will not break. Four carriers, one of them words, on every surface.
One deliberate divergence, and it is a contrast one. The shipped empty ring is --border-dashed, which measures under 1.5:1 on a row ground. That is defensible inside a dialog the player has just opened; this chooser sits inline on a page of receipts, where the mark is the only thing inviting a tap. So the ring takes --fg-faint — 4.72:1 in Low Sun, and a text-ramp token that clears the floor in all six themes by construction, so there is no minted hex and no per-theme tuning. The divergence is from --border-dashed to a token one family over, not from the palette — see 9.

Two sections, because without prices there were only ever two answers. The diff classifies three ways — cleared, reduced, raised — but reduced and raised tell a player the same thing: this deck still has to buy this card. So the UI groups by what the player must do and keeps the classification as a quiet reason on the row (1 of 2 arrived / not in the new list). Three headings for two outcomes was the arithmetic showing through the interface.
No prices anywhere on this page, and no totals at all. A cleared purchase has no honest figure — the app cannot know what was paid, or whether anything was, since a card can arrive from a booster, a trade or a gift. And a figure for what is still owed is real but not actionable here: this is a receipt, and the place to act on a price is the deck's Shopping list. So the page says which cards and which decks, and points at where the money lives.
Dropping the totals fixed a defect rather than only simplifying. A right-aligned figure on a heading rule, directly under the previous section's last row, reads as that list's bottom total — found in review on the seam where the upper list had no Show all row between them. Spacing reduced it; having nothing to misread removes it.
Nothing on this page needs saving, and it says so beside the only button. Every pick writes immediately; Done is a route, not a commit. A page of consequences must not look like a form, or a player reads the whole replace as still pending.
A brew that wants the card is named and excluded, in one line. The spec refuses assignments to decks with no committed version, so listing a brew would invite a certain refusal — but 2 committed decks beside a shortage of three claimants would be a count that quietly lies. The supply line counts committed decks and says the word, and the footnote carries the rest.
Money sections truncate at four; the contested section never truncates. A ~2,900-row upload can clear dozens of purchases and this page is a receipt to be read — but the contested rows are the only part that asks for anything.
The money sections are frozen and the contested section is live. The spec stores the diff in summary_payload precisely so the decision survives a reload; a pick redraws its own row and must not move a figure above it. A receipt that rewrites itself is not a receipt.

6 · Two demands, one card

the spec's copy constraint, drawn

The spec is blunt about this and R1 missed it entirely: “The two figures can legitimately disagree about a card, and the summary's copy must not imply otherwise.”

The two money sections — draft demand
loadDeckBuyRows · built from live deck_cards
They include a deck's draft adds and exclude its pending removals, even when its committed version is untouched. That is the right source, because they answer the question the deck page bills: what will this deck have to buy.
Still short — committed claims
loadAvailability · version-shaped, on purpose
Contention is a question about hard claims, and an assignment can only be written against a card in a deck's newest committed version. A draft-only ask must never displace a hard claimant — “the precise thing seniority exists to prevent.”
So the copy is built to survive the disagreement — four rules
1 · Money sections say to buy; the contested section says committed. Different verbs for different questions, on every heading and every note. No section ever says both.
2 · No section is described as a subset of another, and none claims to be exhaustive. A card in Still short may or may not appear above it, and the page never implies which.
3 · Where a card does appear in both, it says so — the one cross-reference on the page, on the contested row, pointing up. Silence there is what would look like a bug.
4 · The header's money clause is scoped to decks, not to cards. “What moved is what your decks will have to buy” — never a total, never a count of cards affected, because the two reads would each produce a different one.
The case that makes it concrete, and it is on the canvas above. Cavern of Souls is in Still to buy for Atraxa Counters at 1 of 2 arrived, and in Still short with three decks needing it. Both are true and they are different questions — one counts a deck's draft demand, the other counts committed claims — which is why the row carries the link and the page carries no sums.

One amendment is required, and it is a real one. availability-marker.tsx is emphatic that the marker “has no hover, no focus ring and is never a tap target”. Making it a disclosure contradicts a shipped ruling and needs ratifying — it is the one place R2 asks to change a shipped component.
Why not a second control beside it: the card-row floor is name + one availability slot, and the marker is in it. A second affordance on a decklist row is exactly what that floor exists to refuse. Only the over state is ever a trigger — free renders nothing and soft/hard have nothing to allocate.
The deck page takes the two-line option at every width, and that is a ruling, not a fallback. This panel hangs inside the decklist column, 345px — within a hair of the phone option's own 336px. A one-line option there spends 274px on mark, stamp and outcome, plus 33px of gaps and 20px of padding, and leaves the deck name 18px. So the name takes its own line and the stamp joins the outcome beneath it, exactly the phone treatment. One compact form, used wherever the column is narrow rather than wherever the window is.
The this deck tag becomes a plate and never truncates. It shares the name's line as a flex:none sibling, so the ellipsis can only ever eat the deck name — the marker's own split, and the reason it has one: an ellipsis that eats the clause eats the meaning.
Closed by default, one open at a time, and it never reflows the list — the panel is attached beneath its row, and the row above it does not move.
The card-row floor is held, not bent. The decklist row keeps name + one availability slot + one action, and Pull it keeps that action. The chooser is never in the row — it is a panel attached beneath it, carrying the brass-violet left rule the app already uses for a group boundary.
The two offers read as different kinds of thing at a glance — one bordered button on a row, one list of decks in a panel. The caption no list changes is the one place the distinction is spelt out on these surfaces, and it is a caption, not a warning, because giving is the safe action of the two.
This deck is named, never promoted. Kinnan Ramp · this deck sits in commit order with the rest, because the order is the explanation of the default — promoting your own deck would hide why it lost.
One new chip, one borrowed word: 2 still short beside can be pulled, matching the summary's section and the marker's own short N. It is the only addition to a shipped strip in R2.
Every pick from these doors revalidates every claimant. The spec is explicit that revalidateClaims names one deck literally, so a losing deck's page would otherwise go on drawing the marker and buy state it had before the pick — the same silence, one route away.

8 · The seam with the shipped replace flow

raised in review — and it found a defect

The summary does not replace the review screen, and the two are on opposite sides of the destructive action. ReviewScreen is step 2 — it reports problems with the file while the player can still walk away. The summary is what confirmUpload lands on — it reports consequences for decks, and by then the decision is made. Different question, different moment, no overlap.

1 · UploadEntry
Shipped. The full-replace warning and the current count.
2 · ReviewScreen
Shipped, and untouched. unmatched names in --warning, parse errors in --danger, and IssueInspector behind them. About the file.
3 · ConfirmModal
Shipped. Type REPLACE. The point of no return.
4 · Summary — new
Replaces only the router.push("/collection") on line 39. About decks.
The defect the question found
An unmatched name is a card that did not arrive — so a deck listing it keeps billing it, and it lands in Still to buy like any other. But the reason clause said not in the new list, which is true of the parsed list and misleading about why.
And the two cases have different remedies. A card you genuinely don't own: buy it. A card whose CSV line didn't match: fix the file — you already own it. Collapsing them sends a player shopping for a card in their own box. That is this ticket's silent consequence, one step upstream.
So there is a third reason clause — name didn't match, in --warning rather than the neutral ink the other two take, because it is the one reason a player can still act on. Parse errors get the same treatment under their own clause.
Four rulings on the seam
The summary names the counts and never re-lists the issues. IssueInspector is a tabbed, searchable, virtualised surface built for exactly that reading, and it already did the job at the moment it could still change the decision. Re-listing them after the confirm is a second copy, not a second chance.
One line, and it says what the player can do about it — the counts, the consequence for decks, and a route back to the file. Absent when the upload was clean, like every other section on the page.
It reads you saw them before you confirmed, on purpose. The summary must not imply the app has just discovered something, or a player will read a clean replace as a failed one. The review screen is where a surprise belongs; this is a receipt.
One thing to ratify: the route back needs IssueInspector reachable after the confirm. The rows live on csv_uploads, which the spec is already adding summary_payload to — so the data is there, but nothing reads it post-confirm today. If that is more than PR 3 can carry, the line keeps its counts and drops its link — it is still worth saying.

Notes for engineering
The row renders committed claimants only — loadAvailability's claims filtered to mature. A brew must never be a pickable option, and the count in the supply line counts the options drawn, with brews carried by the footnote.
The commander guard is read off claims[].commander, and it is per copy, not per option: a folded claim renders inert for its commander half and live for the rest. Phase 1 of the supply model is the authority; the drawing follows it.
Refusals must be named, and there are three drawable causes. A single generic failure collapses them into the transport treatment and the spec's “reported on the row rather than swallowed” goes unmet.
A refusal response carries the row's fresh state — arrivals, claimants, allocation — from the same transaction that refused. One round trip, not two.
Optimistic on success, authoritative on refusal. The displaced deck's outcome is computed client-side on the optimistic hop, or the trade lags the tap that caused it.
Picking the held deck clears the reservation — a delete, not a write of “first committed” as an explicit choice. A cleared row must be indistinguishable from one never touched.
The per-option commit date is the fact seniority derives from (deck_version_cards.since, per card — not the version's own date). If the drawn date and the ordering can disagree, drop the date rather than approximate it.
The money sections are the stored payload and must not drift. Only the contested section reads live. A pick must not move a figure above it.
Every pick revalidates every claimant's /decks/[id] and /changes in pattern form, not just the chosen deck's.
Two live-region messages, never more: one per settled pick, naming both sides (Lotleth Aggro gets Fauna Shaman. Sidisi Graveyard buys it.), and one per refusal, verbatim. No motion anywhere.
