Card captions · modifiers below the card · PR #635 · design/card-view-captions

Two lines, and the art keeps nothing.

The brief moves four marks off the illustration and asks where they land. The answer is a fixed two-line caption: a mono meta line under the art, a name line under that, and—on the deck tile—the ✕ as a full-height right column. One line cannot hold it: at 178px the deck's meta content measures ~105px and its name needs ~130px, and the ✕ takes 32–44px off the top of both. Two lines is the smallest shape that fits, and because every tile takes both lines the grid stays square.

The move pays for itself twice. Off the art, the availability chip, the money tag and the proxy mark stop needing scrims — and with no scrim they are the same box: mono, radius 4, px 5 / py 1, differing only in ink and edge. The one-slot rule stops being a convention and becomes something a reader can see. And the art's inset 0 -40px 40px -30px shadow, whose only job was holding the bottom-left group legible, deletes with its occupants.

A · the caption's shape (§3)
Two lines, always, on both routes: a mono meta line, then the name. The deck's ✕ is a full-height right column, so it is the only thing the caption pays for twice. 46px desktop / 52px phone, fixed, data-independent.
B · draft state (§4)
The edge transfers, the pair moves house. A 2px accent inset edge on the caption, not the shell — which leaves the over-committed border tint free. The pair lands where the count was, on the meta line, so the name stays whole: 1 → 2 recounted, +1 added, both suppressing ×N.
C · the proxy mark (§5)
The row's P, in the meta line's one slot, at the row's own size. No new form: off the art the slot is already ProxyTag's box. The proxied tile draws no chip, no price, no quantity and a hollow dashed dot — and does not grow.
D · what stays on the art
Nothing. The art is bare, and the bottom scrim goes with it. Ruling 1 permits keeping one corner; keeping one would re-import the shadow that corner needs, which is the whole cost the brief is trying to recover. The tile's only remaining overlay is the 1px inner edge that separates art from plate — a different job.
ADDENDUM the key in card view — erratum 7, ledger item 9 reinstated

Card view gets a key. It is the same component in the same slot, and exactly one of its two words differs from the list's. Erratum 7 is right that one legend cannot decode two vocabularies — and wrong that two legends are two vocabularies. MarkKey takes its marks as data precisely so every future mark is an entry rather than a rewrite; a second surface is the same kind of addition as a second mark.

Shared copy is refused on correctness, not taste: the row draws $ on unowned and spoken-for cards, the tile on unowned only. A spoken-for card is owned, so not owned is false of it; an unowned card in a deck you are not shopping is not to buy. The two glyphs mark two different sets, so no one word is true of both — and P marks the same set on both surfaces, which is why it keeps its word.

It wraps rather than scrolls, as it already does, and two entries at 10px measure ~150px — one line at 390px with room to spare. And it stays absent when the view holds neither mark, which on a fully-owned deck with no proxies is most of the time. An empty legend is furniture.
Why not “no legend” — the phone decides it
The third shape is tempting and is nearly right: the guard's own comment says the tile's marks carry their own words, and they do. Count the carriers for a sighted reader on a phone and there are none.
proxies.ts states the rule this design is held to: the word lives in three places — the key, the title, the accessible name — and “three carriers, never one: a one-character mark is only safe for a fact the app must not be vague about if the word is reachable three ways.” Withdraw the key in card view and the tile has two: title, which is a pointer affordance, and the accessible name, which reaches a screen reader. A sighted phone reader has neither.
MarkKey's own header is the other half: “a glyph identifies and cannot teach, so something on the surface has to say the word” — and the token contract's four hue families are all booked, so colour cannot teach it either. “No legend” is not a ruling this system can hold; it is the list view's rule suspended on the view where the marks are smallest.
Rejected, and one word that has to move
Shared copy, either direction. to buy over tiles tells a player to buy a card they may be proxying or already own; not owned over Shopping rows is false of every spoken-for row there. And the row's word ships today — changing it to serve a second surface is the one move that can regress a live one.
A third word both surfaces could share — no copy, money. This is erratum 7's third vocabulary, arrived at honestly: two true words replaced by one vaguer one, and the row loses the shop.
A new component. The key takes marks as data and is “the surface every future mark is added to”. A second instance is an argument, not a fork.
Entries for the chip, the count or the draft figure. The key decodes glyphs. The availability chip is already words; ×N and 1 → 2 are figures that read as themselves, and the draft's sighted decoder is the N changed in this draft caption, which already renders on both views.
The constraint, discharged. key.regionAria reads “What the marks on these rows mean” and cannot sit over tiles. It is not edited — the list still has rows. A sibling is added: regionAriaCards, “What the marks on these cards mean”. Cards, not tiles — the reader is looking at cards, and no string in this app names an internal noun.

Two lines is arrived at, not preferred. One line fits only by shrinking the name to eight or nine characters — and a card tile whose name is Smothering… has moved the modifiers off the art at the cost of the one thing the caption was already for.
The meta line is above the name, not below it. The marks qualify the picture, so they sit against it; the name then closes the tile, and in a grid the names of six tiles land on one line rather than being pushed around by whatever marks each card happens to carry.
Glyphs and words, split by what the reader does with them. Everything on the meta line is mono — glyph, count, set, pair — and everything is drawn at a size that says this is a figure, not prose. The chip keeps its two words because none free and short 1 are what the marker means, and the colour is never the only carrier rule is what forbids reducing them to a glyph.
Three shapes rejected
One line, name truncated harder. Fits at eight characters of name. The caption's original job loses to its new one, and the modal is then the only place the card's name exists.
Two lines only when there is something to say. Forbidden by §3 and by both components' own comments: the grid goes ragged, or every tile pays the worst case anyway — which is this design, arrived at by a longer route.
The ✕ on the name line only, caption 34px tall. Saves 12px of tile height and costs the name 32px of width — on the line where width is the scarce thing. The right column spends height, which a vertically-scrolling grid has.
The collection caption keeps its set line and gains nothing. SET · rarity is already mono --fg-faint, so it joins the meta line right-aligned rather than becoming a third row — and the shipped phone behaviour, set code without the rarity, is what keeps it inside 175px.

The quantity badge goes down with the rest. It is the loudest thing on the art — accent fill, --bg ink — and a count is the most ordinary fact a tile carries. In the meta line it is a mono figure beside the dot, still absent at one copy.
Why not keep one corner
The tempting version keeps the availability chip top-left, because it paints its own scrim and never depends on the art. It is refused for three reasons, and the third is the one that decides it.
One · the slot would be in two places. The chip, the money tag and the proxy mark are one slot by rule. Drawing the chip on the art and its two alternates in the caption would put one slot's three occupants in two locations, which is a rule the reader can no longer see — and §5's exclusivity is exactly what it would stop showing.
Two · the chip is the state that speaks least often. The marker returns null at free, so the kept corner would be empty on most tiles in a healthy collection: a corner reserved on every card for a mark most cards do not carry.
Three · one mark still costs the whole scrim. Legibility over unknown art is not per-mark. Keep any corner and the inset 0 -40px 40px -30px wash (or a new one at the top) stays — and the brief's own survey says that shadow has no other job. Bare art is the only version that recovers the picture rather than most of it.
Kept: the 1px inner edge, inset 0 0 0 1px rgba(0,0,0,0.25). It is not a scrim for a mark — it keeps a pale card frame from bleeding into the tile plate, which is a job nothing else does. And the art stays one click target, now genuinely undivided.

B · draft state — the edge transfers, the pair moves house

The list view has had this since #389 and the card view has nothing: a deck with an uncommitted draft photographs identically in card view whether the draft is clean or not. Both halves of the row's treatment carry over, and only one of them lands where the row puts it.

The edge — on the caption, not the shell
The row draws a 2px accent inset edge. The tile draws the same 2px accent inset edge on the caption block's left, full caption height, and nothing on the art or the shell.
Because the shell's border is already spoken for. Over-committed tints it, per the sibling handoff's Matrix 2, and an accent inset ring on the tile would sit 1px inside that tint — two unrelated facts on one edge, on the tile where both are most likely to be true at once. The caption is also the honest host: the draft changed a count, and the count is in the caption.
It is the same 2px, not a tile-native thickness. A reader switching views should see one treatment; a 3px tile edge would be a second spec for one fact.
The pair — where the count was, not where the name is
The row prints Sol Ring 1 → 2 in the name's place and suppresses ×N. The tile keeps the suppression and moves the pair to the meta line, into the slot the count vacates.
The row's reason does not survive the move. It puts the pair beside the name because the name is where its count lives. On the tile the count has its own mono line — so the pair can land there, the double statement is still resolved, and the name is not asked to give up 30px it does not have at 178px.
Two figures, one family. 1 → 2 for a recount between non-zero counts — the list view's own form, verbatim. +1 for a card the draft added, which the list view has no form for because a row that did not exist has no count to move. Both are accent, both are mono, both replace ×N rather than joining it.
Neither is a control, and the ✕ is unchanged by the draft. Removal in a dirty draft is the same tap as in a clean one.
Rejected here
A tile-native accent corner or dog-ear on the art. Call D just emptied the art; a draft is the last fact to refill it with, because it is the most temporary thing the tile knows.
The pair in the name's place. The row's shape, and it costs the tile the name.
A word — added, changed. The meta line is figures. A signed figure says the same thing in a third of the width, and the review screen is where the words live.
Accent on the shell border. Collides with over-committed, and a tinted tile edge in a grid of six reads as selection.
C · the proxy mark's tile form — the row's mark, in the row's box

The proxies handoff draws the mark on the decklist row, the receipt and the cross-deck list, and never on a tile. That gap closes with no new drawing, because the caption's slot is already ProxyTag's own box.

One slot, three occupants, one box
Drawn at desktop size. The exclusivity is STATES-proxies.md:27–30 — deckRowTag returns one — and it is cited, not reopened. What is new is that it is now visible: the three occupants are the same rectangle in the same place, so the tile cannot be read as having room for two.

Skin A, unchanged: P at --fg weight 600 on a 1px dashed --fg-locked edge, no fill. And one drop: the money tag's --bg 92% scrim is gone, because the caption is a solid surface. A scrim over a plate is a hole in nothing.
What a proxied tile must not draw
Carried across from the row's rule unchanged: no availability chip, no price, no quantity, a hollow dashed dot, and no growth. Each falls out of the model rather than needing suppression — the anti-join makes a proxied card free, so the marker returns null on its own.
The dot stays hollow, and that is not a lie about a covered slot. The dot answers what is in your box; the mark answers what this deck needs. Filling it would be the app's first false claim about cardboard, which the proxies handoff already ruled.
Card view is All-only, so the Shopping case cannot arise — a proxied card is not a Shopping row at all. The tile therefore never has to decide between a price and a mark; it only has to be a place the mark fits.
One thing this handoff asked for and did not draw: the key — now the addendum. R1 asked for MarkKey above the grid “on the same terms as above the list”, and erratum 7 disproved it: the terms are not the same, because the row draws $ on unowned and spoken-for cards where the tile draws it on unowned only. Called: same component, same slot, one differing word — $ not owned beside the list's $ to buy, and P proxy on both.
