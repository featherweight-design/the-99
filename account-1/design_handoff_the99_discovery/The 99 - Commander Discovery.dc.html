Commander discovery · one ranked list, two sorts · track 2 · stage 1

One list. Two sorts. Every row says why it is where it is.

/commanders gains a scope control that swaps the candidate universe, and under discovery a sort control that picks between overlap and tag distance. RecCard keeps its grid, its stretched link and its ending. What changes inside the row is one slot: the justification, whose contents and whose accent follow the sort key and nothing else.

The 99 is not the denominator. Per §5 a row reports N of the M cards EDHREC associates with this commander — M is the support list, around 260, and it is printed beside N every time it is claimed. Ownership of the commander is a mark on the row, never a filter. Price is drawn, never ranked on, and the row is complete without it — that is a state, not a fallback.

Read README.md for the decisions log, CONTROLS-four-above-one-list.md for the §7.2 composition at both widths, STATES-price-and-empties.md for the §7.4 price matrix and the three empty states, commander-discovery · copy strings.ts for production copy. Not gated — §0's index is populated; #288 is in flight and this list does not wait on it.

§7.1 How a row justifies its rank
One slot, and the accent follows the sort key. Under Best fit it is the overlap sentence with its denominator; under Something new it is the archetypes, named. Same position, same height, never both.
§7.2 Where scope and sort live
Two tiers, not four peers. Scope is a page-header control — it names the list. Sort takes over the inert Order: Fit ↓ · fixed label in the filter bar. Nothing that ships moves.
§7.3 Owned vs. unowned
The rank pip, plus a word. #43's 6px diamond, filled for a commander in your collection and hollow for one that is not, and a mono clause that says which. Both always render, so row height never moves. Not the buy tag — that is a verb, and this is a fact.
§7.4 Price and stock
Last line of the fit column, never coloured by magnitude, never barred. Absent before #288 the slot does not render at all — no dash, no skeleton, no reserved gap. Only an owned commander's price is suppressed on purpose.
§7.5 Distance, expressed
A per-row line that names the tags, and names your deck when there is one to name. Not a list-level framing — the ranking is per commander, so the reason has to be too. No tag filter: that is #159.
§7.6 The two empty sentences
Different sentences, different homes. No decks yet disables the sort segment with its reason (ruling 8). The floor excluding everything is a list-level state that offers Best fit, never a re-tuned floor.
§7.7 3,342 rows
Same reveal, wider numerals, one new sentence. REVEAL_STEP stays 25 and every figure is locale-formatted. The end-of-ranking rule stays — under discovery it is a filtered outcome, not the bottom of 3,342.
Locked in §6 — drawn as written, not re-litigated

Price is the last line of the column and never the first thing read. It is mono, it is --fg-body, it has no bar, no chip and no brass — brass is the sort key's, and price is not ranked on (ruling 7). tier 1 — which list
Scope sits in the page header, beside the title it changes. It is not a filter and it is not a ranking option: it swaps the population, and the subtitle beneath the title re-states the population in words on every flip. Two segments, radiogroup, the shipped segmented geometry at 32px.
tier 2 — how it reads
Colours, Score against and Sort stay in the filter bar, in shipped order. Sort occupies the slot the inert Order: Fit ↓ · fixed label already holds — a label that always said the order becomes the control that sets it. Under the owned scope it reverts to that label verbatim.
why not one strip
Ruling 1 rejected collapsing scope and sort, and the same logic separates the tiers: a menu is right for sort because it holds a disabled option with a reason (ruling 8) — segments cannot carry one legibly — and a segmented pair is right for scope because both halves are always available.

D · §6.8 and §7.6 — three empty states, three sentences
1 · no decks yet — ruling 8. Not an empty list: a disabled sort.
Sort Best fit ▾ Something new — unavailable
Something new needs something old first
We rank “new” by how far a commander sits from the archetypes your finished decks already play — and you have not finished one yet. Best fit works from day one: it only needs your collection, and you have 2,805 cards in it.
This is the first visit of every new player, so it is drawn as a first-run banner in the shipped GuidedBanner shape rather than as a failure. The list underneath is full — 3,342 candidates ranked by fit, since nothing has been built to exclude. Two signals, one fact: the menu option carries the short reason, the banner carries the long one, and neither invents a third sentence.
2 · the floor excludes everything — ruling 4, and it is about your collection
Nothing clears the bar for “new” yet

Something new only shows commanders you could actually build — at least 30% of their support list already in your collection. None of the 3,342 clears that against 2,805 cards, and the closest sits at 22%. Nothing is broken; the collection is just early.

Rank by best fit instead 3,342 commanders, no floor
A different sentence from state 1 and it must stay different: there the machinery is missing, here the collection is. It names the floor as a percentage, names the nearest miss so the number is not arbitrary, and offers the other sort, never a looser floor — ruling 4 fixes the constant, and a “show me anyway” control is the aspirational half returning through the side door. Uses the shipped EndOfRanking hairline-and-diamond furniture, not a new empty-state component.

End of ranking · 61 of 61
step stays 25The step is a reading unit, not a fraction of the list. 25 rows is about a screen of decisions at either width; raising it to 100 because the list got longer makes each activation a wall and still leaves 3,000 rows. 134 activations is not the path anyone takes to the bottom, and it is not meant to be.
figuresEvery count is toLocaleString'd. WindowCount already does; the reveal control's remaining and its aria-label already do; the “Showing N of M” label does not, and at four digits it must. That is the one shipped string this handoff asks to change.
one new lineThe control gains a second, dimmer line naming the rank band it is about to add and pointing at the colour filter. At 242 rows the band was implicit; at 3,100 “show 25 more” without it reads as an infinite scroller with extra steps.
no jumpNo search field, no page numbers, no rank jump. This is a ranking: the interesting rows are at the top by construction, and a jump-to-rank-2000 control advertises otherwise. Narrowing by mechanic or popularity is #159 and applies to both scopes.
the end ruleKept as shipped. Under discovery it is what a narrowed list ends on — two colours and a floor bring 3,342 down to double digits routinely. It is no longer a promise anyone will see at the bottom of the unfiltered list, and that is fine: it never claimed to be.
Token · a11y · motion contract
No new token, no new hex, no new motion class, no new component. Scope and sort reuse the shipped segmented and menu geometry; the ownership pip is RecCard's own diamond; the price dot is the availability dot; both empty states are built from GuidedBanner and EndOfRanking parts.
Scope is a radiogroup, on the shipped ScoreAgainst pattern — roving tab stop, selection follows focus, arrows within. It swaps data, not panels, so it is not a tablist. Sort is a menu because one option can be unavailable with a reason.
One live region, three messages. Scope announces the new population and ordering (“3,100 commanders you have not built, ranked by best fit”); sort announces the re-rank; the reveal keeps its shipped range message. Debounced to one per settle, on the region the page already owns.
Ownership and stock are never colour alone. Filled vs. hollow is a shape difference, the receipt line says the word, and the row's accessible name carries “in your collection” or “not in your collection” before the figures. Price reads as “$18.40, in stock at ManaPool”, and is aria-hidden never.
Contrast. Every figure that carries meaning sits at --fg or --fg-body on --surface. --fg-locked is used only for the disabled sort option and the dimmer of the two reveal lines, both of which repeat information available elsewhere.
Motion: none. Both flips are state changes over data already on the client — no transition, no dimmed list, no skeleton. The list re-renders in place, as the shipped toggle does.
Focus. Global :focus-visible, 2px brass at +2px, unoverridden. The reveal's preventScroll focus move is unchanged, and a scope or sort flip resets the window to the first 25 exactly as a filter change does.
Mock data: the player owns 2,805 cards and has built 8 decks. Commander names, colour identities and support-list sizes are real in shape and fabricated in value — do not read 127 of 264 as an EDHREC figure.
