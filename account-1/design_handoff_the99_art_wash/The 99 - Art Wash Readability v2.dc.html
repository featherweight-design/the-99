The 99 · Readability amendment · R2

Framing the wash

R1 fixed contrast. What's left is framing — the band is too shallow for the crop it's holding. At 2.8:1, cover discarded 51% of the crop's height, and the arts read as texture rather than as portraits. Widening the band is the fix; focal position is the fine-tune on top of it. Below: the geometry, a focal sweep across five arts, and per-surface numbers for Decks, Commanders and both Home treatments.

Anchor to the top, not the centre
Across the sample, the recognisable feature — face, silhouette apex, crown — lands between 27% and 44% down the crop, and the bottom fifth is nearly always foreground rubble. Anchoring to the top keeps the whole face band and lets the window's lower edge stop at 62% — the rubble is never sampled at all. center center does the opposite: pulls rubble in and pushes the face up under the title.
background-position: center top;
Past 4:1, stop cropping
A commander row or a resume row is 6–12:1. No focal point rescues a letterbox that thin — that's why R1's rows read as texture. Those surfaces switch strategy: size the art by height, anchor it left, fade it out, and let a soft full-bleed copy carry the colour behind it.
The corollary matters just as much: once the art has its own column, type stops overlapping it. That is what fixes the resume-row titles below, and it is why those rows need no text-shadow.

Read — and a correction to my own R2 note. The sweep shows the subjects surviving in every column, which means band aspect did the heavy lifting, not focal. Going 2.8:1 → 2.2:1 is what recovered the arts; focal is a fine-tune, not a rescue.

The reason 0/5/10/15% look near-identical is arithmetic: background-position percentages are a fraction of the overflow, not of the image. At 2.2:1 the window is 62.3% of the crop's height and has only 37.7% of travel, so those four columns place the window top at 0 / 1.9 / 3.8 / 5.7% — all inside the top 6%, about 9px apart and 26px of spread in total on a 457px crop. They buy nothing over each other. For scale, 0% → 34% moves the window 58px.

What separates them is where the focal lands relative to our labels, and on this the low end wins. Our titles are top-anchored. The five annotated focals span 27–44% of the crop, so at 50% they ride up to 13–40% of the band — straight under the title — while the window's lower edge reaches 81% and drags in foreground rubble. At center top they sit at 43–71%: below the title, in the thinnest part of the vertical scrim where the art is most visible, and the window stops at 62% so the rubble is never sampled. Faces also read most accurately there — the crop's own top edge is the one boundary that can never cut a head off.

Ship one value: center top. Per-card focal data isn't in the API, and hand-tuning 30k commanders isn't a plan.

The row keeps the full-bleed wash, but the recognisable copy of the art is sized by height and anchored left, so at a 148px band it occupies 203px of width at its true 1.37:1 — a real portrait rather than a 9:1 slice. Name and pips drop to the band's baseline where the vertical scrim is heaviest, which is also where art_crop backgrounds are quietest.
The fade belongs to the art, not the row. The portrait's extent is derived from band height (148 × 1.37 = 203px), so a percentage-of-width fade can never stay aligned with it — at a wider row you get a bare strip, at a narrower one the scrim clips the portrait. Fade the sharp layer with its own mask-image and keep the horizontal scrim's stops in px. Both then travel with band height at any row width.

Escape the apostrophe. encodeURIComponent leaves ' intact, which closes a single-quoted CSS string early and silently drops the art. Commander names are full of them — Yuriko, the Tiger's Shadow; K'rrik, Son of Yawgmoth; Azog, Moria's Ruin. Use double quotes inside url() and append .replace(/'/g, '%27'). Better still, key off the card's Scryfall id rather than named?exact= — the named endpoint is a convenience for this spec, not for production.
