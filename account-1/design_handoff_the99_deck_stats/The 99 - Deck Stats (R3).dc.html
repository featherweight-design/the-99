DECK STATS R3 the measured ledger R2 → R1 →

R2 was the reference's structure with our tokens dropped in, which is why it read as a copy. R3 starts from the three devices this app already owns — the hairline rule, the unit square, and the leader dot — and lets the figures take the shape those devices imply.

The 99 is not a dashboard. It is a records app for a paper game — Cinzel over parchment, hairline-divided cells, mono figures, a decklist that is literally a list. So the block is drawn as a ledger page, not a chart panel: section heads that rule across to the edge, figures set in the margin, and two distributions measured in the only two units this app already counts in.

Device 1 · the unit square
IdentityPips' 2px-radius square, already in the app. The curve is built from it: one square per card, stacked. Not a bar — a tally. You can count it, which is what a player actually does with a curve they distrust, and the hump is still the first thing you see.
Device 2 · the leader dot
A dotted line running from a label to its figure — the oldest device in printed records, and the one the role strip's em dash already implies. The colour spread is six leader lines measured against one ruler, so the shortfall is drawn as dots rather than as empty track.
Device 3 · the ruled section head
Cinzel label, hairline running to the right edge, the denominator set at the end of the rule. It is how the two denominators stop being a footnote: each section states its own on its own rule, so 100 cards and 63 cast are never one number seen twice.
§0

What R3 drops, and what it settles by dropping it

Dropped · the ring
A dashed circle around a number is the reference's signature and it was doing no work ours needed. The count now sits where every other count in this app sits: in the tab, in the spine, in the margin.
Dropped · percentages in pills
And with them the whole placement problem — where the figure sits at 1%, at 95%, at 100%, and which ink clears which fill. The ruler states the proportion; the count states the fact. No percentage is printed anywhere in the block.
Dropped · the y-axis chart
Gridlines, a scale, an axis title and a count inside every bar is four devices to say what a tally says with one. The tally also kills the scale question: a unit square is never rescaled, so no deck's curve is drawn to a different ruler than another's.
Dropped · dimming the absent colour
A colour this deck does not play now draws as leader dots all the way to the ruler's end and an em dash — the app's own not-yet vocabulary, at full contrast. Nothing is faded, so nothing has to be readable while faded.

The ruler is the figure the reference needed a percentage for. Six rules measured against one scale, each starting at the same zero — you read black is most of the deck, green nearly as much, colourless a sliver without a single number, and the counts on the right are there for when you want to check. Because each rule is measured and none is a segment, nothing about the drawing implies the six should add to 100 — which is the arithmetic problem the stacked bar could not survive and the pill percentages could only footnote.

The dotted line past the end of each rule is not decoration. It is the remainder — the part of the deck that is not this colour — drawn in the same ink as the absent colours' full-width dots. So White — and Colourless 9 are visibly the same kind of statement at two magnitudes, and an absent colour needs no dimming, no zero and no special case.

§3

All decks · the same ledger, one denominator up

The roll-up is the identical drawing with a wider ruler. Same section heads, same leader dots, same ruler, same counts on the right — the only differences are the denominator on the rule (487 cards instead of 100) and the record's caption. That is what one meaning at two altitudes looks like when the drawing carries it instead of the copy: a player who has read one has read both, and no ring, checklist or badge appears at one altitude and not the other.

Two figures, not four, and the library still decides which: sumPlayRecords and sumColourSpreads exist, sumManaCurves does not, and cost has the Commitments card. All six colours are drawn here, not a top three — on one ruler six rows cost 26px each and the shape of a whole collection is the reason a player is on this page.

On the deck page the ticks read 0 · 25 · 50 · 75 · 100 and they are cards, not per cent — a coincidence of a 100-card deck that does real work, because a player counting to 99 already thinks in cards. On Home the same five ticks read 0 · 122 · 244 · 365 · 487. Quarters of the denominator, labelled in the unit the figures are in. Nothing is ever a percentage, so nothing has to explain why six percentages sum past 100.

Placement is still the home handoff's call — specified full-width above Home's two-column body, mounted at their discretion. Nothing in this drawing assumes the Your Decks rail survives, and the ready/brewing counts on the section rule are the deck page's own status words, summed.

The only device the roll-up does not inherit is the tally, because it has no curve to draw. That is the shape of the ruling, not an omission: the section simply is not there, rather than being there and empty.

§4

The absences, in ledger vocabulary

A ledger has a ready answer for a line with no figure, and it is the one this app already uses: the leader dots run the full width and the figure is an em dash. Every absence in the block is that one move, at four scales — which is why R3 needs no dimming, no empty tracks, no baseline rules and no arcless rings.
