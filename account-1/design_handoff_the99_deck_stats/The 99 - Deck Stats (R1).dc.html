DECK STATS R1
record · colours · curve · to buy

The page already has a strip measured against a target and a strip measured against the last version. This one is measured against nothing — so no cell in it carries a second number.

That one rule is the whole placement argument. Recommended mix puts rec 10 under every figure; the what-changed strip puts was 10 under every figure. What this deck is puts nothing under any figure, and that absence is how a reader tells the third strip from the first two at a glance once all three are on the page. It is also why the block is not a strip at all: it sits at the foot of the Deck panel, under the list it describes — not above the tabs where the page-level measurements live, and not as a fourth tab.

Three of the four figures were already spoken for and none of them is relabelled here. The record is stated as a figure in the block's own neutral ink and never as a headline, so the Games tab's four placements are untouched. The eight buckets and the versioning strip's one-number average coexist as a deliberate split, and each names the list it read. Cost is printed a fourth time — deliberately, because the deck page is the only surface where both cost figures are visible at once, so it is the only place their difference can be said out loud.

Read README.md for the five rulings and the amendment ledger, PROPERTY-deck-stats.md for the data contract and the per-cell property tables at both widths, STATES-deck-stats.md for the eighteen states, deck-stats · copy strings. Governing: game log, deck versioning, availability R5, refresh R2 (the accent reservation).

Arithmetic 1 · the colour figures sum past the deck
A multi-colour card counts once per colour, so a Golgari deck reports 60 black and 55 green over 100 copies. So the spread can never be one stacked bar — a stack asserts the parts make the whole. Six independent bars, one shared track length, and colours.copies printed beneath them as the denominator every share divides by.
Arithmetic 2 · two denominators, never one
colours.copies counts lands; curve.copies does not. A finished deck is 100 colour copies and 63 curve copies with 37 lands. Both cells print their own denominator on their own caption line, so the two numbers are never one number seen twice — and neither is ever a hard 99.
The word curve is already taken
Versioning's strip uses it for a one-number average over the committed list with no commander, rounded to one decimal. The block's average reads the live draft with both commanders folded in. The two can legitimately disagree, so each states its list beside the figure — one new clause here, one string flagged back to the strip.
Null is not zero, and zero is not $0.00
cost === null means nothing this app will sell you — which a deck short thirty Forests also satisfies, so it never reads done. {copies:3, cents:0} is three copies nobody has a price for. Neither draws as $0.00, and Home's Commitments card is amended to stop doing so.
§1

Where the block sits, and how three strips stay legible

The page's vertical order is fixed by what each measurement is about. Recommended mix and the what-changed strip are page-level — they are true of the deck whichever tab is up, so they sit above the tablist. What this deck is describes the list, so it sits under the list, inside the Deck panel, and it is unmounted with it. Nothing comes out to make room: the block is the last thing on a panel that already scrolls.

§2

The block at rest, both breakpoints

One --surface-sunken panel at radius 11 with a --border hairline — the Games aggregate box's shell, verbatim, so the block enters the page in a container it already has. Desktop is four unequal columns divided by hairlines (152px / 1fr / 1.28fr / 176px): the two scalar figures bookend, the two distributions take the width they need. Phone pairs the two scalars on one line and gives each distribution a full-width row of its own — a deliberate reorder, because pairing is decided by shape and a scalar beside a distribution wastes the distribution's only asset. Glance before study.

The spread is horizontal rows; the curve is vertical columns. Two distributions six pixels apart must not read as one chart, and the axis is what separates them — not a colour, not a border, not a heading. Deck drawn throughout: a finished single-commander Golgari deck. colours.copies 100, curve.copies 63, lands 37, average 3.4127.

§3

The four cells, and what each one refuses to say

Record — a figure, never a headline
wins–losses–draws at mono 600, 22px desktop / 19px phone, in --fg — not --accent, which is the Games tab's record-figure colour in one of its four outcomes and is reserved here for To buy. The draw is dropped from neither the figure nor the caption: 3–1–0 prints its zero, because a record with a missing third term reads as a different record.

Refuses: the four deriveHeadline outcomes, the pattern sentences, the word games as a label. The block states a figure and a denominator; the Games tab keeps every headline it has, at every one of its four placements and inks.
Colours — the pip never appears alone
The 2px square with its rgba(0,0,0,.4) inset ring is IdentityPips' shape, reused — a colour is a colour. What makes this a different claim is what is bolted to it: a letter, a bar and a count. A bare row of pips asserts the deck's legal bounds; pip + letter + bar + count asserts how the 99 landed inside them.

The letter is not decoration. Five bars separated by hue alone is the failure the filter chassis measured; WUBRG + C carries in greyscale. Pips are never dimmed, tinted or tokenised away, per that same ruling.

Refuses: one stacked bar, a percentage, and any denominator inferred from the six figures.
Curve — bars, because the hump is the reading
Eight vertical columns, --fg-muted fill, track 56px desktop / 44px phone, scaled to the tallest bucket and never to copies — against 63 every bar is under a quarter of the track and the shape flattens out of existence. The count prints above each bar that has copies, so the chart is auditable by addition; an empty bucket prints no figure and draws a 1px --border-strong baseline, because a gap mid-curve is a real shape.

Bucket 7 is labelled 7+: the ceiling is made visible rather than mistaken for a count of sevens. The average prints rounded to one decimal, matching averageManaValue's precision so the two figures on the page share a scale. The raw value is never printed.

Refuses: lands as a ninth column, a fractional bucket, and the average without its list clause.
To buy — the copies are the figure
Copies at 22px in --accent, money at 13px in --fg, the priced fraction at 9.5px. That order is the deck tile's own (3 to buy · $18.40): copies is what you act on, money is the consequence. Home's rail prints copies without money; everything else prints both. The block prints both, and that settles the smaller disagreement too.

The last line is the only reason this cell earns a fourth printing: after the copies you already own. The price strip 400 pixels below sums the decklist's own rows; this is the roll-up's deficit. Same page, two questions, and now each says which.

Refuses: $0.00 in any state, the word complete, a buy link (the strip owns the link-out), and basics.
§4

The states that decide the block

The block never hides and no cell is ever omitted — a hole in a four-part block is worse than a stated absence, and the loader always hands over all four. Absence is drawn with the em dash the role cell already uses for not yet known, so the vocabulary is borrowed rather than minted. The colour cell has no empty state by construction: commander_oracle_id is NOT NULL and the commander is folded in at one copy, so colours.copies is never below 1. §5

The roll-up altitude carries two of the four

Record and colours. Not the curve, not the cost. The selection is decided by the library rather than by taste: sumPlayRecords and sumColourSpreads exist and were written for this altitude; there is no sumManaCurves, and its absence is the argument — eight buckets summed over five decks describe no deck anybody owns, and an average of averages is not a mana value. Cost already has a home on Home in the Commitments card; a second print at one altitude on one page is the exact defect this brief exists to stop.

Sequencing, stated rather than left implicit: the roll-up block is specified here — both cells, both widths, same figure names, same functions — and its placement is handed to the home handoff, because Home's two-column body is being reshaped by a brief that is not written and waits on the commander-image treatment. Nothing here assumes the Your Decks rail survives.

§6

Drawn and rejected

Four alternatives that were real candidates, each with the reason it fell. Toggle this section off with the showRejected prop.

rejected · the curve as a row of counts
Curve
0·2 1·7 2·12 3·14 4·11 5·8 6·5 7+·4

Cheaper in height and it fits both widths. But a curve's only reading is where the hump is, and eight numbers make that a mental exercise rather than a glance — nobody reads 2 7 12 14 11 8 5 4 and sees a shape. The bars keep the counts anyway, so the counts version costs a reading and buys 34 pixels.

rejected · one stacked colour bar
Colours
60 + 55 + 9 = 124, drawn as 100%

The most compact drawing and the one a reader recognises instantly — which is the problem. A stack asserts that the parts make the whole, and here they make 124% of it. Every fix makes it worse: normalising to the sum prints shares of a number that describes nothing, and clipping at copies drops real cards silently.

rejected · a fourth tab
Deck Consider Games Stats

Unlimited room, and it is the wrong trade twice. A 320px block does not need a room of its own — it would sit in a mostly empty panel. And a tab is a destination a player has to choose to visit, which is precisely the wrong shape for a description of the list they are already looking at. The tab strip also carries counts; Stats counts nothing.
