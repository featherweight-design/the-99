HANDOFF · R2 Desktop rail · nav.tsx NavRail + RailLock · intake H3

Keep the rail thin wherever a pointer can ask. Print the names where it can’t.

R2 picks the rail by input type, not screen width. If the device can hover, the rail stays 62px and the cards keep the room. Hovering or focusing a slot shows a drawn label beside it, and the browser’s tooltip goes. If it can’t hover (a tablet at md and up), it gets R1’s labelled rail at 88px, because there’s nothing to point with. One media query, (hover: none), decides. There’s no toggle and no stored state. R1’s sections below are kept: D1 to L1 now describe the touch rail. Source: docs/superpowers/design-briefs/2026-10-02-desktop-nav-labels.md.

Breakpoints hold. The app’s splits (Home and its resume rows at 1180, the row inset at 1000) are viewport queries. The rail doesn’t move them; it narrows the columns on each side by 26px. Column arithmetic moves. The commanders, decks and deck-page handoffs all subtract 62. Add --rail-w: 88px to globals.css next to --tabbar-h, have the rail and those calculations read it, and the next change to the rail is one line. One check before merging: any page that puts a fixed column beside its list at md loses the 26px from the list, not from the fixed column. For the 262px filter column at 768, that’s 418px of list instead of 444.
