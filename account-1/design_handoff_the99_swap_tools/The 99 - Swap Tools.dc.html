Deck page · card modal · sub-project 4 · R1

The small swap tools

Shopping gets a per-card limit and cheaper stand-ins on every row. The card modal gets Fits your decks (#218), directly under the list of decks that hold the card. Both answer from queries. Nothing here is a score: a stand-in shows its price or free, its mana value or what it gives up, and the commander figure, and a fitting deck shows two facts in two separate chips. Taking a stand-in is the same swap as Suggested changes' Apply, and Undo swap reverses both halves. Drawn at 1300 and 390. The fixture is HD Gishath: 8 cards to buy, $407.78.

§4

The seven calls

1 · A sheet, at both widths

Stand-ins open in a ModalChrome body: 640 centred on desktop, a sheet from 52px on the phone. Inline doesn't work on desktop, where Shopping is a two-column grid: a panel opened under a left-hand row either leaves its right-hand neighbour stranded or reflows the whole list. To look at a stand-in, hover its name or thumbnail for the shipped preview, or tap it to open the card stacked over the sheet, with Take in the card's footer (1c).

2 · A default chip, then reset to $10

The line reads Marking cards over $10 at all times. An untouched deck adds a default chip. Once a player sets their own figure, the chip is replaced by reset to $10. The limit is never off, so there's no toggle and no blank state (1a).

3 · Drawbacks in words, no marks

A land's drawback is a mono clause: enters tapped unless you pay 2 life. A mark would need a key, and the classifier's categories aren't settled. When the classifier can't name the drawback, the slot says so in italics and points to the card (Rugged Prairie, 1c). The original shows its own drawbacks, or gives up nothing, so the two can be compared.

4 · Undo swap stays in the row's slot, and the strip shows only the total

If the row is gone, a dashed receipt takes its slot, like the shipped UndoRow. If the row is still there with one copy fewer, the receipt sits right under it. The strip shows only the new total. A saved figure would describe past swaps, and after two takes and one undo nobody could check it against the list (1e).

5 · Decks listed by name

Fits your decks lists decks A–Z by name, and a small by name label says so. Sorting by either fact would look like a fit ranking. Sorting by last edited would make the list change for reasons the reader can't see.

6 · Proposed: an Add on each deck row

The Add calls addCardToDeck on that deck's draft. It then reads in the draft and says what the card joins as, with a link to the deck. Removing it again is the deck page's ✕, which has its own undo, so the modal gets no second one. This is Hugh's call. Without it, the rows lose one control and nothing else changes (1f).

7 · R2: a callout in Card Details, and a second modal

R1 put an always-open block in the modal. That cost too much room in a sheet that already carries bracket facts, combos and the collection. It's now one 44px row, Find a deck for it →, which opens a stacked modal only when the player asks (1f).

R2: Undo swap, not Put it back

Put it back names only the card that left. Undo swap says both halves reverse: the original comes back and the stand-in goes. This diverges from Suggested changes, which still reads Put it back for the same act. The recommendation is to move that key to Undo swap too, so there's one word for one mechanism. The removal undo keeps Put back.

1a

The limit: the untouched $10 default, and a $25 the player set

Under the strip, not inside it

The strip sums the list, and it renders null when there's no price feed. The limit is a setting on the deck and has to show anyway. So it's the next line in the same GroupToggle container, mounted only under Shopping, with no plate. R2: the count (4 over, or none over $10) now follows the figure on the same run, after a dot, instead of being pushed to the far right on desktop. The ⇄ key sits under it.

Changing it

Pressing the figure turns it into a $ field with Save and cancel, in place (see below). Whole dollars only, 1 and up. Save writes the decks column, and the marks move as soon as it's saved. reset to $10 writes null. At 390 the figure and reset are 44px targets, and the line is 44px tall.

One deck, both states

At $10 four rows are over: Plateau, Wooded Foothills, Mana Vault and Doubling Season. At $25 Wooded Foothills ($22.10) drops out, and the count reads 3 over. Only marked rows carry ⇄. The limit decides which rows are marked, and so which rows get a shortcut.

1b

A row over the limit, and a row under it

Over: three things mark it, and one of them is words

The buy tag gets a 1px --warning ring at 60%. The meta line gets the words over $10 in --warning mono, after the EDHREC figure (inline on desktop). The ⇄ glyph turns --warning. The row shell, the dot and the name don't change, so a marked row never reads as an over-committed card, which uses --avail-over-border.

R2 · Under: no ⇄ on the row, and the way in is the card

Ruling 3 still holds: any Shopping card can be swapped. An under-limit row just doesn't advertise it. Opening Karplusan Forest (tap the row, as today) shows See cheaper stand-ins → under its buy tag, which opens the same sheet. Desktop keeps an empty 22px slot so the buy tags stay in one column. The phone doesn't reserve it, and gives those 44px back to the name.

The width it costs

Only marked rows pay for the second 44px box at 390. Plateau keeps about 131px of name, and an unmarked row keeps today's width. The $293.84 figure fits BuyTag's 46px slot. On desktop's two columns (about 614px each) nothing truncates. The meta line gains a third item, over $N, which amends the row's rule that only the cost and the figure ride that line.

1c

The stand-ins, five states

The head names the job

The card being replaced sits on a --bg plate at the top of the sheet, with its buy tag and its over $10. Under that, a mono line states the job the stand-ins were matched on: makes white and red, finds a Mountain or Forest card, ramp · mana value 1. It's what the tool read, stated up front.

Groups carry the order

yours, free always comes first. Lands then go to buy · cheapest first. Spells go to buy · by Gishath decks, then not listed for Gishath · cheapest first. The heading says why the rows are in this order, so the rows need no rank number. A free copy held softly by a brew keeps the shipped soft diamond (Sacred Foundry).

A row, at two widths

Desktop is one grid: the name and its facts, then the price (96, right-aligned), then Take (64). The phone stacks the name, the facts and the price, with a 72×44 Take spanning all three. free takes the buy tag's plate shape in --success. Take borrows Apply's skin exactly.

Looking at a stand-in before taking it

A thumbnail on every row, and a hover preview

Each row and the head now carry suggestion-card's 22×31 thumb. With a fine pointer, hovering the name or the thumb shows the shipped hover preview (usePreviewRow) beside the sheet. That's the quick look, with no click needed.

Click or tap: the card, stacked

The name opens CardModal with stacked and backLabel set to Cheaper stand-ins, the Analysis tab's receipt pattern. Desktop puts the back pill in the close slot. The phone starts the card at 96px so the sheet's header stays visible. Prev/Next page through the stand-ins, in the sheet's order.

Take from the card itself

A stacked card opened from the sheet gets a footer: what it replaces, its free or buy tag, and Take. Taking there closes the card and lands the taken row in the sheet, so a player who read the card doesn't have to find the row again. This adds a footer slot to CardModal, shown only when it's opened from this sheet.

1f

R3 · Find a deck for it: a footer link in Card Details, and a second modal

R3 · The door lives in the fixed footer

Card Details scrolls: bracket facts, combos, the collection receipt and the set / rarity / identity / you-own row all sit in one column. A callout at the end of that column is below the fold for exactly the cards that carry the most. So Find a deck for it → goes in the footer, stacked under Buy any printing at Mana Pool ↗. The footer never scrolls, so the link is visible on every card, at the top of the scroll and the bottom. The two links answer the same question: where this card goes next.

What it costs

The body adds nothing. The footer grows from about 64px to 92px on desktop, because a second 13.5px link line with an 8px gap sits beside Prev/Next. Prev/Next keep their place on the right. On the phone the sheet already scrolls to its footer, and the link is one more 44px row between Buy and Prev/Next.

Shown whenever there’s a question to ask

The link shows on any card that’s legal in Commander, for a player with at least one deck. That includes cards with none free: where else a card could go is still a fair question. It’s absent for a card that isn’t legal, or for a player with no decks. Pressing it opens the stacked picker below. Nothing loads until then.

Kept from R1

Decks are listed by name, and every deck row shows two separate chips (commander / this deck) that are never combined. The need chip is absent when a deck's targets aren't measured (Atraxa). The proposed Add, and its in the draft state, are still Hugh's call.

States that moved

Not legal and no decks are no longer drawn states: the callout simply isn't there. All hold it keeps its sentence, with the holding decks listed under it. R4: Colors rule them out is a single sentence now. The picker lists only decks that can play the card, so the can't play it group is gone, along with R1's footnote naming the excluded decks. The picker has a loading state because it now loads on open.

Real estate

R1's open block added about 250px with four decks, plus more for every deck the player built. R2's callout row added 62px to the scroll. R3 adds none to the scroll, and 28px to a footer that's already on screen.

R4 · Copies: the count leads, and Add says what it spends

The picker's head shows the collection count beside the card: ×2 · 1 free. While a free copy is left, each deck row offers Add (Apply skin), which uses that copy. Once the last free copy is spoken for, by this Add or anything else, every remaining row changes to Add to buy in the quiet skin, and the head reads none free in the hard-claim ink.

Why not disable it

A deck can already hold a card the player doesn't own: that's the Shopping list's whole job, and the deck page's add panel allows it. A disabled Add would say you can't, when the truth is you'd have to buy one. So the second Add stays live and says what it costs. The card then shows up on that deck's Shopping list with its buy tag. Both labels have full accessible names: using your free copy or as a card to buy.

If Hugh wants a hard stop instead

Swap the second skin for no control at all, and add one line under the head: No free copy left. Add it from the deck page to buy one. Layout and states don't change. Adding to a brew is a soft claim, so it doesn't use a free copy and never flips the rows. Only a committed deck's draft does.
