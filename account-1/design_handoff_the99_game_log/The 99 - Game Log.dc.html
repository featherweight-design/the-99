Win/loss tracking · #9 · the Games tab

The denominator is the design.

A third tab on /decks/[id], beside Decklist and Consider. Four things stacked: a record line, a form that opens in place, the aggregated axes, and the history. The eight axes are one polarity-neutral vocabulary — the same label in both columns — so card_flow nets to one number instead of two phrases nobody can join.

§5 imposes no minimum-games gate, so confidence is drawn rather than gated. Three dials do it: the denominator travels with every figure, the heading is a description below three games and a finding at three or more, and colour is earned at three. Nothing is ever hidden for being thin — it is quieter, and it says how thin it is.

Read README.md for the decisions log, STATES-log-form.md for the form matrix, LOW-N-summary.md for the §8.5 ladder in full, game-log · copy strings.ts for production copy. Phase 2 only — §6 leaves schema, actions and the read module ungated.

§8.1 The eight labels
Plain nouns, plus a gloss that flips per column. The label is shared and stable; the small line under it is direction-specific, which is what stops a tired player ticking the wrong side.
§8.2 Is Mixed shown
Yes — as one line, not a third group. It is a real finding and it ranks nothing. A third heading beside Fix would claim a parity it does not have.
§8.3 Form presentation
Inline, expanding in place. The builder is dense; this tab is not. A dialog interrupts a busy surface — here it would hide the history you are logging against, and cost a full-screen sheet at 390px the product does not own.
§8.4 The empty state
It pitches the third game, not the first — the payoff is counting, so the copy says so out loud, and says plainly that this tab names the weakness and stops.
§8.5 Low N
Denominator, heading, ink. One game gets no buckets and no colour — it gets a record. Two gets buckets in muted ink. Three earns the finding voice and the state colours. No percentages, ever.
Reused, not reinvented
role-counter-strip's cell carries the axes verbatim — same box, same 5px bar, split into two segments instead of one fraction. BuilderTabs gains a third pill and nothing else.
Locked upstream — drawn as written, not re-litigated
Eight axes and their keys · one polarity-neutral vocabulary in two columns, What worked and What let me down · result and pod size are the only other fields · two-to-four marks is the expected load, and a game with none is valid · the composite key makes one axis in both columns unrepresentable · summarizeGames returns Keep / Fix / Mixed regardless of what the UI shows · no minimum-games gate · and the hard line: this surface names a weakness and never suggests a card. That is #10.

Order is fixed and is the order of the constant — roughly the order a game happens in, from the opening turns to the last. It is not alphabetical and it is not by frequency: a list that reorders itself as data arrives is a list you have to re-read every time.
Why nouns, and why a gloss at all
The label must survive both columns, so it carries no direction. “Speed out of the gate” is fine under What worked and slightly odd under What let me down. Speed is fine under both, and it is the word said at the table.
A bare noun is ambiguous about which side it belongs on — which is the one error that actually corrupts the aggregate, because a mis-sided tick does not just fail to count, it counts backwards. So each row carries a mono gloss, and the gloss is the column's: kept drawing on the left, ran out of gas on the right. One vocabulary, two readings, no new keys.
Rejected: Card flow — the placeholder, and the clearest case. Nobody says it. “Cards in hand” is what running out of gas actually feels like, and it is what a player would search for.
Rejected: Interaction, Resilience, The engine — all three are correct and all three are the language of a deck review, not of a Tuesday night. Answers, Comebacks and The deck's plan say the same things in words already in use. “The engine” also assumes every deck has one; plenty do not.
Rejected: Finishing for closing. In this product “finish” already means foil or nonfoil (collection_items_finish_check). Closing the game is longer and unambiguous.
The keys are untouched. Eight labels, eight glosses × two, zero key changes — so #10 still joins on card_flow and relabelling stays a copy change, exactly as §3 promised.

The count rule
The shipped pill takes a count when it has one (Shopping · 8) and the shipped convention is that a control with nothing to say is absent. Applied here that lands on the count, never the tab: at zero games the pill reads Games, because Games · 0 is a tab advertising its own emptiness, and hiding the tab hides the feature from every player who has not used it.
The count is games logged, not the record. Games · 2–1 in a pill row that otherwise counts things is a different kind of number wearing the same clothes.
Geometry unchanged — 999px pill, brass solid when selected, 1px --border ghost at rest, 13px. No new tab construct, no icon, no badge.

What the copy is doing
It pitches the third game, not the first. §1 says the log is worth close to nothing until the app can count across games, so the headline is the count and not the act. “Track your wins” would sell the worthless half.
It quotes the payoff verbatim — ran out of gas, 3 of 3 games is a real sentence from the aggregate panel, italicised because it is the app talking. Showing the output is the only honest way to pitch an output.
It states the cost in seconds, because the objection is friction, not value. Fifteen seconds is three taps and a number.
It draws the scope line in the player's favour: this tab names the weakness, and what to do stays their call. True today, no roadmap promised, and #10 arrives as a gift rather than a correction.

Inline, and why not a dialog
§8.3's premise is that the builder is dense — and it is, on the other two tabs. This one is a record line, a strip and a list. There is nothing here worth interrupting, and a dialog exists to interrupt.
The history is context, not clutter. People log by comparison — “same as last time but I drew better” — and a modal covers exactly the thing being compared against.
At 390px a dialog becomes a full-screen sheet, a construct the product does not own. Inline needs none: the phone form is the same form in one column, and the two columns become two stacked blocks with their own headings, which is clearer than side-by-side at that width.
The empty state's button opens this same form expanded, so the first log has no extra step and no second surface. One form, one place, two entry points.
Cost, stated: the tab gets long — expanded form plus aggregate plus history is a scroll at 390px. Accepted, because the form collapses back to a 44px row the moment it is saved or cancelled, and that is its resting state on every visit but the first.
Mobile draws four rows then “4 more” here purely to fit the frame on this canvas — in the build all eight render in both columns. There is no disclosure on the axis list: eight is not a long list, and hiding half of a fixed vocabulary teaches the wrong shape.

Tier one, in full
The heading is a count, not a claim. One game logged. — a description. At three it becomes Cards in hand is letting this deck down. The grammar is the confidence signal, and it costs nothing to read.
No Keep and no Fix at N=1. A bucket named “Fix” from a single game is the app giving an instruction on one data point. The marks are still all shown, under What that one game said — which is a true title.
No state colour. Figures in --fg-body, the bar's single segment in --border-strong. --success and --warning are earned at three, and a green figure on one game is a verdict in a colour.
The figure is a direction and a count — 1 down, 1 up — never a net and never a percentage. A net of −1 invites arithmetic across games that has not happened; “100%” from one game is the single most dishonest number this feature could print.
Nothing is hidden. §5 forbids a gate, and hiding thin figures is a gate wearing presentation's clothes. Every mark the player made is on screen; it is just not shouting.
The nudge — two more and this turns into something you can act on — is the only forward-looking line, and it promises a count, not a recommendation.

The panel, and §8.2
Fix is drawn above Keep. The reason to open this tab is the thing that is wrong; Keep is what you protect while fixing it. It is also what #10 reads first, so the surface and its successor agree on what matters.
The cell is role-counter-cell unchanged — --surface box on 1px --border, radius 9px, padding 11px 14px, mono 10px uppercase label, mono 16px figure, 5px bar, 9.5px sub-line. One change, and it is to the bar's meaning: role counters fill one segment toward a target; here it is split — worked in --success, hurt in --warning, and the whole bar is the games that marked the axis, not the games played.
The figure is 3 of 3, not −3. net sorts the buckets and never shows its face: a signed integer needs a legend, and “3 of 3” carries its own denominator, which is the whole low-N strategy in four characters.
§8.2 — Mixed is one line, under a rule. It is a real finding: mana cutting both ways is not the same as mana being fine. But it ranks nothing and prescribes nothing, and a third heading with its own strip of cells would claim equal standing with the bucket that is the point of the tab. So it prints as a sentence, always, with the same counts and no colour.
The headline is the single strongest Fix, in the finding voice, with its denominator inside the sentence. If Fix is empty it becomes the strongest Keep, phrased as a keep; if both are empty and only Mixed exists, it states that plainly. Never a bare record line — the record is the small figure, not the finding.
Sorting is summarizeGames' own: magnitude of net descending, then key ascending. Design adds no second sort, so the panel's order is the pure function's order and stays testable.

dial 1The denominator is always in the figure, never beside it and never as a percentage. 1 of 1 and 4 of 5 are self-describing; 100% and 80% are the same claim with the evidence deleted.
dial 2The heading changes grammar at three. Below three it describes what was logged; at three and above it states a finding about the deck. One sentence, two moods, and the mood is the confidence.
dial 3Colour is earned at three. --success / --warning below three would put a verdict's colour on an anecdote. Below three, figures are --fg-body and the bar is --border-strong.
not a dialNothing is hidden, dimmed to unreadable, or disclaimed away. No “low confidence” badge, no asterisk, no tooltip, no progress-to-three meter. Three is a threshold in the presentation; it is never a gate on the data, and it is never spoken of as a rule the player has to satisfy.
Why three, and what was rejected
Three is the spec's own number, twice: §1's three games is enough for a person to notice, and this happened three times out of three as the sentence the feature exists to print. Design did not pick a threshold; it drew the one already in the document.
Rejected: opacity. Fading the panel at low N makes thin data harder to read rather than easier to weigh, and it fails contrast at exactly the moment a new player is deciding whether this tab is worth using.
Rejected: a confidence badge or a star rating. A second scale to interpret, invented here, with no definition anywhere in the data. The denominator is already the confidence and it is already exact.
Rejected: “2 more games for a pattern” as a progress meter. It reinstates the gate §5 refused, in copy, and turns logging into a chore with a target. The one nudge that survives sits at N=1 and promises a count, not an unlock.
Rejected: percentages at any N. Not a low-N rule but a permanent one, because it is what makes the low-N rule unnecessary elsewhere: “3 of 3” cannot be quoted out of context, and “100%” is designed to be.
The threshold lives in one constant beside the copy, not scattered across three components. If the audit wants four games instead of three, that is one number and no redraw.

the ruleTicking an axis on one side unticks it on the other. The composite primary key makes both-at-once unrepresentable in the data; the form makes it unreachable in the UI, by the same logic and not by a validator.
rejectedDisabling the opposite row — eight disabled rows on the right after four ticks on the left, and a player who changes their mind has to find and untick the original first. An error on save — a rejection for a thing the interface let them do. A three-state toggle per row (worked / neutral / hurt) — tidier in the data model, and it deletes the two column headings that are doing all the teaching.
announcedThe move is spoken once on the live region — Mana moved to what let me down — because for a screen-reader user two rows changed and only one was touched.
serverThe validation in §4 stays exactly as specified. It is not dead code — it is what a crafted request meets. The UI simply never generates one.
History row — anatomy
result26px square, W / L / D. W takes --success; L and D are neutral — a list of losses in --danger reads as a list of errors, and this is a diary, not a lint report.
metaMono 11px: 4-player · 12 Aug. Pod size first because it is the thing that changes how a game read; the date is a locator.
marksChips with a 6px dot — success worked, warning hurt. Direction is the dot, not the order, so the chips stay in vocabulary order and the row does not resort itself. Worked chips first would sort two lists inside one row.
delete30px × at the row's end, --fg-locked at rest, --danger on hover. 44px at phone. One confirm step, inline in the row — Delete this game? with Delete / Keep — because §4 has no edit action, so delete-and-relog is the correction path and a stray tap loses the only copy.
no marksA game with none is valid. The chip area holds — in --fg-locked: absence, never an empty gap that reads as a rendering fault.
phoneResult and meta on line one, chips wrapping on line two, 64px minimum. No truncation of the mark list — four chips is the ceiling the form imposes.
lengthFull list, newest first, no pagination and no cap. Dozens of rows is the stated scale; a deck with sixty games logged has earned sixty rows.
Token / a11y / motion contract
No new token, no new hex, no new motion class, no new empty-state construct, no modal, no toast, no tray. Everything here is globals.css as it ships.
Colour is never the only carrier. Worked and hurt are two headed columns in the form, two headed groups in the panel, and a dot plus a position in history. The dots are 6px and repeat a mark this product already uses for owned / unowned.
Targets: 46px form rows (desktop), 48px (phone); 40px result and pod squares desktop, 44px phone; 30px delete desktop, 44px phone. The whole form row is the label — <label> wrapping a real checkbox, so the 18px box is decoration and the hit area is the row.
Semantics: two fieldsets with legends carrying the column headings, so the accessible name of every box is “what worked, mana” and not “mana” twice. Result and pod are radio groups. The gloss is the box's aria-describedby, not part of its name.
Live region: the existing one. One message per settle — the move, the save (Game logged. 2–1 across 3 games.), the delete. Never one per tick.
Motion: the form's open and close is the only animated thing, and it is the shipped panel transition. No count-up on the figures, no bar animation — a bar that grows on load makes a two-game figure look like a measurement being taken.
Focus: global :focus-visible, 2px brass at +2px, not overridden. Opening the form moves focus to the result group; saving returns it to the collapsed Log a game row.
Refusal: the shipped MutationError under the save button, form state preserved. logGame returns DeckMutationResult, so this needs no new surface.
