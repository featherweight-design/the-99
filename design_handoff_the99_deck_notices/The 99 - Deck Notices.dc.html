1 · the notice, on the deck page and on the tile · §3.1

Anatomy. 24px tall at 1300, 22 at 390, the chip's own heights, radius 6, so it sits level in the chips row. No plate: a 1px hairline of its ink at 55% on the ground it stands on. A 6px dot, then the face in mono 11 / 10. Tapping it opens Notices; 44px hit area as the chip has.

Two faces, one element. Data only: ● 3 notices in --fact, the ink card facts already carry, because every data notice is a fact about a card that moved. Any legality entry: ● Not legal in --danger, and the count moves into the sheet. The more serious face wins; there is never a second tag.

A deck with no chip can only carry legality: data notices qualify an analysis, and there is none. So the tag takes the chip's slot after the status pill, and the face is always Not legal. The placement rule is one sentence at both surfaces: after the bracket chip, or where it would be.

Why after the chip, not on it. The chip is one component with two mounts and one meaning; a dot on its corner would change it everywhere, including Home. Beside it, the tag reads as a note about the reading, and is left out cleanly when there is nothing to say.

The tile. The tag sits after the chip, condensed to ● 3 for data (the tile's link name carries the words) and ● Not legal for legality. The tile draws unread data notices and any legality; once read, a data notice leaves the tile.

Payload: the brief's one boolean becomes one small enum per deck from listDecksWithStats: none | unread | notLegal.

2 · Notices, the list and every entry · §3.2 · with the About entry

Container: ModalChrome, width 560, no mark, title Notices, aside since analyzed {date} (the deck's name when there is no analysis). backLabel defaults to the title, so a card opened from it reads ← Notices.

Group order (§4.5): Not legal in Commander · Outside the commander's colors · Game changers · Two-card combos · Mass land denial · Extra turns · Bracket rules. Legality first because it is a standing state and the only one that asks something of the deck. The bracket groups follow the findings row's own order and carry the findings' own glyphs and labels, so a reader who knows the tab knows this list.

Direction is a word in a 64px mono column: added or removed, the same two for every card-scoped kind (the list's, or Spellbook's). Never + / −, green or amber: those are the pending banner's marks for your own edits, and borrowing them is exactly the confusion §4.4 forbids. A rules entry reads moved.

Every card name opens the card: bold --fg on the dotted accent underline, the Overview's CardDetailTrigger, stacked on the sheet. A combo names both pieces joined by +, then Spellbook's own result line. A rules entry names the rule and says what it says now, in words, and lists no cards.

Dates (§4.7): yes, on every data entry, mono 10.5 --fg-faint, right-aligned: the day the refresh published, as the app writes dates (21 Sept). No "week of": the log has the day. Legality entries carry no date; they are states, not events.

No prediction, anywhere. No entry says what it would do to the bracket. The foot says only that the bracket holds until you re-analyze, and offers it.

Why reading, not only re-analysis. Re-analysis of a changed list writes the overview and spends a credit; making it the only way to quiet a notice would charge the player to dismiss news. Reading is the player's decision made: they know, and chose. The quiet tag keeps the list one tap away until a re-analysis makes it history. A new entry after reading brings the dot back.

4 · the legality entries · §3.4

Two groups, heads in --danger, no glyph, no date, no direction column: the card name, then the reason on its own line. Not legal in Commander. is the one reason the app can state today, since commander_legal folds banned and not_legal together. If engineering keeps Scryfall's value, a banned card reads Banned in Commander. and nothing else moves.

Outside the commander's colors names the colors that put the card outside, as pips and words, then the commander's identity: Blue, outside Tovolar's colors (red and green). One foot under the last legality group: These stay until the cards leave the deck. Reading them does not clear them. No remove action: a notice never edits.

6 · the check before the synopsis · §3.6 · §4.6

Withheld, not warned. Analysis runs (ruling 7). The overview is not written while any legality entry stands, and the press says so before it is made, in the credit note's own slot and form: Reads the bracket only. The overview waits until every card is legal in Commander. It reuses the used-up note's lead, so a player who has seen one knows the other. No credit is spent, and no confirm step sits in front of a free press.

Why not a passable warning. A confirm on every press is friction on the free half to protect the paid half, and ruling 6 already says the allowance should not go on an illegal deck. Withholding is reversible in one edit and never blocks one. After the read, the Overview says No overview while this deck holds cards that are not legal in Commander. with Write one in the disabled form and See which → opening Notices.
