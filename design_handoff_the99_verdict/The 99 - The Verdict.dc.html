Tutors — a fifth finding, and a fifth mark
T1 — the drawn card
A card lifted off the library. The most literal reading of search your library. The risk is the silhouette — a small tilted rectangle is the shape of every card tile on the surface, so at 12px it reads as decoration.
T2 — drawn up ✓
Recommended. A rule and an arrow rising off it: something specific pulled out of the library, which is what a tutor is and what makes it worth counting. Strongly vertical, so it cannot be mistaken for bolt's diagonal or peak's baseline mass at any size.
Tutors get the mark but never the ink of a limit. The glyph is --fact like the others — it is a bracket fact, just not a gated one — and the tile carries a no limit tag so the deletion of the clause is stated where the count is, not in a footnote.

How we make these decisions ▾
Four things decide a bracket. One of them is a published list, so we can be exact. The other three we work out by reading your cards — here is where each answer comes from, and what it might miss.
Game changers Wizards publishes the list, and we check your 99 against it. This count is exact. From the Commander Format Panel's bracket rules ↗
Mass land denial There is no official list, so we use two community-maintained ones. Between them they name 23 of the 24 cards we would expect. From source one ↗ and source two ↗ names and URLs from code We miss Wave of Vitriol, which destroys everyone's lands without saying so in words we can match.
Extra turns We read the rules text. 56 cards say they give you an extra turn, and we find all of them. Card text from Scryfall ↗, checked against source two ↗ second name from code We miss turns made by one card looping on itself, like Nexus of Fate.
Two-card infinites We use Commander Spellbook's ↗ index of known combos. We miss combos needing three or more cards, and ones that only work with a particular printing.
Why we tell you this: if someone at the table disagrees with your bracket, the argument is usually about one of these four. Knowing which one we are least sure of is how you settle it.
Rewritten as four plain answers, one per finding. The old version printed the raw coverage strings — 2 sources, no misses among the 56, known miss — which are notes to ourselves, not sentences. Each row now says where the answer comes from and, underneath, what it misses and why, naming the card. Game changers is included precisely because it has no caveat: it is the contrast that makes the other three legible.
Every outside source is named and linked. Spellbook, Scryfall and the Commander Format Panel do the work these figures rest on, and a figure that cites nothing is the thing this panel exists to prevent. Links are --accent on a solid underline with a ↗, the app's existing out-of-app treatment — distinct from the dotted underline that opens a card. The two land-denial lists and the extra-turn cross-check are drawn as slots: I do not know which lists engineering ingested, and inventing a citation is worse than leaving the slot visible.
And it now says why it exists. The closing line answers the question the panel was silently begging — this is what you reach for when someone at the table disagrees. A disclosure rather than a tooltip: tooltips do not exist on touch, and both widths ship together. Closed by default, local state, never persisted — you read it once, mid-argument.
The climb no longer opens anything. Carets gone, allowances shortened to 3 allowed and no cap, tutors moved out. One rule now: the climb states, the findings open.

Turn 2 · your decks, and the limits table reconsidered

The finding is the content. The limits are the reference behind it.

You are right that the table and the climb say the same thing twice, and adding allowances to the climb made the overlap total. So the table stops being a block of the tab. What replaces it is what is in the deck — named cards, opened properly — with the limits available on demand to ground any figure a reader wants to check. 2b, 2c and 2d are three ways of doing that; they differ in where the limits live and how much of the ladder is worth drawing.

Everything is drawn on your three decks. Turn 1 below is superseded where it disagrees — its limits table (1b, 1c) and its inline disclosure (1d) are the things being replaced. The verdict block, the population line and the detection slot carry forward unchanged.

Flagged — the 3 or 4 split contradicts the brief, and it is your call
You are right about the shape of the system: 1–2 and 4–5 are the only pairs that share card limits, so those are the only two splits intent can produce. But the brief's §4.1 lists five values for permits — [1,2] · [2] · [3] · [3,4] · [4,5] — and [3,4] is there for a different reason: not intent, but that nothing in a decklist says which turn a combo lands, and bracket 3 only permits one that cannot land early.
What I would do instead, if you want the two splits only: a deck at 3 with a two-card infinite reads 3, and the combo is reported as an unresolved fact beside the verdict rather than as a wider answer — “one two-card infinite; whether it lands early is not something a list can tell you.” Same honesty, no third kind of range to teach. It is drawn that way in 2a and everywhere below. It needs a spec amendment, because slotDeck is merged and returns [3,4] today.

1f · the detection slot — provenance, not a badge
All three readings are partial, including combos. A binary badge would be worse than nothing — a field that always says the same thing trains readers to skip it — so the slot carries the actual coverage sentence per fact, one line each, at the foot of the table it qualifies.
