Card surfaces · #349 #350 #351 #363 + parity Q1–Q4 · R2: the buy tag collapses, the unowned tile's slot is filled

Most of this already ships. On /collection.

The card-view brief asks Design to invent a middle-weight tile and to find a home for a third control axis. Neither is a new design: collection/grid-view.tsx is already the tile — 63/88 art, quantity badge top-right, availability chip top-left, name caption below — and collection/filter-bar.tsx:256–281 is already the toggle, a two-segment sunken well with ≣ and ▦ glyphs. The brief mentions neither. So #349 is an adoption with three deltas, not a specification — and that is what frees the pixel budget for #350, which is the one thing here with no prior art.

The pip track is the whole design problem. A 400px desktop row spending 273px cannot absorb an unbounded run of symbols, and rows-view's shingling does not transfer, because colour identity has a hard ceiling of five and a mana cost does not — Progenitus is ten symbols. So the track is fixed at 40px and capped at five slots, and the phone meta line the brief already locked turns out to be the generous width, not the cramped one.

Why this is not a fifth rejected shape
Not shape 1 (peers in one bar). A peer is a bare pill. This is a well with icon segments — a different container, so it reads as a different class of control before it is read as a control at all.
Not shape 2 (a tab strip). Tabs promise a panel swap; this swaps the rendering of the same list, with the same grouping and the same filter still applied.
Not shape 3 (extra segments on an existing strip). It shares no strip with By type / By role and cannot be mistaken for a grouping.
Not shape 4 (a second bar). No new row, no second sticky surface, no new vertical spend at any width.
And it is the shipped answer to the same question one route over. A player who has switched /collection to grid already knows this control. Reusing it is not stacking a fourth knob; it is declining to invent a second vocabulary for one idea.
Adopted — and four things deliberately not adopted
Not the URL. /collection writes ?view=. Ruling 3 forbids it here, and the repo's a cache lives for one visit rule agrees. Component state, resets on navigation.
Not the auto default. browser.tsx:227 resolves null to grid on desktop, rows on phone. The deck page defaults to list at both widths, always: browsing is looking, and a decklist is being edited. The editing surface is the default.
Not 5-up. See panel 1 below — the add panel opens.
Not the 28×24 segments, and not the accent fill. Both were adopted at R1 and both are overruled by refresh R2 — on both surfaces. See the reconciliation panel below.
Absent on Shopping. Ruling 2 makes card view All-only, so the toggle is not rendered under Shopping rather than rendered disabled. A disabled control on the tab where it can never apply is a puzzle; an absent one is a fact about the tab.
Reconciled with refresh R2 — two rulings, one PR, both surfaces

R1 adopted /collection's toggle whole and flagged the target divergence rather than taking it silently. R2's chrome rules answer both open points, and they answer them the same way. R1's adoption argument was about sameness, never about size or hue — so nothing about the adoption is given up by changing both surfaces together. The rule sets the values; the adoption makes the change bilateral.

1 · the phone target floor
No exception. Both surfaces go to 44×44 phone / 32×32 desktop in the same PR.
R2 grants exactly one sub-floor exception and states its test: the 26px … on a deck tile passes because it sits inside a tile that is itself clickable, so the small target is a shortcut rather than the only route. Apply that test to the view toggle and it fails. The toggle is the only route to card view — there is no gesture, no menu, and ruling 3 forbids persistence, so not even a deep link. A control that is the sole route to a whole rendering of the page is the last place to spend the floor.
Desktop moves too, from 30×28 to 32×32 — the shipped desktop figure also sits under R2's 32px icon-only floor, and square is right for a glyph with no label to widen it. Measured cost is above: no wrapped line at 390px.
2 · accent on the builder's bar
Yes, the rule reaches the bar. The bar is not exempt. And the treatment already ships six pixels away, so it is not a new design.
Selected state, pill and segment alike: --fg label on --elevated with a 1px --border-strong ring, 600 weight. That is verbatim what group-toggle.tsx already draws for the active By type / By role pill. Idle stays --fg-faint on transparent.
The bar is internally inconsistent today: its grouping pills say selected in bone-and-elevated, its filter pills say it in accent. Unifying on the half that already obeys R2 removes a real inconsistency and costs nothing to draw.
And the collision is real, not theoretical. The Shopping tab is exactly where accent-as-to buy lives on the rows, so an accent-filled control sits a few pixels from accent carrying its reserved meaning, on the one screen where both appear.
Noted, not ruled — two consequences for whoever takes the PR. The Shopping pill's idle --warning tint is accent's reserved meaning wearing Unresolved's colour; the count beside it already carries the fact, so Design's recommendation is that it becomes the same hairline pill as its peers — but that is shipped behaviour beyond the active state, so it belongs to refresh R3 rather than this ruling. The card view tile's desktop ✕ failed the same 32px floor for the same reason the toggle did, and is drawn at 32×32 throughout this handoff — it fits the caption row without changing the tile, so it needed no trade.

3 & 4 · the pip track, and why the phone line is the generous one

The brief offers rows-view's shingling as prior art and asks whether the decklist row inherits it. It inherits the fan and refuses the ceiling: colour identity cannot exceed WUBRG, so five pips is a hard bound and one overlap rule solves it forever. A mana cost has no bound — Progenitus is ten symbols — so a fan alone still widens the track. The cap is the new part, and it is the only new atom in this handoff.

Desktop — 12px pips in a fixed 40px track, right-aligned

Right-aligned inside the track, and the track sits between the name and the availability slot. That is the only position where the pips of adjacent rows land on one vertical line — left of the marker and the buy tag, both of which are conditional and variable in width. It is the same move card-search-row makes with its fixed 34px slot, and for the reason its own comment gives.
Phone — 13px, plain, uncapped, on the meta line

The meta line is the reason phone can be more generous than desktop, not less. Ruling 7 grew the row to hold the pips; having grown it, the pips get a whole line to themselves and need neither a fan nor a cap. A ten-symbol cost measures 157px on a 358px line whose only other run is edhrec 24% at ~60px. The two widths draw the same fact at two fidelities, and the narrow one is the higher.
4 · what else sits on the meta line: nothing
Pips alone on the All tab. pips · edhrec 24% on Shopping, because that line already exists there and the figure already owns it (addendum A2). One line, two views, no third arrangement.
The marker does not move down. It is the floor's own slot; moving it below the name would make the one required element the least prominent thing on the row, and on Shopping it would collide with the figure that is already there. It stays on the primary row at both widths.
The type line is refused. The list is grouped, and under By type the heading immediately above the row already says it. Under By role the modal is one tap away and the type line is the widest of the three candidates. It is the only one whose absence a reader can resolve without scrolling.
A meta line is not a shelf. Ruling 7 created it to hold one thing that would not fit. Every further fact that lands there is a fact nobody decided to show — and the row it grows is one of ninety-nine.

Answered — R2 call 1: the tag collapses, it does not leave
R1 asked Product to drop the price from the All tab. Product said no, and was right: seeing which cards still need buying while browsing the whole deck is the point of the tag. R1 had read the tag as a shopping affordance because ruling 1 calls it one on the tile — but a tile is a picture of a card and a row is a line in a list you are working through. On the row the tag is a shopping fact, and this handoff's own test says facts stay.
The counter-proposal, adopted: on the All tab the tag collapses to a bare $ in the same chip skin. Shopping keeps the full buy $12.40. The word and the figure both go; the chip, its colour and its position do not. Measured: 62px \u2192 18px, so ~44px comes back — more than the ~40px R1's ask was chasing, and the signal survives because whether to buy is a glyph and how much is a number.
So the 40px track ships and the 34px fallback is withdrawn. The desktop worst case returns to roughly where it stands today, which was the whole point of the ask.
Sold-out is accepted as an edge case. A card with no price collapses to the same $ on the All tab, so sold out only reads on Shopping. Product's call, and it holds up: it is rare, and a sold-out card is still a card the player has to buy eventually. The chip must not restyle itself for it — a second glyph meaning unpurchasable would put two marks in a slot that R1 spent a whole ruling keeping to one.
Phone — zero horizontal cost, and the heights change only at group edges
Pips land on the meta line, so the primary row is untouched: nothing is displaced at 358px. The cost is vertical — 44px becomes 52px on every row whose card has a mana cost.
And that is not every row. A land has no mana cost, CostPips returns null on an empty symbol list, and no pips means no meta line: those rows stay 44px. In a typical 99 that is ~38 rows. Two heights in one list would be drift — except that lands are their own group under By type and carry the land role under By role. So the height changes at a group boundary under both groupings and never inside a group. The reader sees two blocks of consistent rows, not a ragged list.
Measured scroll cost: ~61 rows × 8px ≈ 488px of extra scroll on a 99-card deck. Corrected against refresh R2: its chrome spends 52px on the tab bar plus a 14px home indicator, so that is about 1.5 scrolls of the list region, not the 1.3 viewports R1 quoted against the full screen.
It does not flip the trade, because the trade was never about total scroll. The meta line's cost is per row and constant — more rows below the fold does not make any single row worse to read. What would flip it is rows-per-screen falling below a scanning floor, and it does not: in a ~700px list region a 52px row lands 13 per screen against 15 at 44px, and the ~38 land rows stay at 44px and 15. Accepted at the corrected figure: the alternative is a cost the player has to open a modal to read, on the surface where they are deciding what to cut.

Auto-fill, because the add panel opens. It is 340–346px and it is a sibling pane, so the tile column's width is not one number — a fixed 5-up would land 148px tiles with the panel closed and 79px tiles with it open. Auto-fill re-flows to three or four columns instead and the tiles keep their size, which is what middle-weight has to mean on a two-pane page.

The parity ruling's reason was that a border adds 2px to one row and shunts every row below it; a grid tile already carries a 1px border in every state, so there is nothing to shunt and no reason to redraw it. Radius 7 and the inset ring are row rulings. Do not unify the tile to them.
The floor reaches the tile
Parity Q1's floor is name + one availability slot, and it is a property of a thing standing for a card — not of a rectangle being wide. So the tile carries it, and the shipped chip is the slot.
The consequence worth stating: a player cannot use card view to escape knowing what a card costs them. Ruling 1 drops the buy tag, the price and the auto tag, but the chip and the dot stay, so an unowned card in card view still reads as unowned and a spoken-for card still reads as spoken for. That is the difference between dropping shopping affordances and dropping shopping facts.
One exception falls out of it: with the buy tag gone, the tile's availability slot can never hold two things. The Shopping second-copy case — the one row where the slot legitimately holds a glyph and a tag — cannot occur in card view, because card view is All-only. The slot contract is stricter on the tile than on the row, and it is stricter for free.
6 · the double-faced mark — the game's own notation, and no corner
On the row — after the name, 17px all-in
Delver of Secrets // ✕
Design is declining the corner glyph ruling 5 assumes. A row has no corners, and on the tile all four are allocated or deliberately empty. More decisively: any invented mark at this scale is a 7–9px shape sitting a few pixels from a 7px ownership dot, and the app would then be drawing two small marks on one row meaning two unrelated things. // is typographic, so it cannot be confused with a dot at any size.
On the tile, and in the modal
Delver of Secrets // ✕
modal art, flip control
⇄
Flip control: bottom-right of the modal's art block, 34×34 desktop / 44×44 phone, radius 7, on an --bg scrim at 86% with a --border-strong hairline. Accessible name is the other face's name, not "flip" — the reader wants to know what they are turning to. The image swaps; it does not animate (zero net motion, card-availability row 3.7). It sits on the art rather than in the modal chrome so it cannot be read as a sibling of Prev/Next, and Prev/Next resets to the front face — a new card starts on its front.
One constraint eng needs before starting the data work
The mark must be driven by the layout column and never by the // heuristic the brief describes in toCardRow and type_line. Split and adventure cards carry the same separator and have no back to turn over, so a heuristic-driven mark promises a flip on Fire // Ice that the modal cannot deliver — and a control that is sometimes a lie is worse than a control that is late.
So #363 has no partial ship. The mark and the flip control land together, after the layout column, the back-face image storage and the re-seed. Ruling 8 already says that work needs no design gate; this handoff adds only that it also has no design-only slice to ship ahead of it.
And the notation belongs with the name for a second reason: a two-faced card's real name is Delver of Secrets // Insectile Aberration. The mark is not a badge about the card, it is the name's own punctuation, abbreviated.

It does not fade or scale in. Zero net motion is a locked constraint, and the delay is what makes an instant appearance calm rather than twitchy — 240ms is long enough that crossing the list does not strobe, short enough that a deliberate hover feels answered. The 0ms row-to-row rule is the other half: once open, the preview follows the pointer down the list without re-arming, which is what stops a scan from feeling gated.
What it shows besides art: nothing, and that is arithmetic
imageUri is a full card image at 63/88, so at 252px of width the printed name renders at ~11px and the printed mana cost at ~14px. Every fact the preview owes the reader is already printed on the thing it is showing — adding a name caption or a pip row would be the app restating Wizards' own typography over the top of it.
The rules text is not readable at ~6px, and the preview does not pretend otherwise. That is the scoping: hover answers which card is this, the modal answers what does it do, and the name is already the modal's trigger a few pixels away. A preview that tried to be readable would be the modal, which the brief already refused as too heavy to reuse.
The same arithmetic is why the tile keeps pips and the preview does not. Printed cost is legible above roughly 200px of card width. The preview is 252px, so it reads. Tiles land 132–190px desktop and 159px phone, so they do not, and CostPips covers the gap — which is exactly why the suggestion tile already carries it. One threshold, applied twice, rather than two aesthetic judgements.
A two-faced card previews its front face only, with no flip on hover: a control inside a transient the reader cannot click into is not a control.
